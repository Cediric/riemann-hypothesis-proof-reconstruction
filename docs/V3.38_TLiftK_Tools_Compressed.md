# V3.38 — TLiftK compressed: fourteen tools and the theta-lift assembly

> Scope: turn the universal theta-lift theorem TLiftK into a proof skeleton, identify the fourteen fields of Tools_k, and record how the source constructs them. This is a structural compression; it is not a replacement for the detailed analytic proofs of the tool theorems.

## 1. The target theorem

TLiftK is a universal existence theorem. Given:

- a CM number field E and its maximal real subfield;
- a good base B;
- a Hermitian source space X;
- a sufficiently large skew target space W;
- compatible Hecke characters and exponent data;
- a unitary automorphic representation U with the prescribed weights;

the theorem produces a target representation U' and an explicit theta-lift relation

\[
\operatorname{IsLift}(B,\chi_X,\chi_W,m_X,m_W,\lambda,U,U').
\]

The target dimension d is allowed to be sufficiently large, with a threshold d0 depending on the source data.

This is the correct interface for the human proof. It is much shorter than the collection of individual imports, but it retains the actual analytic and representation-theoretic content.

## 3. The assembly theorem tLift_of_tools_k

TPGTop.lean proves TLiftK from Tools_k and HgtEx_k. Its proof can be read as the following chain.

### Step A — Height and branch existence

The height-existence input HgtEx_k and the branch-existence theorem provide the admissible range of the auxiliary height parameter. The first-height estimate hgtFst_k is derived from Tools_k.

### Step B — Real-pair construction

realPair_k converts the complex/adelic representation data into the compatible real pair required by the theta-lift construction. It uses the compactness, action, splitting, and Lie-derivative fields of Tools_k.

### Step C — Nonvanishing

nonVan_k is assembled from:

- Rallis_k;
- Decomp_k;
- BumpAny_k;
- Datum_k;
- Tail_k;
- the absolute-nonzero statement;
- SchurStep_k.

This is the nonvanishing engine. It combines the integral identity, decomposition of the relevant adelic group, local bump functions, the chosen datum, tail control, and the Schur estimate.

### Step D — Automorphy and projection

liftAut_k proves automorphy of the constructed lift. projEx supplies the required projection, and readLift_k identifies the resulting representation with the requested theta-lift object.

Combining Steps A–D gives TLiftK.

In compressed form:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Tools}_k+\text{HgtEx}_k\\
&\Longrightarrow
\text{height/branch data}+\text{real pair}\\
&\qquad+\text{nonvanishing}+\text{automorphy/projection}\\
&\Longrightarrow \text{TLiftK}.
\end{aligned}
\]

## 4. How tools_holds_k constructs the fourteen fields

TPGHolds.lean constructs Tools_k explicitly.

### A. Siegel–Weil and Eisenstein block

- siegelWeilAniso_of_k combines standard Siegel–Weil, frame conjugation, finite-level, and stabilizer-compactness inputs;
- eisContinues_of_k supplies Eisenstein continuation;
- swFullX_of_k combines the anisotropic formula with continuation and the box-section estimate.

This produces swFull.

### B. Finite-level and box-section block

boxSecX_of_k is built from finite-level and stabilizer-compactness data. The double identity, stabilizer compactness, and vacuum-fixing fields are supplied by their named global theorems.

### C. Compactness block

cptAll_of_k combines:

- the topological group structure;
- the epsilon estimate;
- the Siegel-set/unitary reduction;
- finite volume;
- unimodularity.

This supplies cpt.

### D. Theta, pair, and splitting block

- thetaBasic_k supplies basic;
- polSch_k supplies polSch;
- unfold0_k supplies unfold0;
- pairEx_of_k combines modelEx_k and weilEx_k;
- splitFree_k supplies split;
- vacFix2_k supplies vacFix;
- actsEx_k supplies acts.

### E. Lie-derivative block

The second Lie identity is built from localLift_holds and derEx using lieSecond2. The first identity is obtained by applying lieFirst to the second. These supply lie1 and lie2.

Thus the fourteen fields are all populated by explicit theorem applications; tools_holds_k is not an axiom or an unfilled placeholder.

## 5. The height input is also constructed

The theorem tLift_holds_k applies

\[
\text{tLift\_of\_tools\_k}
\bigl(\text{tools\_holds\_k},
\text{hgtEx\_of\_k}(\text{stab2Cpt\_k})\bigr).
\]

So HgtEx_k is derived from the stabilizer-compactness theorem. It is not an additional external parameter of the final theta-lift theorem.

## 6. The resulting final-theorem spine

Combining V3.37 and the present tool compression:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Siegel–Weil, Eisenstein, compactness, theta, Lie tools}\\
&\Longrightarrow \text{Tools}_k,\\
&\text{Tools}_k+\text{Stab2Cpt}
\Longrightarrow \text{TLiftK},\\
&\text{GeoOne}+\text{TLiftK}
\Longrightarrow \text{Row0b},\\
&\text{Row0b}
\Longrightarrow \text{VWin and literature/definite-side packages},\\
&\text{those packages}
\Longrightarrow \text{EveryCharacter}
\Longrightarrow \text{RiemannHypothesis}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The formal theorem is closed. The human-readable proof still needs to explain the analytic and representation-theoretic theorems used to populate Tools_k.

## 7. Highest-value next reductions

The fourteen-field interface suggests three useful subprojects:

1. **Siegel–Weil/Eisenstein:** compress the construction of swFull and the associated box-section/double identities.
2. **Nonvanishing:** reconstruct nonVan_k as one lemma combining Rallis, decomposition, bump, datum, tail, and Schur control.
3. **Compactness/Lie structure:** reduce cpt, stab2, lie1, and lie2 to a small number of geometric and real-Lie-theoretic statements.

The nonvanishing block is likely the most central to the actual theta-lift existence argument; the other blocks provide the analytic framework in which it operates.
