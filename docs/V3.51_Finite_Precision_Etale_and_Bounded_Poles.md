# V3.51 — Finite-precision étale factorization and bounded poles

Pinned upstream 7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4. Continuation of V3.50. We give shorter mathematical arguments for the finite-precision étale factorization, Frobenius integrality and equivariant-map descent. Deep inputs are named explicitly; no new Lean kernel run is claimed.

## 1. Kernel lattices and flat base change

Let p=ℓ, D=Z_p, W=W(C^flat), O=bk.OE, S=bk.S, A=Ainf, and i:A→W, e:S→A, eW=i∘e. All integral matrices are implicitly embedded in W as needed.

For Π:D^b→D^d equivariant modulo p^n between representations ρ' and ρ, the lattice K=ker(Π mod p^n) is stable under ρ' (and its inverse), since Πρ'(g)=ρ(g)Π modulo p^n. It contains p^nD^b. Thus K is free of rank b over the PID D, with basis matrix f of nonzero determinant. The restricted action is ρ''=f^(-1)ρ'f in that basis, an integral group representation. This proves exists_kernel_lattice_equivariant_modPow.

The torsion-free D-module W is flat over the PID D. Tensor the sequence exact at K and D^b:
    0 → K → D^b → (D/p^n)^d.
It gives ker(Π:W^b→(W/p^n)^d)=fW^b. This is a direct proof of flatKer/kerW, without the explicit linear factorization of the original Lean proof. Exactness at D^b does not require surjectivity; Onto separately supplies surjectivity.

## 2. A Smith-normal-form shortcut for finite-precision étale bases

Abstract lemma. Suppose O⊂W are DVRs with common uniformizer p, Q∈M_(d×b)(W) is surjective modulo p^n, n≥1, and its congruence kernel is PW^b for P∈M_b(O). Then there exist T∈GL_d(W), R∈M_(d×b)(O), R'∈M_(b×d)(O) such that
    Q≡TR mod p^n,   QR'≡T mod p^n.

Proof. Smith normal form over O gives P=U diag(p^a_j) V with U,V∈GL_b(O), after absorbing units; all a_j finite and ≤n, since the kernel contains p^nW^b (a zero diagonal would exclude a basis vector after multiplication by p^n). It follows that
    W^b/PW^b ≅ ⊕_j W/p^a_j W.
By surjectivity and the kernel hypothesis this quotient is (W/p^n)^d. Hence exactly d of the a_j equal n and the others equal zero. For an elementary way to see this, the dimension of p^(k-1)M/p^kM over W/p for M equal to the quotient is the number of indices a_j≥k. For k=1,...,n this dimension is d. Therefore no exponent lies strictly between 0 and n.

Let J consist of the d indices with a_j=n, and let E_J be the b×d matrix selecting those columns. For the other indices Ue_j∈PW^b, so the corresponding columns of QU vanish modulo p^n. Thus T=QU E_J has reduction modulo p^n inducing the surjection (W/p^n)^d→(W/p^n)^d, and its mod-p determinant is nonzero. So T is a unit in W. Put
    R=E_J^t U^(-1), R'=U E_J.
Then Q≡TR mod p^n and QR'=T exactly.

This proves the SmithQuot.main interface with a direct module-invariant argument replacing the original separate column-selection theorem. The rank d=0 case is allowed (empty invertible matrix); no division by n is used. The O-DVR property follows from the upstream p-power unit factorization.

Application: Q=ΠF'.T. The isogenous-lattice theorem supplies T''∈GL_b(W), P over O with f T''=F'.T P. Section 1 gives ker(Q mod p^n)=(F'.T)^(-1)fW^b=PW^b. Since Π is onto modulo p^n and F'.T is invertible, Q is onto modulo p^n. Apply the abstract lemma to produce the finite-precision data used in V3.50. The isogenous-lattice theorem is a genuine remaining input, not proved by Smith normal form.

## 3. Frobenius integrality in a single induction

Let K=C^flat with its rank-one non-Archimedean norm, and O_K its perfect valuation ring. Suppose matrices Z over W(K) and L,R,B over W(O_K) satisfy
    φ(Z)=LZR+B.
Reduction modulo p gives the entrywise pth-power equation
    z^[p]=l z r+b.
If M=max(1,max_ij|z_ij|), then M^p≤M when M>1, because l,r,b have entries of norm ≤1 and finite sums satisfy the strong triangle inequality. This is impossible for M>1. Hence all z_ij∈O_K.

Lift z by entrywise Teichmüller lifts Z_0 over W(O_K), and write Z=Z_0+pZ_1 over W(K). Substitution yields
    φ(Z_1)=L Z_1 R+B_1,
    pB_1=L Z_0 R+B−φ(Z_0).
The numerator is integral and vanishes modulo p in W(K). Witt truncation and injectivity of O_K→K imply it is divisible by p already in W(O_K), so B_1 is integral. Repeat to lift one more digit at every stage. The resulting p-adic sum of integral lifts converges in W(O_K) and equals Z by separatedness of W(K). Thus Z has integral entries. This proves the exact inhomogeneous matrix form used by FrobInt.frobenius_integrality; no bound on dimensions is needed. The zero-size case is vacuous.

## 4. Bounded poles for preframes

Let t=i(τ), q=eW(E), with φ(t)=qt and both t,q units in W. A preframe T of height N has
    q^N φ(T)=T eW(A_0),   A_0 A_0'=E^(2N)I.
The inverse equation is q^N φ(T^(-1))=eW(A_0')T^(-1), obtained by inverting the first equation and substituting the product relation. Therefore
    φ(t^N T)=(t^N T)eW(A_0),
    φ(t^N T^(-1))=eW(A_0')(t^N T^(-1)).
Both are instances of Section 3 with integral coefficients. Consequently there are Y,Y' over A with
    i(Y)=t^N T, i(Y')=t^N T^(-1).
Multiplication and injectivity give YY'=τ^(2N)I. This is the full pre_bounded_poles argument from Frobenius integrality and the preframe equations, in a few lines.

## 5. Equivariant maps descend by the same period functional

Let F,F' be preframes of heights N,N' with bases T,T', and f an integral intertwiner fρ(g)=ρ'(g)f. Set X=(T')^(-1) fT. The fixed-subgroup equations give g(X)=X for g∈FixFlat. Define B=eW(E)^(-N)eW(F.A), B'=eW(E)^(-N')eW(F'.A); both are invertible over O using the corresponding A' partners. The Frobenius equations give
    φ(X)=(B')^(-1) X B,
    X=B' φ(X) B^(-1).
Thus after vectorizing X, it satisfies the exact invariant Frobenius solution hypothesis with O coefficients. The source's invariant_phi_solution_mem_OE implies X∈M(O). This uses a separate descent theorem for the fixed subgroup, not merely fixedness.

By Section 4 the matrices t^(N') (T')^(-1) and t^N T are integral. Hence their product with f shows t^(N'+N) X is integral. Apply the period-functional descent of V3.50 §4 with M=N'+N. This gives Y over S with eW(Y)=X and T'eW(Y)=fT. The upstream uses the larger exponent 2N'+N; the symmetric bounded-pole statement allows N'+N directly. This is a shorter proof of EquivKisin.main given the same input theorems; no extra integrality of X is assumed.
