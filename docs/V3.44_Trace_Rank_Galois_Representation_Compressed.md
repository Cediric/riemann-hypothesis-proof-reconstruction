# V3.44 — From polynomial trace approximation to a continuous semisimple Galois representation

## Scope and result

Companion to V3.38 Trace_Limit_Uniform_Approximation (not the parallel V3.38 theta-tool document). This is an alternative natural-language proof of the interface of GlobalTheory.Cong.exists_limit_rep, especially its algebraic reconstruction step. It uses a direct trace-pairing rank bound instead of the upstream longer finite_semisimple argument via Cayley–Hamilton, bounded orthogonal idempotents and absence of nil ideals. It is not a new proof of RH, not a claim of mathematical priority, and has not been translated back into Lean or independently kernel-checked.

## 1. Exact interface

Let K = algebraic closure of Q_l, G a compact topological group, d a nonnegative integer, x:J→G dense, L/K's base Q_l a finite intermediate extension, θ:J→L, and C a set of continuous d-dimensional K-representations of G. Assume PolyApprox(θ,{tr(ρ)∘x:ρ∈C}).

PolyApprox means: for every ε>0, there exist finitely many approximant traces f_i and coefficients c_i∈K, THE SAME for every integer-coefficient polynomial P in finitely many indexed coordinates, such that
    |P(θ) − Σ_i c_i P(f_i)| ≤ ε.

Conclusion: there exists a continuous semisimple r:G→GL_d(K) with tr(r)∘x=θ and PolyApprox(tr(r),{tr(ρ):ρ∈C}).

In the application LimFinOddOf, J=G and x=id after V3.38 has constructed the continuous limit t, so the following first step is already available. No assertion that θ is an ordinary limit of individual representations is needed.

## 2. Analytic extension in one argument

Take ε_n→0 and choose the corresponding finite trace sums s_n:G→K. The coordinate polynomials give |s_n(x(j))−θ(j)|≤ε_n. By density and continuity,
    |s_n(g)−s_m(g)| ≤ ε_n+ε_m for every g∈G.

For fixed g choose j_n with |s_n(x(j_n))−s_n(g)|≤ε_n. Then θ(j_n) is Cauchy in the complete finite field L, and defines T(g)∈L. Taking the limit in the Cauchy estimate yields |T(g)−s_n(g)|≤ε_n, so T is continuous and T∘x=θ. This uses completeness of L, not of K.

For any fixed ε and its polynomial witness, each polynomial depends on finitely many group elements; approximate these elements separately by x(J), and take the continuous extension of the polynomial inequality. The same finite witness gives PolyApprox(T,{tr(ρ)}). This proof needs no first-countability assumption on G.

## 3. Universal polynomial identities pass to T

For a polynomial P with K coefficients in finitely many trace coordinates, write P as a finite linear combination Σ_b α_b M_b of integer-coefficient monomials. The same approximation coefficients c_i work for all M_b. By the ultrametric inequality,
    |P(T)−Σ_i c_i P(trρ_i)|≤ ε max_b |α_b|.
Thus if P(trρ)=0 for every ρ∈C, P(T)=0.

In particular T(1)=d, T(gh)=T(hg). Extend T K-linearly to S:K[G]→K. For each ρ extend it to the algebra homomorphism K[G]→M_d(K). Every polynomial identity in traces of finitely many products of group-algebra elements, valid for all these ρ, passes to S, because expanding their finite supports expresses it as a polynomial in finitely many group-trace coordinates. This observation also handles rational coefficients in Newton identities. The constant polynomial is important; the approximation set cannot be empty.

## 4. Direct finite-dimensionality: the rank shortcut

Define the trace radical
    I = {u∈K[G]: S(uv)=0 for all v∈K[G]}.
Cyclicity makes I a two-sided ideal: S((ua)v)=S(u(av)) and S((au)v)=S(u(va)). Since S(I)=0, S descends to a cyclic linear form τ on A=K[G]/I, and the pairing (a,b)↦τ(ab) is nondegenerate in both arguments.

For any g_1,...,g_(d²+1) and h_1,...,h_(d²+1), the determinant
    det [tr(ρ(g_i h_j))] = 0
holds for every d-dimensional ρ: the matrix factors through the d²-dimensional space M_d(K) by the trace pairing. It is an integer polynomial in trace coordinates, so
    det [T(g_i h_j)] = 0.
By multilinearity of the determinant the same holds for arbitrary u_i,v_j in K[G], hence for all a_i,b_j∈A.

If dim_K A>d², choose d²+1 independent a_i. Nondegeneracy says the linear forms b↦τ(a_i b) are independent; the map b↦(τ(a_i b))_i onto K^(d²+1) is therefore surjective (a proper subspace of a finite-dimensional target has a nonzero annihilator). Choose b_j dual to a_i under this map. Their pairing matrix is the identity, contradiction.

Therefore dim_K A≤d². This replaces the upstream proof of finite-dimensionality by a single rank argument; it depends on the stronger PolyApprox-of-actual-traces input, not just an arbitrary trace-like linear form.

For d=0 the same argument gives A=0 and T=0; the unique zero-dimensional representation suffices. Below take d>0.

## 5. Nilpotents have trace zero: Newton polynomial argument

For any a∈A, put p_j=τ(a^j), and define e_0=1 and recursively
    k e_k = Σ_(j=1)^k (−1)^(j−1) e_(k−j) p_j.
These are universal rational polynomials in the p_j. Choose a lift u∈K[G]; then p_j=S(u^j). For a genuine d×d matrix M=ρ(u), e_k is the coefficient of z^k in det(1+zM), hence e_k=0 for every k>d. By Section 3 the same holds for a, independently of its chosen lift.

Consequently E_a(z)=Σ_(k=0)^d e_k z^k satisfies the formal identity
    E_a'(z)=E_a(z) Σ_(j≥1)(−1)^(j−1) p_j z^(j−1).
Indeed coefficient comparison is precisely the Newton recurrence for every k≥1, including k>d.

If a is nilpotent then p_j=0 for all sufficiently large j, so the right-hand series is a polynomial H. If H≠0, deg(E_a H)≥deg(E_a), whereas deg(E_a')<deg(E_a) or E_a'=0; contradiction. E_a≠0 because e_0=1. Hence H=0, in particular τ(a)=0.

The finite-dimensional algebra A has a nilpotent Jacobson radical J(A) (standard finite-dimensional algebra theorem). For a∈J(A) and any b∈A, ab∈J(A) is nilpotent. Thus τ(ab)=0 for all b, and nondegeneracy implies a=0. Therefore A is semisimple.

No nilpotency assumption on K[G] itself has been made.

## 6. Multiplicities are positive integers, not arbitrary weights

Artin–Wedderburn over algebraically closed K gives
    A ≅ ∏_(i=1)^m M_(n_i)(K).
A cyclic linear form on each matrix block is a scalar multiple of the ordinary trace: off-diagonal matrix units have trace zero by cyclicity, and diagonal matrix units have equal trace. Thus τ=Σ_i λ_i Tr_i.

For a rank-one matrix idempotent e in block i, p_j=τ(e^j)=λ_i for every j≥1. The Newton recurrence gives e_k=binomial(λ_i,k) (equivalently, the formal differential equation for (1+z)^λ_i). In particular
    0=e_(d+1)= λ_i(λ_i−1)···(λ_i−d)/(d+1)!.
Characteristic zero implies λ_i∈{0,1,...,d}. Nondegeneracy excludes λ_i=0 on any block. Write m_i=λ_i, positive integers. Normalization gives
    d=τ(1)=Σ_i m_i n_i.

Let A act on V=⊕_i (K^(n_i))^(⊕m_i) by the natural block actions. This is a faithful semisimple A-module of dimension d and has character τ. Composing the group map G→K[G]^×→A^× with this action gives the desired abstract group homomorphism r. Since the images of G span A, G-stable subspaces are A-stable, so r is semisimple as a group representation. Its trace is T.

## 7. Continuity follows from finitely many trace coordinates

Choose group elements h_1,...,h_q whose classes form a basis of A (q≤d²). By nondegeneracy the map
    Φ:A→K^q, a↦(τ(a[h_i]))_i
is an isomorphism. The matrix of r(g) is therefore a fixed K-linear function of
    (T(gh_1),...,T(gh_q)).
Every coordinate is continuous, as is a finite linear combination. The inverse matrices are continuous too, being r(g^(-1)), so r is continuous with the topology on GL_d. The original PolyApprox statement now follows by substitution T=tr(r).

The proof above uses standard Newton identities, finite-dimensional radical nilpotence and Artin–Wedderburn, which are stated explicitly rather than developed from axioms. It gives a mathematical proof of the same interface, not a shorter Lean proof or a proof of the entire Galois-representation theorem. The rank shortcut cannot be substituted for the general upstream finite_semisimple theorem without either its stronger trace-approximation hypothesis or a separate derivation of the rank identities.
