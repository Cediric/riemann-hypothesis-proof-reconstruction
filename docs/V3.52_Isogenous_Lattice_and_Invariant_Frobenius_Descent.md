# V3.52 — Isogenous lattices and invariant Frobenius descent

Pinned source: 7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4. Continues V3.51. Reconstructs the saturated étale lattice argument through a lattice intersection, reduces its determinant-unit criterion to a residue kernel descent, and unifies inhomogeneous Frobenius lifting with the same residue descent. The underlying tilt two-copy descent theorem is retained with its exact hypotheses. This is not a fresh Lean kernel check.

## 1. Saturation as intersection in a fraction field

Write W=W(C^flat), O=bk.OE, p=ℓ. Both W and O are DVRs with uniformizer p, and O⊂W is p-saturated (upstream integral_elements_unit_factorization). In particular Frac(O)∩W=O: write a fraction of O as p^k times an O-unit and use the valuation of W. All embeddings below are the specified ones.

For Y1∈M_d(W) with det(Y1)≠0, set m=v_p(det Y1). In the fraction field of W define
    L = {v∈Frac(O)^d : Y1 v∈W^d}.
The adjugate gives Y1^(-1)∈p^(-m)M_d(W). Hence
    O^d ⊂ L ⊂ p^(-m) O^d
by Frac(O)∩W=O. Thus L is an O-lattice of rank d. Equivalently take a basis P of the O-submodule
    N=p^m L={v∈O^d : Y1 v∈p^m W^d}.
The columns of P are in O, det P≠0, and Y'=p^(-m)Y1P∈M_d(W). The O-linear map P is a basis of N; hence Y' is a basis of Y1L.

If v∈O^d and Y1v∈p^nW^d, then p^(-n)v∈L, hence p^(m-n)v∈N. Thus v=p^(n-m)P w for w∈O^d; equivalently
    p^m v=p^n P w.
If Y'w∈p W^d for w∈O^d, then p^(-1)Y'w∈Y1 L∩W^d in the following precise sense: p^(-m-1)Pw∈Frac(O)^d has integral image under Y1, and hence lies in L. Since p^(-m)P is a basis of L, this implies w∈pO^d. This proves the exact lattice_abstract and exists_lattice_basis conclusions. It avoids the explicit adjugate-vector reconstruction except for the single denominator bound.

Caution: this does not assert that Y' is a W-unit for arbitrary Y1. Saturation only concerns O-vectors; the residue extension can have a nontrivial kernel with no nonzero O-residue vector. Frobenius and Galois conditions are essential for the next step.

## 2. Determinant-unit criterion from residue kernel descent

Suppose Y∈M_d(W) has nonzero determinant and Yw∈pW^d for w∈O^d implies w∈pW^d. Assume
    φ(Y)=YA, A∈M_d(O),
and for each g∈FixFlat there exists an invertible R_g over W with R_g g(Y)=Y. Frobenius preserves the p-adic valuation, so taking determinants gives
    v_p(det A)=v_p(φ(det Y))−v_p(det Y)=0.
In particular A is invertible over W and over O.

Write C=C^flat, k=red(O)⊂C, and bar for reduction modulo p. Suppose det(bar Y)=0. The kernel H=ker(bar Y) is a nonzero C-subspace. Since φ is entrywise pth power on C and A is invertible,
    H=bar A φ(H).
Indeed bar Y bar A φ(v)=φ(bar Y v); equality follows by equal dimensions and perfection of C. The Galois identity gives g(H)=H. These are precisely the conditions of exists_kernel_vector_mem_OE_of_frobenius_stable.

The residue kernel descent (Section 3) gives nonzero vbar∈k^d∩H. Lift coordinates to v∈O^d. Then Yv∈pW^d while v is not zero mod p, contradicting saturation. Therefore det(bar Y)≠0 and Y is invertible over W.

This is SatInvUnit.isUnit_of_saturated. The argument requires no bound on det Y other than nonvanishing, and no invertibility of the Galois matrices over O.

## 3. Kernel descent via the unique normalized projector

The exact source uses the following residue principle T.tilt_invariant_mem_reduction_of_frobenius_equation: for a finite tuple x in C fixed by FixFlat, assume that for every commutative ring T and homomorphisms i1,i2:C→T agreeing on k, their differences Δx satisfy some equation
    Δx_j=Σ_i (Δx_i)^p c_ij
with c_ij∈T. Then all x_j∈k. This principle is not the assertion that every FixFlat-invariant tilt element lies in k.

To apply it to H of Section 2, choose coordinate indices I such that projection to I gives an isomorphism H→C^I. Write Q:C^d→H for the projection given by retaining those coordinates and reconstructing all others. Its I-rows are standard coordinate rows, its columns outside I vanish, and Q|H=id. These conditions uniquely characterize Q. Because H is Galois-stable, g(Q)=Q.

Since H=bar A φ(H), the columns of bar A φ(Q) span H. Choose a matrix Λ with
    Q=bar A φ(Q)Λ.
Also Q bar A φ(Q)=bar A φ(Q) because Q acts as the identity on H.

For any two maps i1,i2 as above, write Q_a=i_a(Q), A*=i1(bar A)=i2(bar A), Λ_1=i1(Λ), and Φ_a=i_a(φ(Q)). The normalized shape implies Q_2 Q_1=Q_2 (the product uses only the I-rows of Q_1). Consequently
    Q_1−Q_2=(I−Q_2)Q_1
            =(I−Q_2)A*(Φ_1−Φ_2)Λ_1.
The subtracted term vanishes because Q_2 A* Φ_2=A* Φ_2. In any nonzero target ring T, characteristic is p and entrywise Φ_1−Φ_2=(Q_1−Q_2)^[p]; the zero ring is trivial. After flattening the matrix this is exactly the required two-copy equation. Thus the residue principle gives Q∈M_d(k). A nonzero column of Q yields a nonzero kernel vector over k.

This condenses EchelonProjectorKernelFrobenius and KernelDescent.two_copies into a canonical-coordinate argument. It is valid for arbitrary rank of H, including full H; the nonzero column exists because H≠0.

## 4. Isogenous-lattice étale basis

Use a preframe T=F.T, height N, and a square integral intertwiner f with det f≠0 satisfying fρ''(g)=ρ(g)f. Assume ρ''(g) is invertible for every g as in EtBasis.main. Let
    Y1=adj(f) T.
Then det Y1≠0, using det adj(f)=(det f)^(d−1) for d≥1; d=0 is trivial. Since φ fixes the integral coefficients,
    φ(Y1)=Y1 B, B=eW(E)^(-N)eW(F.A)∈GL_d(O).
Similarly ρ''(g)g(Y1)=Y1 for g in FixFlat, because adj(f)ρ(g)=ρ''(g)adj(f), a consequence of the intertwining equation after passing to the fraction field.

Choose P,Y' by Section 1. The coefficients of Bφ(P) lie in O, and
    Y1 B φ(P)=p^m φ(Y')∈p^m M_d(W).
By the definition of N in Section 1, every column of Bφ(P) is in N=P O^d. Thus Bφ(P)=P A'' for A'' over O. Cancelling p^m gives φ(Y')=Y'A''. For g in FixFlat, g(P)=P, so ρ''(g)g(Y')=Y'. Section 2 now applies and gives Y'∈GL_d(W).

Set T''=Y', P_final=T^(-1) f Y'. Then
    p^m P_final=(det f) P
has entries in O (the p-adic integral embedding belongs to O). By p-saturation, P_final has entries in O. By construction
    f T''=T P_final.
This gives the exact isogenous-lattice theorem used in V3.51 §2. The lengthy original adjugate manipulation reduces to a fraction-field identity and the lattice intersection. This step uses the residue descent theorem, not just Smith normal form.

## 5. Inhomogeneous Frobenius descent by digit lifting

The same residue principle gives the Witt-level invariant_phi_solution_mem_OE theorem. In row-vector notation assume x,b∈W^s satisfy
    x=φ(x)C0+b,
where C0∈M_s(O), b∈O^s, and every coordinate of x is fixed by FixFlat. Reduce modulo p. For any two maps i1,i2 on the tilt agreeing on k, the difference of the two copies of the equation is
    Δbar x=(Δbar x)^[p] i1(bar C0)
because both coefficients and b agree. Thus the residue principle implies bar x∈k^s.

Choose v∈O^s lifting bar x and write x=v+px1. Substitution gives
    x1=φ(x1)C0+b1,
    p b1=b−v+φ(v)C0.
The right side belongs to O^s and p-saturation implies b1∈O^s. The residual vector x1 remains FixFlat-invariant since v is fixed and p is not a zero divisor.

Repeat this construction with unchanged C0. At each stage
    x=v0+p v1+...+p^(n−1)v_(n−1)+p^n x_n
with all v_j∈O^s. Hence x is approximable modulo every p^n by O-vectors, and the closedness of O gives x∈O^s. The homogeneous theorem is the special case b=0. This is the exact LadicDescentStepToOE / InvariantPhiSolutionMemOE argument, combined into the same residue-descent and lifting mechanism as V3.51's integral Witt argument. Here the residue input is more delicate: a bound on absolute values alone does not imply membership in O.

## 6. Remaining arithmetic input, without hiding it

The two-copy principle in Section 3 is actually proved upstream through the following chain in TiltInvariantMemReductionOfFrobeniusEquation.lean. First identify k as the embedded Laurent field F_q((X)). Multiply the finitely many tilt coordinates by one power of the nonzero topologically nilpotent flat parameter to place them in the tilt valuation ring. Use its injective coefficient-ring retraction onto F_q[[X]]. Maps of that ring into test rings where the flat parameter is invertible extend to the tilt fraction field, preserving agreement on k; the two-copy equation persists with rescaled coefficients. Agreement of valuation-ring maps on F_q[[X]] implies the required FixFlat Galois transport by exists_fixFlat_of_tilt_maps_agree. The theorem descent_of_points_killed_alt then puts the integral coordinates in the image of the retraction, and clearing the flat denominator yields k-membership.

This paragraph is a source-level interface map, not a proof of the two-copy principle. The next target is the descent_of_points_killed argument and its geometric Galois comparison. The Ainf topological basis construction and period-seed inputs also remain separate. V3.52 closes the isogenous-lattice and invariant Witt Frobenius glue conditional on those exact upstream inputs; it does not claim the entire local arithmetic proof independently reconstructed.
