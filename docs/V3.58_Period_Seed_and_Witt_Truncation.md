## 1. One ideal bound for the seed

Write p for the source prime l, h=deg E>0, u=ev(X), A=Ainf, D=DA(rho), rho=p^(-1/h). Retain the closed-disc gauge basic properties: D has ultrametric submultiplicative norm, iA maps A into its unit ball, ||iA(u)^h||≤rho^h, and ||py||=p^(-1)||y|| for every y in D. The Eisenstein relation E=X^h+pQ gives ev(E)∈J=(p,u^h). For every a∈J, write a=pb+u^hc with b,c∈A. The ultrametric inequality gives ||iA(a)||≤p^(-1). More generally J^n maps into the closed ball of radius p^(-n), by finite sums of products. Since ker(theta)=(ev(E)) and iS=iA∘ev, this single estimate gives both seed bounds; the exact scalar equality is the retained gauge property. This replaces coefficient-by-coefficient polynomial estimates. The stronger ideal-power estimate is an immediate consequence, not a claim about a new gauge construction.

## 2. Period units and nonvanishing

Let P=OCb, F=Cb=Frac(P), and j:W(P)→W(F) be the coefficientwise inclusion. Retain P perfect and a domain. For every bk and every tau satisfying bk.IsPer tau, the source supplies epsilon with IsEps epsilon, a unit eta of A, and the equation whose constant coefficient is
    tau_0^p = eta_0 (epsilon-1).
IsEps excludes epsilon=1, and eta_0 is a unit because constant coefficient is a ring map. Thus tau_0≠0, so tau≠0 and j(tau) is a unit by the Witt unit criterion over the field F. Every g∈GQ p acts compatibly with j by a ring automorphism on W(F); hence j(sigmaA(g)tau) is a unit as well. Finally iA(tau)≠0 by the closed-disc injection theorem at rho, using the adic topological basis and 0<rho<1. No injection of the disc map is inferred merely from the unit in W(F).

## 3. Saturation at all precisions simultaneously

For any perfect characteristic-p ring B, the kernel of truncation W(B)→W_k(B) is V^k W(B)=p^k W(B), since Frobenius is bijective and V F=p. This includes k=0 with the zero-length truncation. The inclusion P→F induces an injection W_k(P)→W_k(F), because its underlying map is coordinatewise. The commuting truncation square therefore gives
    j^(-1)(p^k W(F)) = p^k W(P)
for every k≥0, without induction or cancellation in W(F). In particular W(P)/p^k injects into W(F)/p^k. This saturation is independent of bk and tau, so it can be proved once before either period-unit theorem. It replaces the source's successive first-coordinate extraction and p-cancellation, retaining perfection and the standard Witt V/F identities.
