## 1. The exact window property

The paper's H12 requires data at every x in intervals [Z,Z^(3/2)] for arbitrarily large Z, with constants independent of x. The Lean source represents this by Scales.windows:

\[
\forall y,\ \exists Y\geq y,\quad
\forall x\in[Y,Y^\lambda],\ x\in S.Win,
\qquad \lambda>1.
\]

This is the precise “arbitrarily large windows” formulation: for every lower cutoff y, there is a larger interval whose every scale belongs to the admissible set. It does not mean that every sufficiently large starting point Y is itself a window endpoint. The paper's summary table writes the interval exponent as 3/2, while the Lean construction is parameterized by a general lambda > 1 and the final FormBAt theorem accepts that parameter. The exact chosen lambda should be recorded when matching a particular H12 instantiation; the zero-free bridge itself only uses lambda > 1.

FamCore.Windows lambda is the same property for Fm.Win. The construction preserves the window set, so the scale-window statement is not lost when the per-scale kits are assembled into a family.

There are two parameters that must not be confused:

- lambda is the multiplicative window exponent in [Y,Y^lambda];
- FamP.J is an internal family parameter used in the scale normalization and epsilon exponents.

The window exponent is the parameter that corresponds to the J>1 in the H12 interval statement.

## 2. What KitCPW actually promises

KitCPW(lambda,Q) is the central uniform data-supply interface. Its quantifiers say:

1. there is epsilon0 > 0;
2. for every sufficiently small epsilon, choose constants CT2, C8, C5, c6, Cb and a transported alternative K0;
3. for every standard parameter object C satisfying the fixed CT2/C8 and largeness conditions, there are thresholds c1 and c2;
4. for every cD ≥ c1 and ch ≥ c2, there is a threshold x0 and a Scales(lambda) object S;
5. for every scale s in S with the chosen epsilon and x ≥ x0, there is a kit k;
6. every point k.pt(i) satisfies the requested object predicate Q and the thick-set, bad-character, and quadratic-discriminant conditions.

The constants are chosen before the scale s and point index i. This is the formal uniformity that H12 needs.

## 3. Constructing KitCPW from HostWW and ObjCP

The theorem KitS.kitS proves

\[
\text{HostWW}(\lambda,G.req)+\text{ObjCP}(Q)
\Longrightarrow \text{KitCPW}(\lambda,Q).
\]

The two inputs have complementary roles.

### HostWW: a windowed host

HostWW supplies, uniformly for small epsilon:

- the fixed host constants CT2, C5, c6, Cb and transported alternative K0;
- thresholds for the auxiliary real parameters cD and ch;
- a scale threshold x0;
- a Scales(lambda) object S with the window property;
- for each admissible scale s, a rank R, host H, q0, and integer data a,b satisfying HostGood and WinOK.

Thus HostWW is where the actual scale windows enter.

### ObjCP: construct a point inside a host

ObjCP says that, for every admissible parameter, scale, rank, host, q0, and integer data satisfying the host conditions, one can construct a point p with:

- p.L.sc = s;
- the required Val condition;
- HostArith p.D.host;
- p.From H a b c u0;
- CanonInst p.ta.Gx;
- the requested property Has(Q).

This supplies a valid arithmetic/geometric point at every scale already provided by HostWW.

### The combination in KitS.kitS

The proof chooses the common epsilon threshold as the minimum of the HostWW and ObjCP thresholds. It enlarges constants by maxima/suprema and takes the maximum of the two sets of scale thresholds.

For each scale s, HostWW gives the host data; ObjCP then constructs the point for each required index. The proof packages these points into a Kit and carries through the thick-set, bad-character, and QD conditions.

The important point is that the window property comes from HostWW and is preserved unchanged. ObjCP fills the window with actual points.

## 4. From kits to a family with windows

DesignS.designS takes:

- lambda > 1;
- the Minkowski divisibility condition MinkDiv;
- KitCPW(lambda,Q);
- the requested family class G.

It invokes exists_famP_of_kits to assemble the per-scale kits into one FamP Fm. The construction sets

\[
Fm.Win=S.Win.
\]

The proof of G.cls(lambda,Fm) uses S.windows directly, giving FamCore.Windows(lambda) for the assembled family. The other part of G.cls is the eventual G.At condition.

The theorem also preserves the uniform constants and chosen data needed later, including the large-family bounds, thick-set data, chosen top set, bad-character and QD data, code data, and the twisting/distinctness conditions.

MinkDiv is used in the twisting/choice construction. It is a separate number-theoretic input, proved in MinkowskiDivisible.lean; it is not the source of the scale windows.

## 5. The two namespace-specific DesignChoices packages

There are two declarations with this name:

- Formalism.MainNotions.DesignChoices;
- GlobalTheory.EX.DC.DesignChoices.

DesignS produces the DC version. WorkingClassesFrozen.lean supplies the translation to the Formalism.MainNotions version under the corresponding MinkDiv hypotheses.

Do not silently identify the two definitions. The translation theorem is part of the proof route.

The family class produced by DesignS contains G.cls(lambda,Fm), hence the FamCore.Windows(lambda) property. This is the part of DesignChoices that supplies H12's scale windows.

## 6. How the windows are used in the final RH deduction

TopWin separates the final argument into two independent sides.

### ExWin: existence of a windowed family

ExWin supplies, for each sufficiently small epsilon and each transported parameter K, a family Fm with:

- the required family predicate P;
- the uniform family constant EFamC;
- FamCore.Windows(lambda);
- the required HypV condition.

This is the H12/data-supply side. It is obtained from the DesignChoices construction and its namespace translation.

### ChainWin: the arithmetic estimate on every admissible window

ChainWin supplies a uniform Chebyshev inequality on the family windows for every family satisfying P, EFamC, and HypV.

This is the analytic side. It comes from the ChainTied/ChainCore route: the family-level arithmetic inputs are assembled into ChainCore, the growth estimate is proved, and the result is restricted to x in Fm.Win.

### Tie the two sides together

The theorem tiedWin_of_ex combines ExWin and ChainWin into ChebyshevInequalityAtWindowScale.

Then level_of_tiedWin uses:

1. the window property FamCore.Windows(lambda);
2. the Chebyshev bound on every admissible window;
3. FormBAt(lambda), the analytic zero-free criterion.

For any lower cutoff y, choose a window [Y,Y^lambda] with Y ≥ y and Y beyond the Chebyshev threshold. Apply the bound inside that window. Since such windows exist arbitrarily far out, the required large-scale estimate follows and the zero-free level is obtained.

The final chain is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{HostWW}+\text{ObjCP}
\Longrightarrow \text{KitCPW},\\
&\text{MinkDiv}+\text{KitCPW}
\Longrightarrow \text{DesignChoices}
\Longrightarrow \text{ExWin},\\
&\text{ChainTied/ChainCore}
\Longrightarrow \text{ChainWin},\\
&\text{ExWin}+\text{ChainWin}
\Longrightarrow \text{TiedWin}
\Longrightarrow \text{ZeroFreeLevel}
\Longrightarrow \mathrm{RH}.
\end{aligned}
\]

## 7. What remains to prove upstream of H12

The window logic itself is now explicit. The remaining existence inputs are:

- HostWW, or its lower-level construction from HostExQW, ObjC, and WClosed;
- ObjCP, constructed from RecExP and LevelPrime via the admissible-twist/object-existence theorem;
- the precise scope of MinkDiv in the twisting construction;
- the family-level G.At conditions and uniform constants;
- the ChainWin estimate from the arithmetic main inequality (M).

This distinction matters. The Lean source has a precise windowed implication and explicit constructors from HostWW/ObjCP to the family supply. The substantive existence burden is concentrated in those host and object inputs, plus the arithmetic ChainWin estimate.
