## 1. Bounded powers via a finite module, not strong induction on divisibility

Let R be any commutative ring, u regular, with 1-ur invertible for all r in R. Fix x in R and M≥0. Put v=u^M, T=R[1/(ux)], t=v/x in T and S=R[t] inside T (the notation means the image of R together with t). If u is invertible in S, write 1=uP(t), P in R[X]. Then q(t)=0 for q=1-uP, whose constant coefficient is a unit. Write d=deg q (take d=0 for a constant polynomial). Homogenizing gives

    Σ_(i=0)^d q_i v^i x^(d-i)=0 in T.

In R this equality holds after multiplication by (ux)^N for some N. Cancel u^N using regularity. In R[1/v] set z=x/v. Dividing by v^(N+d) yields

    z^N Σ_(i=0)^d q_i z^(d-i)=0.

Since q_0 is a unit, z satisfies a monic polynomial of degree D=N+d over R. Consequently all powers z^j belong to the R-module generated by 1,z,...,z^(D-1). (For D=0 the relation forces R[1/v] to be zero, and the desired bound follows immediately; regularity then forces R to be zero.) Multiplying by v^D puts this whole module inside the image of R. As R→R[1/v] is injective, for every j,

    v^j divides v^D x^j in R.

Thus c=MD works in the exact upstream bounded-power statement. This replaces the strong induction in pow_dvd_pow_mul_pow_of_homogeneous_relation with the standard finite-module characterization of integrality. It also covers M=0 and x nilpotent: the localization T may be zero, but clearing denominators is still valid. No assumption that x is regular is used.

## 2. The other branch: a valuation witness

If u is not invertible in S, take a maximal ideal m containing u and a minimal prime q contained in m. Every regular element of S avoids q: localization at q has only one prime, so an element of q becomes nilpotent, and a regular element cannot do so in a nonzero localization. The image of u is regular in S because it is invertible in T. Therefore its image in S/q is nonzero.

Take an algebraic closure L of Frac(S/q). The standard valuation domination theorem (the Mathlib Ideal.image_subset_nonunits_valuationSubring input) supplies a valuation subring V of L dominating (S/q)_(m/q). Thus the composite f:R→V sends u to a nonzero nonunit. The relation xt=v in S gives f(x) | f(u)^M. This is exactly the alternative of bounded_powers_or_exists_valuationSubring_dvd. The valuation domination theorem is retained as a standard algebraic input.

## 3. Principal completion of a valuation domain

Let V be a valuation domain and a a nonzero nonunit. Write C=lim V/a^n V and A for the image of a. For principal ideals, the kernel of the nth projection is A^n C: a compatible family vanishing modulo a^n can be divided by a^n using its components at levels n+k and cancellation in V. Conversely multiples of A^n vanish at level n. Thus C is complete and separated for (A), with dense image of V. A^n≠0 since a^n is not divisible by a^(n+1) in V. Also A is a nonunit by projection to V/aV.

Every nonzero y in C has a nonzero projection at some n. Lift its projection at n+1 to b in V. Then a^n does not divide b, so b divides a^n by valuation comparability. Write a^n=bc. Since y=b+A^(n+1)w (images in C understood),

    y=b(1+cAw).

The parenthesis is a unit by completeness (or by compatible inverses in the quotients). Thus every nonzero y is associated to an image of b dividing a power of a. Products of two such images cannot vanish because their product divides a nonzero power of A. Hence C is a domain. Any two nonzero elements are comparable for divisibility by comparing their representatives b in V. Thus C is a valuation domain.

This proves the full principal-completion package, including nonzero/nonunit preservation, without assuming the original V is a-adically separated or rank one.

## 4. Perfect completion by cofinal inverse limits

For any perfect characteristic-p ring V and a in V, Frobenius is an automorphism F with F((a^n))=(a^(pn)). Hence it induces compatible ring isomorphisms

    V/(a^n) → V/(a^(pn)).

The subsequence pn is cofinal in the natural numbers. Taking inverse limits gives an automorphism of C=lim V/(a^n), which is precisely its Frobenius (check each component by reduction). Characteristic p in the sense p·1=0 is inherited componentwise. This replaces both the Cauchy-root construction and the separate injectivity argument in PerfectCompletion.perfect. It applies even if V is not a domain or not separated; the zero completion is harmless.

## 5. Closing the perfect valuation point growth theorem

Now suppose p·1=0 in R and every map R→O to a perfect characteristic-p valuation domain, with u mapping to a nonzero nonunit and O complete for that element, kills x. For any M use Sections 1–2. In the witness branch V has characteristic p and is perfect: every element has a pth root in the algebraically closed L, and the root belongs to V since its valuation is the pth root of a value ≤1 (multiplicative convention). Complete V at f(u). Sections 3–4 give an admissible O. The divisibility f(x)|f(u)^M persists in O, contradicting the assumed vanishing because the image of f(u) is nonzero in a domain. Hence the bounded-power branch holds for every M, proving exactly s_points_kpt, the growth input retained in V3.54.

## 6. Geometric comparison: exact terminal rigidity

For perfect R of characteristic p, a characteristic-p target O separated for (a), and maps ψ,ψ' with ψ(y)-ψ'(y) in (a^e) for every y and fixed e>0, choose z with z^(p^k)=y. Then ψ(y)-ψ'(y)=(ψ(z)-ψ'(z))^(p^k) lies in (a^(e p^k)). Separation gives equality. No perfection of O is needed for this last step.

In ExistsFixFlatOfTiltMapsAgree, the two Witt maps agree on the BK coefficient ring, so their quotient by the common image of E yields two injective untilt embeddings agreeing on O_K. The upstream Galois comparison supplies g agreeing on algebraic elements and modulo p on all integral elements. Equality on the flat parameter implies equality on all its inverse Frobenius roots (target Frobenius injective); their untilts are algebraic, so injectivity of the first untilt embedding and the root criterion imply FixFlat(g). The modulo-p comparison on Teichmüller lifts and the quotient divisibility input then give the uniform congruence of Section 6 with a=ψ(flat), e=deg E>0. This closes the terminal step but retains the precise upstream inputs: WittVectorMapBreuilKisinEvEq, ExistsInjectiveMapIntegersClToWittQuotient, ExistsGaloisEqEmbeddingsOCModPrime, UntiltOfTiltRootFlatAlgebraicAndGaloisFix. Their proofs have not yet been reconstructed here.
