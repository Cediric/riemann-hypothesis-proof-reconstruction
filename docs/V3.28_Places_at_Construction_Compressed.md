## 1. What Places_at must produce

For each word u, Places_at(L,C) supplies a local comparison function sg' and seven real parameters. The proof chooses them explicitly:

\[
\begin{aligned}
sg'(w)&=
\frac{\left\lfloor \operatorname{ur}(w,sg(u,w))\right\rfloor}
{\operatorname{ramIdx}(w)},\\
de&=1-\frac{\operatorname{rank}(N_u)}{duS(u)},\\
muMu&=\mu(\operatorname{MuH}(D,u)),\qquad
muMaxMu=\mu_{\max}(\operatorname{MuH}(D,u)).
\end{aligned}
\]

The remaining four quantities are weighted sums over the support and the support of the local level term:

\[
PT=LT=
\sum_{w\in\operatorname{suppT}(u)}
\frac{2\,lE(w)\,\operatorname{lenMN}(u,w)}
{\operatorname{rank}(N_u)},
\]

\[
Rd=RX=
\sum_{w\in\operatorname{supp}}
2\,lE(w)\left(
sg(u,w)-
\frac{\lfloor\operatorname{ur}(w,sg(u,w))\rfloor}
{\operatorname{ramIdx}(w)}
\right).
\]

With these choices, the three abstract unit-weight conditions HypLow, HypLow2, and HypHigh become concrete statements about the first lattice, the corrected lattice, and the local floor-rounding error.

## 2. The inputs to places_at_of

The theorem places_at_of in PlacesP.lean takes the following inputs.

### Lattice and rank inputs

- NprimeDef and NuDef: the definitions of the corrected lattice and the integral comparison lattice;
- NuCommens: finite generation and commensurability of the comparison lattice with the local integral lattice;
- positive rank of every corrected lattice N_u.

The proof first applies nuFull_of to NprimeDef and NuCommens. This identifies the rank of N_u with the arithmetic dimension du. It then uses the local level statement adm26 to obtain the five numerical relations c1–c5. In particular, these determine the rank defect

\[
de=1-\frac{\operatorname{rank}(N_u)}{duS(u)}
\]

and provide the bounds needed by UnitWeightPartition.deficit_bounds.

### Level and denominator inputs

- adm26, obtained from LocalQuadraticParts.LevelTFamily, supplies the local level-prime correction;
- NprimeMuR, supplied by LinkMu, controls the remaining slope/denominator contribution;
- the comparison map and local length bounds are supplied by LinkG.

The relevant LinkG input says, away from the level prime,

\[
\operatorname{LocLe}\bigl(w,\lfloor\operatorname{ur}(w,\tau(w))\rfloor,
N_u,M_u\bigr)
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{lenMN}(u,w)
\leq duS(u)\bigl(\tau(w)+io(w)\bigr).
\]

Here M_u is the local comparison lattice. These statements are what allow the floor-rounded local exponent to be interpreted as a genuine integral divisibility exponent.

### Support and integrality inputs

The proof also requires:

- tau(w) is an integer for every w;
- io(w) is nonnegative;
- tau(w)=0 at places above the level prime T(u);
- outside the support, either the local length vanishes or lenMN vanishes;
- the sum of the local tau-size weights is bounded by b².

The first four are obtained from the family fill/level data and the local lattice comparison. The last is the estimate ta_le, obtained from a Mertens bound for the prime sum.

### Elementary scale inequalities

For every word u, the size package supplies

\[
b^3\leq T(u),\qquad 16f\leq b.
\]

The lemma size_alt turns these into

\[
T(u)>2,\qquad
\frac{2f}{T(u)}\leq\frac12,
\]

and

\[
\frac{4f}{T(u)}b^2+
f\frac{\log(T(u)-1)}{T(u)-2}\leq1.
\]

These are exactly the small denominator and rounding-error inequalities needed in the local partition argument.

## 3. The proof of places_at_of in five steps

The Lean proof can be read as the following mathematical argument.

### Step 1 — Identify the corrected rank

From NprimeDef and NuCommens, finite generation and commensurability imply NuFull. Hence

\[
\operatorname{rank}(N_u)=du.
\]

The five local relations from adm26 then give the deficit parameters required by UnitWeightPartition.

### Step 2 — Replace local exponents by integral floor data

For each place w, define the integer exponent

\[
q_w=\left\lfloor\operatorname{ur}(w,\tau(w))\right\rfloor.
\]

The integrality of tau makes q_w equal to ramIdx(w) times tau(w). At a place over T(u), tau(w)=0. Away from the level prime, LinkG shows that q_w gives an actual lattice inclusion and bounds the corresponding local length.

This is the point where the local exponent correction becomes a usable divisibility statement.

### Step 3 — Compare the two lattices

The theorem nu_le_mram gives the inclusion of the corrected lattice into the integral comparison lattice. The commensurability input and the local divisibility conditions then allow the source to compare their slopes.

The identities mu_eq and muMax_le convert these lattice inclusions and local lengths into the lower- and upper-slope quantities used by UnitWeightPartition.

### Step 4 — Build HypLow, HypLow2, and HypHigh

The chosen sg' is the normalized floor exponent. The chosen de is the rank defect. The parameters muMu and muMaxMu are the slopes of the auxiliary MuH lattice.

- HypLow and HypLow2 follow from the lower-slope comparison, the local level correction, and the explicit rank-defect bounds.
- HypHigh follows from the upper-slope comparison and the local divisibility estimates.
- The remaining quantities PT, Rd, LT, and RX are the corresponding weighted local-length and rounding sums.

The exact sum identities are obtained by expanding the definitions of the weights and using the rank identity from Step 1.

### Step 5 — Close the error budgets

The bound ta_le controls the total tau-size term by b². The size_alt inequalities control the floor-rounding error and the terms involving f/T(u). Finally, LinkMu bounds the residual slope contribution by a constant multiple of X''.

At this point all witnesses required by Places_at have been constructed. No new slope theorem is invoked: the output is the concrete input package consumed by slopesN_at_of.

## 4. The family-level construction placesF_min'

The theorem placesF_min' in PlacesMin.lean upgrades the single-record construction to a uniform family statement.

Its assumptions are:

1. LevelTFamily: uniform local level data;
2. NuCommensF: uniform finite generation and commensurability;
3. TuSize: lower and upper size bounds for the level primes;
4. MuRank: positivity of the corrected ranks;
5. LinkG: the family of local divisibility and length estimates;
6. LinkMu: the family of denominator/slope estimates.

The proof obtains a threshold for each input, then chooses one larger threshold that dominates all of them. It also chooses

\[
C=\max(C_{26},C_{\mu},4)
\]

so that the two constants in adm26 and LinkMu are absorbed by one common constant.

The Mertens estimate supplies the uniform tau-sum bound. The size bounds supply b³ ≤ T(u), 16f ≤ b, and the three inequalities from size_alt. With these uniform choices, places_at_of applies to every record in every family fiber for all sufficiently large x. This is precisely PlacesF.

Then slopesNF_of turns PlacesF into the family-level corrected-lattice slope estimate SlopesNF.

## 5. The compressed dependency chain for H9

The corrected-lattice half of H9 is therefore:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{LevelTFamily}+\text{NuCommensF}+\text{TuSize}
+\text{MuRank}+\text{LinkG}+\text{LinkMu}\\
&\qquad\Longrightarrow \text{PlacesF}\\
&\qquad\Longrightarrow \text{SlopesNF}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last implication is the short UnitWeightPartition argument. The substantive local construction is places_at_of; the uniformization is placesF_min'.

This refines the human exposition's statement that H9 rests on the local level correction and denominator bound: the Lean proof also exposes the lattice commensurability, positive-rank, local divisibility, support, Mertens, and scale-size inputs needed to make that statement precise.

## 6. What remains outside this proof

The present result is conditional on the inputs listed above. It does not prove:

- the family-level local level theorem LevelTFamily;
- NuCommensF from the underlying lattice construction;
- the global denominator/slope statement LinkMu;
- the local divisibility and length statement LinkG;
- the separate first-lattice covolume input H5.

Those are now explicit upstream targets rather than hidden parts of H9. In particular, the main inequality (M) is still not fully reconstructed in natural language.
