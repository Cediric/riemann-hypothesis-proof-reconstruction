## 1. Completeness of the concrete tilt without a norm on the tilt

Let R=OC/pOC, where OC is the rank-one valuation ring of the completed algebraic closure of Q_p, and P=lim_F R, the inverse limit along Frobenius. For y in P write y_j for its jth coordinate. If y is a nonunit, y_0 is a nonunit: otherwise every y_j is a unit since y_j^(p^j)=y_0, and coordinatewise inversion gives an inverse in P. Any nonunit of R is nilpotent. Indeed a lift a in OC cannot be a unit, so |a|<1; some |a|^m≤|p|, and a^m belongs to pOC. This uses the rank-one valuation on OC, not a presumed valuation on P.

Choose m≥1 with y_0^m=0. Then y_j^(m p^j)=0. Consequently any element of the intersection of all y^n P has every coordinate zero.

For a y-adic Cauchy sequence f_n, each coordinate stabilizes, because y_j is nilpotent. The stabilized coordinates define f in P, since Frobenius compatibility holds at each finite pair of coordinates. Coordinatewise convergence alone does not establish y-adic convergence: we supply the divisibility witnesses. For a sequence satisfying f_(n+1)-f_n=y^n b_n, define
    t_k = sum_(i≥0) y^i b_(k+i)
coordinatewise. Each coordinate sum is finite, and Frobenius compatibility follows by taking a common sufficiently large truncation for two consecutive coordinates. Hence t_k=b_k+y t_(k+1) in P, and telescoping gives
    f_0+t_0-f_n=y^n t_n.
This proves convergence in the principal ideal topology itself. Every Cauchy sequence has a subsequence satisfying the displayed successive-difference condition, and convergence of that subsequence implies convergence of the original sequence. Equivalently apply the argument directly to representatives of compatible residues modulo y^n. This establishes completeness and separation, including the case y=0. No domain hypothesis is needed for this inverse-limit argument.

This combines perfection_haus, perfection_tails, summ_of_tails and precomplete_of_summ from NilpotentOfNotIsUnit.lean. For the BK flat parameter it supplies the exact concrete completeness input left in V3.56, and V3.56 §1 then supplies the Teichmuller and mixed (p,[flat]) Witt completeness needed in V3.53.

## 2. General Witt completeness for an arbitrary nonunit

The upstream theorem is stronger than the Teichmuller case. Let A=W(P), z∈A a nonunit, y=z mod p. The standard p-adic unit criterion implies y is a nonunit. For any sequence b in A^N, define the additive operator D_z(t)_k=t_k-z t_(k+1). Section 1 proves D_y is surjective on P^N. Lift a solution modulo p to a sequence t^(0) in A^N. The residual b-D_z(t^(0)) is divisible by p coordinatewise. Divide by p and repeat, obtaining t^(r) with
    b-D_z(sum_(r<N) p^r t^(r)) ∈ p^N A^N.
Since A is p-adically complete and separated, the coordinatewise p-adic sum solves D_z(t)=b. Telescoping as in §1 gives z-adic precompleteness.

For separation, use that P is a domain (the upstream tilt-domain fact) and A is p-torsion-free. If y=0, z∈pA and separation follows from p-adic separation. Otherwise p,z is a regular pair. For J=(p,z), the associated graded argument of V3.53 gives gr_J A=(A/J)[U,V], with U the initial class of p. In particular multiplication by U is injective. It follows that (J^(n+1):p)=J^n: if pa∈J^(n+1) but a∉J^n, choose the largest k<n with a∈J^k; its nonzero initial class would be killed by U, a contradiction. The converse inclusion is immediate. For a∈intersection J^n, reduction modulo p and §1 give a=pa_1. The colon identity implies a_1 is again in every J^n. Iteration gives a∈intersection p^n A=0. This also proves z-adic separation.

For J-adic precompleteness, choose a Cauchy subsequence f_n with f_(n+1)-f_n∈J^(2n), and write the difference as p^n a_n+z^n b_n, using J^(2n)⊂(p^n,z^n). The two series converge respectively p-adically and z-adically by the established precompleteness. Their sum gives a J-adic limit since both principal ideals' nth powers lie in J^n. Thus A is complete and separated for both J and (z), precisely AinfComplete.main. This retains the p-adic Witt completeness input, and replaces the mixed separation induction by a single graded colon identity.

## 3. Exact improvement of the BK divisibility estimate

In Ainf put e=ev(E), x=flat and h=deg E>0. The Eisenstein relation gives e mod p=x^h. The source FlatDisc proves x≠0 and x is a nonunit: if x=0, applying theta to ev(E) gives p times a unit equal to zero in OC; if x is a unit, the relation E=X^h+pQ gives a unit divisible by p after theta, impossible. Also J_BK=(p,X^h)=(p,E), and the radius rho=p^(-1/h) satisfies 0<rho<1 and rho^h=p^(-1).

As x≠0 and P is a domain, p,e is a regular pair. For I=(p,e)=(p)+ker(theta), the exact colon identity of §2 applies:
    (I^(n+1):p)=I^n.
Thus p a∈I^(n+1) implies a∈I^n for every n≥0. This strengthens AinfDiv.mem_pow, which assumes p a∈I^(2n+2). The exponent n+1 is optimal in general for n≥1: the class of p^(n-1) in gr_I A is nonzero (I proper), but p times it belongs to I^n. No claim of a downstream numerical improvement is made until the relevant consumers are audited.

The other two divisibility conclusions of AinfDiv.main are immediate on reduction modulo p: p|ev(X)a implies x times the residue of a is zero, hence p|a; and if for every m there is b_m with p|(a-ev(X)^m b_m), the residue of a lies in every x^m P, hence vanishes by §1. Together with concrete completeness and the Eisenstein relation this reconstructs all seven fields of AinfDiv.main.
