## 1. The exact H5 carrier

The 22 November working exposition identifies H5 as the covolume bound for the lattice of forms at stage zero. The corresponding Lean-side row property is:

- CovTop.CovolumeBoundThirdPlaceSeries in ThirdPlaceSeries/GeometricWeakTies.lean.

It is not merely the statement that a lattice exists. It is an eventual row condition: after selecting the relevant extension and bundle data, the third-place series satisfies the L3 bound for the associated parameter object.

The family-level geometric input feeding this row is:

\[
\text{CovTop.CovolumeBoundOfX}.
\]

In LiftLambdaBound.lean it is a closed family condition asserting that there is a threshold C0 such that, eventually at every family point, every C ≥ C0 satisfies

\[
(p.S(\text{false})).L3(p.N(C,CH)).
\]

Thus there are two distinct layers:

1. CovolumeBoundOfX: the uniform geometric/Arakelov covolume estimate over the family;
2. CovolumeBoundThirdPlaceSeries: the row-level L3 condition after the automorphic extension and bundle data have been selected.

## 2. How the row-level H5 statement is assembled

The theorem linked_bundles_l3_of_open_alt in LinkedBundlesL3OfOpen.lean turns CovolumeBoundOfX into CovolumeBoundThirdPlaceSeries.

Besides the covolume estimate, it requires:

- DiscreteSeriesCover;
- HarishChandraModules;
- CuspRapidDecay;
- CasselmanWallach;
- RayField;
- the extension-law existence statement;
- OpenParameterStatements for the chosen extension parameters.

The proof first obtains the class-sum/divisor identity, combines the open parameter rows, chooses compatible selections, and applies eventual_L3_of_covolume_alt. The discrete-series, Harish-Chandra, cusp-decay, and Casselman–Wallach inputs are used to pass from the selected bundle rows to the required automorphic summand rows.

This is the exact point where the geometric covolume estimate becomes the automorphic third-place-series bound used by the main argument.

## 3. The geometric theorem that supplies CovolumeBoundOfX

The theorem topCovolSlim2_use_alt_alt in TopCovolSlim2Use.lean proves CovolumeBoundOfX from fifteen named inputs:

1. ResolutionCharZero;
2. PrincipalisationCharZero;
3. PoincareLelongInv;
4. HSUse;
5. MoriwakiBigStatement;
6. FarguesExact;
7. ZarhinStatement;
8. NefRR;
9. SiegelToroidalIntegral;
10. ToroidalForFanKS;
11. SemiAbelianLieSeminegative;
12. PointSeparationPair;
13. ArDegree;
14. PublishedHodgeBundleTheorems;
15. GrothendieckDeformation.

The proof is a composition of two geometric packages.

### A. The deep slim-volume estimate

The first eight inputs are passed to

\[
\text{PositivityTheory.HtPt.Ah.deep\_slim\_use}.
\]

They provide resolution and principalisation in characteristic zero, the inverse Poincare–Lelong statement, arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel, Moriwaki bigness, and the Fargues/Zarhin/Nef-RR estimates.

This yields the deep slim-volume bound used by the global covolume theorem.

### B. The toroidal/Hodge bundle package

The remaining inputs construct:

- HypothesesX, using ToroidalForFanKS, SemiAbelianLieSeminegative, PointSeparationPair, and the Fubini–Study/Chow-height formula;
- toroidal charts for the chosen fan;
- HypothesesH, using SiegelToroidalIntegral, the toroidal charts, and ArDegree;
- the Hodge-bundle and Chow-form hypotheses used by topCovolG_alt_alt.

The theorem then invokes topCovolG_alt_alt with the deep slim-volume estimate, the Hodge bundle package, GrothendieckDeformation, and the published Chow-form/vanishing/intersection statements.

The Lean proof is therefore a short composition at the top level, but the mathematical content is concentrated in the named geometric and Arakelov inputs.

## 4. What PublishedHodgeBundleTheorems contains

PublishedHodgeBundleTheorems is not one theorem. It is a structure of published inputs, including:

- toroidal compactification and volume statements;
- arithmetic calculus and abelian de Rham inputs;
- ray-class and semistable-reduction statements;
- strong approximation and Green-series inputs;
- Chern–Weil, Landherr, and Hasse-norm statements;
- semi-abelian hom extension and lowest-alcove results;
- principal-polarisation and abelian multiplication statements;
- Grothendieck–Messing and prismatic Dieudonne/Hodge inputs;
- Kawamata–Viehweg, nef numerics, and L2 extension;
- arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel, Chow-form existence, and cycle heights;
- Serre holomorphic sections and Nadel vanishing;
- principalisation and formal algebraisation.

The source also derives several auxiliary published statements from this structure, including finiteness of Hermitian classes, canonical extension rank one, and proper-intersection length. It can be converted into a classical-theorem bundle once GoodChernWeil implies Mumford proportionality.

The important distinction is that the formal Lean proof checks the use of these inputs; it does not turn the contents of the published geometric theorems into elementary calculations.

## 5. A second, more modular route to CovolumeBoundOfX

CovolXOfStepsStatement.lean contains the theorem

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{GramCondition}
+\text{LiftRecursive}
+\text{LiftTruncation}
+\text{LiftBadSet}\\
&+\text{LiftLambdaBound}
+\text{SizeCondition}
+\text{CoreBound}'
+\text{LiftLowBound}\\
&\qquad\Longrightarrow \text{CovolumeBoundOfX}.
\end{aligned}
\]

It delegates to the assembly theorem covolX_of_lift_bounds_and_coreLe.

This gives a second way to understand the H5 bottleneck:

- the top-level route supplies CovolumeBoundOfX from published Arakelov/Hodge inputs;
- the modular route supplies it from eight analytic/geometric estimates about Gram matrices, recursive lifts, truncation, bad sets, lambda bounds, sizes, core bounds, and low bounds.

The second route is more useful for proof compression because it exposes the intermediate estimates that must be proved. The next audit should determine exactly how topCovolSlim2_use_alt_alt constructs these eight conditions, rather than treating the fifteen published inputs as one opaque package.

## 6. Do not conflate this with the other covolume statements

The source also contains results such as:

- covolume_integralStabiliser_normalisedHaar;
- covolume_pi_lattice_unitary_coordinates;
- the IndefSide and S5/S6 lattice-volume statements.

They are important in the broader lattice/automorphic argument, but they are not interchangeable by name with H5. H5's exact carrier in the human exposition is CovolumeBoundThirdPlaceSeries, whose family-level input is CovolumeBoundOfX. Any proof simplification should preserve that distinction.
