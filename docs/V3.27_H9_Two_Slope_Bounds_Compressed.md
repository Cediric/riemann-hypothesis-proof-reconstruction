# V3.27 — H9 compressed: two slope bounds, one local weight engine

> Scope: reconstruct the Lean-side content of hypothesis (H9), the range of slopes, as two explicit inequalities. This proves the formal implication from the named local packages; it does not independently prove the arithmetic construction of those packages.

## 1. The correct objects

Fix one record D and its parameter structure L.

Let

\[
\Lambda_0(u)=(D.fm\,u).lat\,0
\]

be the first lattice in the chain, and let

\[
N_u=D.div.Nu\,u
\]

be the corrected/second lattice. Write \(\mu(\Lambda)\) for the slope of a lattice and \(\mu_{\max}(\Lambda)\) for its maximal sublattice slope.

The two estimates below are the Lean-side form of the “range of slopes” hypothesis. They are not one inequality about one lattice: one controls the first lattice, and the other compares the first and corrected lattices.

## 2. First slope estimate: the first lattice

The declaration SlopesM_at in QuadraticLogDerivCongr.lean is exactly

\[
-C\,|TT|\,a\,L_a\left(1+\frac{m}{k}\right)
\leq \mu(\Lambda_0(u)),
\qquad
\mu_{\max}(\Lambda_0(u))
\leq C\,|TT|\,a\,L_a.
\tag{H9-M}
\]

The theorem slopesM_at_of proves this from the following inputs:

- Conclusion5 and Conclusion6 for the two parts of the weight/prime estimate;
- NumsL and WtsL, which control the relevant numerical and weight data;
- ColVal, which identifies the values attached to each word;
- a bound for log T(u), and the elementary estimate log k ≤ 5 log x;
- the two size inequalities
  \[
  f k(b'+S_c)\leq |TT|a,\qquad fk^2\leq |TT|a.
  \]

The proof has a short mathematical structure:

1. Use the top-place weight bound to replace each distinguished weight a_t by at most 2a.
2. Sum over the top set; the source proves that the top-set cardinality is |TT|.
3. Apply the lower and upper numerical estimates from Conclusion5/6.
4. Absorb the remaining terms using the two size inequalities and the logarithmic bounds.

The technical inequalities jnt_slope_low and jnt_slope_up perform the final absorption. They are elementary real inequalities once the listed inputs are available. The output constant is explicit:

\[
C_M=2C_c+\max(\max(C_5,C_6),0)(12+C_c+c_L).
\]

Thus the first half of H9 is a consequence of the weight and prime-sum estimates; its difficult content is upstream in Conclusion5/6, NumsL, WtsL, and ColVal.

## 3. Second slope estimate: comparison with the corrected lattice

The declaration SlopesN_at is

\[
\mu_{\max}(N_u)
\leq
\mu_{\max}(\Lambda_0(u))
+G_a+F_o-E_e(u)+C\,mB_0,
\tag{H9-N-upper}
\]

and

\[
\mu(\Lambda_0(u))+G_a+F_o-E_e(u)-\operatorname{LoU}(u)
\leq \mu(N_u),
\tag{H9-N-lower}
\]

where

\[
\operatorname{LoU}(u)=
C\,\operatorname{IOsum}(L)
+C\,|TT|\,m\left(\frac ak+b'\right)\log T(u)
+C\,X''_n+1.
\]

This pair compares the corrected lattice against the first lattice after adding the gain term G_a, the auxiliary term F_o, and subtracting the local error E_e(u).

The theorem slopesN_at_of proves the pair from Places_at(L,C), together with C ≥ 4 and IOsum(L) ≥ 0.

Places_at supplies three weighted local inequalities:

- HypLow and HypLow2, which give the lower-slope information;
- HypHigh, which gives the upper-slope information;

and two quantitative remainder bounds:

\[
LT\leq C\,|TT|\,m\left(\frac ak+b'\right)\log T(u),
\qquad
RX\leq C\,X''_n.
\]

The proof then consists of:

1. apply UnitWeightPartition.lower_bound_simplified to HypLow and HypLow2;
2. apply UnitWeightPartition.claimHigh_valid to HypHigh;
3. rewrite the resulting weighted sums using the exact identities for G_a, F_o, and E_e(u);
4. absorb the remaining IOsum term using C ≥ 4 and IOsum(L) ≥ 0;
5. absorb B_0 using the nonnegativity supplied by the loss bound.

This is the second half of H9. Its hard input is not a hidden slope theorem: it is the local weight package Places_at.

## 4. How Places_at is structured

In TopDensityNGeSixteen.lean, Places_at(L,C) is an explicit existential package. For each word u it supplies:

- a local comparison function sg';
- the auxiliary real parameters de, muMu, muMaxMu, PT, Rd, LT, RX;
- UnitWeightPartition.HypLow;
- UnitWeightPartition.HypLow2;
- UnitWeightPartition.HypHigh;
- the LT and RX bounds above.

The family-level proposition PlacesF requires a uniform C and a threshold x0 such that Places_at holds for every record in every family fiber with x ≥ x0, together with IOsum ≥ 0. The theorem slopesNF_of then turns PlacesF into SlopesNF.

The source therefore separates two tasks cleanly:

- the slope conversion theorem, which is short and explicit;
- the construction of Places_at uniformly over the family, which contains the substantive local arithmetic and denominator estimates.

The human exposition says H9 rests on the denominator bound and the local statement behind H2. On the Lean side, the denominator input is represented by LinkN/LinkN_at and the finite Sha data; the local level correction is carried by ErrDefs and the E-definition. The exact path from those inputs into every field of Places_at remains to be checked, so this document does not claim that the construction of Places_at has been completed here.

## 5. Family-level and one-scale interfaces

The family-level properties SlopesMF and SlopesNF are eventual-in-x statements: beyond a threshold, every record in every fiber satisfies the corresponding first- or corrected-lattice estimate with one uniform constant.

These are among the explicit premises consumed by linkCore in LossesSmallShares.lean. They are then converted into the LChain slope and quotient-slope components inside the one-scale ChainCore package.

The logical route is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Conclusion5/6, NumsL, WtsL, ColVal, size bounds}
  \Longrightarrow \text{SlopesMF},\\
&\text{PlacesF}
  \Longrightarrow \text{SlopesNF},\\
&\text{SlopesMF and SlopesNF plus the remaining family inputs}
  \Longrightarrow \text{ChainTied}
  \Longrightarrow \text{ChainCore}.
\end{aligned}
\]

## 6. What is now reduced, and what remains

**Reduced:** H9 is no longer a single opaque “slope range” statement. It is the conjunction of (H9-M) and (H9-N-upper/lower), with explicit proof conversions:

- slopesM_at_of: weight/prime estimates → first-lattice slope bounds;
- slopesN_at_of: Places_at → comparison of corrected and first lattices;
- slopesNF_of: uniform PlacesF → family-level corrected-lattice bounds.

**Not yet reduced:** the construction of Places_at from the local level correction, denominator/cohomology bounds, and the other local inputs. This is where H2 and the denominator theorem enter. The other deep assumed input H5—the covolume of the first lattice—also remains a separate bottleneck for the full main inequality (M).
