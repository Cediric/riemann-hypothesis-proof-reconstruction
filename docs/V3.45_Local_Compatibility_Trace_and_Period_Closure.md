# V3.45 — Local compatibility: trace identities, joint approximation and period closure

Companion to V3.38 (trace limit) and V3.44 (trace-rank reconstruction). Pinned source 7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4. This document separates the elementary local compatibility argument from the arithmetic-geometric closure theorem. It gives a condensed proof of the elementary parts and a source-level reconstruction of the deep part; it does not replace the period-ring theorems by an unproved continuity assertion.

## 1. One common limit, different approximation families

Use the notation of limWU4_of. For every finite place set Y, define I_Y={i∈I: i.1.1=Y}. Let C_Y={ρ_i:i∈I_Y}. The congruence construction supplies a polynomial approximation of the target joint eigenvalue system by the systems indexed by I_Y. Projecting away the local power-sum coordinates gives hpaY Y.

V3.38 produces a single continuous function t on G and preserves approximation for every admissible subset. V3.44 gives one semisimple continuous r with tr r=t. Consequently LinApprox(r,C_Y) holds for EVERY Y, not merely the union of the C_Y. The representation is not reconstructed independently for each place. This distinction is essential: different places may require different approximants, but all conditions are imposed on the same r.

In the source:
    hpaJ Y → approx_left → hpaY Y
    exists_trace_limit → ht
    exists_limit_rep → r, htr
    ht + htr + linApprox_of_polyApprox_trc → hlin.
For a fixed place w choose Y={w}. No uniform approximation with a single individual seed representation is claimed.

## 2. Kernel inheritance as a group-algebra annihilator principle

Let K be a characteristic-zero algebraically closed normed field, G a group and r a semisimple representation. Suppose tr r is uniformly approximable by finite K-linear combinations of tr ρ for ρ∈C, in the exact LinApprox sense. Write S_r and S_ρ for the linear trace maps on K[G].

More generally than ker_of_linApprox, if u∈K[G] is annihilated by all approximating representations, then r(u)=0. Indeed for any v∈K[G], S_ρ(uv)=0 for all ρ∈C. Write uv as a finite sum Σ α_g[g]. Uniform linear trace approximation implies
    |S_r(uv) − Σ_i c_i S_(ρ_i)(uv)| ≤ ε Σ_g |α_g|.
The approximant sum is zero, and ε→0 gives S_r(uv)=0. The span B=r(K[G]) is a finite-dimensional semisimple algebra: the representation is faithful as a B-module and is semisimple, since G-stable and B-stable subspaces coincide. Artin–Wedderburn identifies B with a product of matrix algebras; the trace on the representation is a sum of ordinary traces weighted by positive integer multiplicities. Hence (X,Y)↦Tr(XY) on B is nondegenerate in characteristic zero. Since S_r(uv)=Tr(r(u)r(v)), r(u)=0.

Thus the intersection of group-algebra kernels of the approximants is contained in the group-algebra kernel of r. In particular, u=[s]−[1] gives
    (∀ ρ∈C, ρ(s)=1) ⇒ r(s)=1.
The source proves this special case by estimating |tr r(sg)−tr r(g)|≤2ε and using nondegeneracy.

For w away from ℓ in W0, the seed compatibility hseedFE gives trivial action on inertia for every seed in C_{ {w} }. Apply the result to each inertia element and each chosen place A over w. No assertion of local semisimplicity is required: semisimplicity of r on the FULL global group is sufficient.

Warning: equality of characters does not in general imply equality of nonsemisimple representations. For example the unipotent representation of the additive group Z_ℓ given by g↦[[1,g],[0,1]] and the trivial two-dimensional representation have identical traces but different kernels. This is why the global semisimplicity hypothesis cannot simply be discarded.

## 3. Frobenius characteristic polynomial: only d positive moments needed

The exact source FrobOpsIdx constructs indices
    j=(w,A,s,n), n≥1,
where s is the specified Frobenius lift and x(j)=s^(−n).
The local operator eigenvalue at j is the n-th power sum of the normalized Satake multiset β=algSat(d,Nw,B), of cardinality d. In particular the index set includes ALL positive n, not just n=1; the coverage statement hcov records n=1 for density, whereas hconv uses every positive n.

For fixed w and j above w, all i∈I_{ {w} } satisfy η_i(j)=tr ρ_i(x(j)) by seed Frobenius compatibility and hidx. V3.38's subset interpolation conclusion therefore gives
    tr r(s^(−n))=θ(j)=ι^(−1)(Σ_b∈β b^n).
Under ι these are exactly the power traces of M=ι(r(s^−1)). For n=1,...,d they determine the characteristic polynomial by
    e_0=1,
    k e_k=Σ_(j=1)^k (−1)^(j−1)e_(k−j)p_j,
    char_M(X)=Σ_(k=0)^d (−1)^k e_k X^(d−k).
The same recurrence computes the elementary symmetric functions of β, so char_M(X)=∏_(b∈β)(X−b).

This is the complete elementary argument after hseedFE, hidx and the interpolation property, using the source's inverse-Frobenius convention. No convergence of individual characteristic polynomials is used. In particular it would be wrong to apply a nonlinear polynomial in traces to an arbitrary linear combination of matrices and claim it commutes with that combination.

## 4. Joint approximation: keep the same coefficients

For places above ℓ, the congruence system includes coordinates
    θ'(w,k)=ι^−1(c_w(k) PowSatU(U,w,k)), k∈Z,
and the analogous η'_i. The normalizers c_w(k) are nonzero.

Fix a Weil element σ. The desired Satake Weil–Deligne power sum S(n) is obtained from the coordinate k=−deg(σ)n by multiplying by u_n=ι^−1(c_w(k))^−1. The seed power sums s(ρ_i,n) are obtained with exactly the same u_n, using the seed WD condition.

For any finite range 0≤n≤N and ε>0 choose
    M=max(1,max_(0≤n≤N)|u_n|), δ=ε/M.
Take ONE polynomial approximation witness F⊂I_{ {w} }, coefficients c_i, at precision δ for the concatenated system (θ,θ'). Restriction to the left coordinates and V3.38's witness-preserving estimate give
    |tr r(g)−Σ_i c_i tr ρ_i(g)|≤δ≤ε for every g.
Coordinate evaluation on the right gives simultaneously
    |S(n)−Σ_i c_i s(ρ_i,n)|≤|u_n|δ≤ε for n≤N.
If different indices correspond to the same representation, sum their coefficients; the local observable s is a function of that representation for the fixed local datum, so this preserves both sums.

This is precisely JointApprox of CongPhi, proved in the source by joint. It is a finite simultaneous approximation, not a conclusion that the local WD power sums of r equal S. The latter needs the subsequent ClosedPhi/closed trace machinery. The same coefficients are the essential information lost if one constructs unrelated global and local approximations.

## 5. Above ℓ: period closure is a separate arithmetic theorem

Source crysDRU_of_linApprox takes ClosedAtL as an input. Each approximant has CrysDRU at w and matches the target weight there (SameWtAt). It constructs a multiset J(τ) of Hodge jumps from the target automorphic weights. For each local embedding τ the seed representation has the same multiset, and crystalline implies semistable for the local model. ClosedAtL returns:
1. de Rham of the limit local representation;
2. exact prescribed Hodge jumps at every induced embedding;
3. crystalline of the limit if all approximants are crystalline.

The actual closedAtL theorem is supplied unconditionally as a Lean declaration, not an extra assumption of limWU4_of. However its human proof depends on the following constructions, which must be kept visible.

### 5.1 Global trace cover, then local pullback

cover_exists constructs a Z_ℓ integral image-span lattice for the global r with matrices b_i, action R, and a read vector e. It uses trace nondegeneracy to control divisibility in that lattice. For n it selects trace approximants with error ℓ^(−(n+K+c)), and enlarges their finite support cumulatively. Each approximant admits a finite coefficient extension and stable spanning lattice.

The layer-basis theorem builds an auxiliary integral lattice representation R'_n inside the tuple of seed matrix spaces, with read maps to each seed and a map P_n to the fixed target lattice. The map P_n is equivariant/surjective modulo ℓ^n in the precise Onto sense, reads the identity up to ℓ^n, and satisfies a trace comparison bound on each basis vector:
    |Tr(Σ_i (P_n)_(i,s) b_i) − Σ_k c_(n,k) Tr(b'_(s,k))|≤ℓ^(−n).
A transition map f_n from layer n+1 to n is exactly equivariant, with P_n f_n≡P_(n+1) mod ℓ^n.
These are the concrete components of Cover (not ordinary convergence of matrices of the same dimension).

closedAtL pulls this entire structure back along the continuous local decomposition-group map j_l:G_K→G_E. The target restriction need not be semisimple; the global cover is built before restriction. The target local finite-field model is obtained by finite coefficient descent and may be enlarged to contain any requested coefficient extension and the split local coefficient field L0.

### 5.2 Bounded-period-frame closure

closed_of_cover defines one bound
    N=max_(τ, h∈J(τ)) |h|
(with zero for an empty multiset). The layer representations qRep R'_m are semistable via isSemistable_latticeInMatrixAlgebraLayer. The layer D_dR bounds give Breuil–Kisin frames F_m of common pole bound N, with de Rham condition and crystalline condition whenever the layer is crystalline.

The theorem exists_deRhamFrame_limitLattice_breuilKisinTower uses the tower maps and uniform bound to construct a target frame F_0 for the fixed lattice R. Then isDeRham_of_breuilKisinFrame_isDeRham gives de Rham for R; deRham_crystalline_of_lattice_reads passes this to the target local representation. If all seeds are crystalline, the corresponding crystalline conditions hold on each layer and F_0, and the same read theorem gives crystalline for the target.

This is the point where the period-ring theory is genuinely needed. It is not an application of character continuity alone.

### 5.3 Exact jumps: an algebraic recovery after period comparison

The source chooses a Galois element and cyclotomic-period value z≠0 that is not a root of unity. The adapted Hodge basis produces matrices U and U'_m with eigenvalues z^h for the relevant integer weights h. The bounded compatible frame theorem gives uniform entry bound B and for every n some m≥n with
    ||U P_m−P_m U'_m||_max≤B ℓ^(−n)
(after the source's indicated coefficient maps).

At each layer's seed read, the trace of the n-th power of the Hodge operator is the common value
    S(n)=Σ_(h∈J(τ)) (z^h)^n.
The trace_pow_eq_along_latticeTowerCover theorem transfers this exact scalar to the target read, using the trace comparison and compatibility above. In particular the n=0 normalization is S(0)=d. The target Hodge basis then identifies the read with the power sums of its own weight multiset.

The final step can use the SAME Newton recovery as Section 3: the two multisets of d values z^h agree because their first d positive power sums agree. Since z is not a root of unity, h↦z^h is injective on Z, hence the two integer weight multisets agree with multiplicities.

The essential unexpanded inputs are now bounded Breuil–Kisin frame existence, limit-frame construction, lattice read transfer, and trace-power transfer. Nothing in this document claims an independent proof of these deep statements.

## 6. Exact conclusion and remaining branch

The three output clauses of limWU4_of are now separated:
- away from ℓ, the unramified spherical Frobenius compatibility (inertia and inverse-Frobenius characteristic polynomial);
- above ℓ, CrysDRU with fixed Hodge weights via ClosedAtL;
- above ℓ, a compatible family C with semistability/jumps and JointApprox of WD power sums.

The final clause is NOT by itself full equality of WD parameters. Trace-power closure for the actual local WD representation is a later step; monodromy requires further information as well. This distinction prevents accidentally promoting the intermediate LimWU4 statement to the stronger Row0b compatibility theorem.
