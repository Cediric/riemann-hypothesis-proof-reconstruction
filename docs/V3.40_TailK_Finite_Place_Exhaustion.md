# V3.40 — Tail_k compressed: finite-place exhaustion and continuity from above

> Upstream snapshot: CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean at commit 7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4.
>
> Scope: reconstruct the proof of Tail_k. The key observation is that the tail estimate is a continuity-from-above argument for a finite weighted measure over an increasing collection of finite sets of places.

## 1. The target inequality

Given an adelic height function a, an embedding epsilon, an exponent t for which

\[
g\longmapsto a(\epsilon(g,1))^t
\]

is integrable, and any initial finite set S0 of places, Tail_k produces a finite S containing S0 such that

\[
\int_{\{c\in gOff(S):\neg IntAll(c)\}}
a(\epsilon(c,1))^t\,d\mu_C
<
\mu_C\{c\in gOff(S):IntAll(c)\}.
\tag{Tail}
\]

The right side is the measure of the integral off-place subgroup. The nonintegral tail is made strictly smaller.

## 2. The finite weighted measure

Let

\[
U=\{g\in UA:IntAll(g)\}.
\]

The decomposition theorem gives U compact and open, with the identity in U. Hence

\[
0<\mu(U)<\infty.
\]

The height invariance condition IsHgt says that right multiplication by an integral element does not change the height:

\[
a(\epsilon(gu,1))=a(\epsilon(g,1))
\qquad (u\in U).
\]

Therefore the weight

\[
f(g)=\operatorname{ofReal}\bigl(a(\epsilon(g,1))^t\bigr)
\]

is locally constant on the relevant integral cosets.

Define the finite weighted measure

\[
m(A)=\int_A f\,d\mu.
\]

The assumed integrability gives

\[
m(UA)<\infty.
\]

## 3. Exhaust the nonintegral locus by finite-place conditions

For a finite set S of places, define

\[
A(S)=
\{g:\ IntAt(v,g)\text{ for every }v\in S\}\setminus U.
\]

Three facts are immediate from the definitions.

1. A(S) is measurable: each local integrality condition is closed, and U is open.
2. If S ⊆ S', then A(S') ⊆ A(S); the family is antitone.
3. The intersection over all finite S is empty. Indeed, if g is locally integral at every place, then IntAll(g), so g∈U and cannot lie in A(S).

Thus

\[
\bigcap_{S\text{ finite}}A(S)=\varnothing.
\]

Since m is finite, continuity from above gives

\[
m(A(S))\longrightarrow0
\]

as S increases through finite sets of places.

Because μ(U)>0, choose S containing S0 such that

\[
m(A(S))<\mu(U).
\tag{1}
\]

This is the entire finite-place selection argument.

## 4. Convert the global estimate into the off-place tail estimate

The remaining step is measure-theoretic. Decomp_k gives a topological group decomposition

\[
UA \simeq gInf\times gAt(S)\times gOff(S)
\]

and the corresponding Haar-measure factorization.

The auxiliary lemma abs_tail_pk applies to:

- the open compact integral set U;
- the finite weighted measure m;
- the integral subset K of gOff(S);
- the strict estimate (1).

It converts (1) into

\[
\int_{K^c}F(c)\,d\mu_C<\mu_C(K),
\]

where \(F(c)=a(\epsilon(c,1))^t\) and \(K=\{c:IntAll(c)\}\). This is exactly (Tail).

## 5. The compressed proof

The full Lean proof in TPGTail.lean can be summarized in four lines:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{integrability}+\text{height invariance}\\
&\Longrightarrow \text{finite weighted measure }m,\\
&\bigcap_{S\text{ finite}}A(S)=\varnothing
\Longrightarrow m(A(S))\to0,\\
&\mu(U)>0
\Longrightarrow \exists S\supseteq S_0,\ m(A(S))<\mu(U),\\
&\text{Haar product decomposition}
\Longrightarrow \text{Tail}.
\end{aligned}
\]

So Tail_k is not a separate deep automorphic theorem. It is a general finite-place exhaustion lemma plus the adelic product decomposition.

## 6. Why this matters for NonVan_k

In V3.39, Tail_k was the mechanism that made the nonintegral off-place contribution smaller than the integral compact contribution.

The present reduction shows that the strict inequality comes from:

- integrability of the height weight;
- local constancy under integral right translation;
- compact openness and positive Haar measure of the integral subgroup;
- countability/exhaustion by finite sets of places;
- Haar measure decomposition.

This isolates the genuine inputs and removes the appearance of a mysterious global tail estimate.
