# V3.31 — The LinkG no-kernel proof: compressing the family constructor

> Upstream snapshot: CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean at commit 7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4.
>
> Scope: make the internal construction of LinkG_fam_noKer explicit. The goal is to replace a long Lean term with a short dependency graph and identify which family hypotheses are actually doing the work.

## 1. The two quantitative theorem inputs

The constructor takes two named family estimates.

### FirstTheoremAtFamily

There are constants CH and C satisfying

\[
0\leq CH\leq k_I,\qquad CH\leq D_I,\qquad 2CH\leq\rho,
\]

\[
0\leq C,\qquad 2C\leq\kappa_{\mathrm{threshold},1},
\qquad C\leq\kappa_{\mathrm{threshold},2},
\]

and the family estimate flipOneThree.Conclusion2Family holds with La, CH, and C.

The theorem family_first_bound_of_theorem converts this into Adm8F, using KapIDefF and the elementary type-one bound Joints.type_one_hypothesis_of_bounds.

### SecondTheoremAtFamily

There is a constant C with

\[
0\leq C\leq
\min(k_{II},t_{II},La,D_{II},sz_{II},k'_{II}),
\]

and tableLineDegreeFive.OrbitValuationBound holds uniformly on the family.

The theorem family_second_bound_of_theorem converts this into Adm6F by applying Joints.type_two_hypothesis_of_counts after the elementary scale comparisons Nm_le_Sc, w''_le, kS_le, and two_b'_le_a.

These are the only two inputs to linkG_fam_noKer that are explicitly named “first theorem” and “second theorem.”

## 2. The middle admissibility package is largely derived

The proof of linkG_fam_noKer constructs the following pieces before invoking linkG_without_kernels:

| Component | Construction |
|---|---|
| Adm6F | SecondTheoremAtFamily via family_second_bound_of_theorem |
| Adm7F | KapIDefF via family_kappa_bound |
| Adm8F | FirstTheoremAtFamily via family_first_bound_of_theorem |
| Adm9F | Weyl sandwich plus exponent bound, with constant Ce |
| OrbitLatticeBounds | Linear programming from Adm6F, Adm7F, StdSubAt, hypIAt, hypIIAt, and CbLarge |
| LatticeSandwich | sandwich_of_exponent_clause applied to Adm9F |

Two details matter:

- KapIDefF is obtained directly from the record field kapI_def in Fm.ex. Thus Adm7F is not an additional arithmetic theorem.
- Adm9F is obtained from the model Weyl sandwich and the exponent bound at twisted places. It is not a separate unexplained slope assumption.

The proof also obtains a family global-order estimate from UnusedSlot33.field_lemmas_except_seven. It then constructs:

- Adm1F from family_first_lemma;
- Adm3F from family_orders_first_part and the global-order estimate;
- Adm4F from family_orders_bound_of_lemma and the family-window log bound;
- Adm5F from family_sizes_bound;
- AdmUF from the unit-bound data;
- the first-growth bound from the family size package.

Adm2F and NPDescF are passed as explicit inputs.

## 3. The linear-programming step

The OrbitLatticeBounds package is obtained from:

1. Adm6F, the type-two/count estimate;
2. Adm7F, the kappa-share estimate;
3. StdSubAt, the standard-subspace condition;
4. hypIAt and hypIIAt, the two orbit hypotheses;
5. CbLarge, the large-scale comparison bound.

Joints.linear_programming_input_of_hypotheses combines these estimates with the global relation between rho and rhoB. The output is exactly the orbit-lattice bound required by the no-kernel theorem.

This is the key finite-dimensional optimization step in the LinkG construction. It should be stated as one lemma in a human proof, rather than reproducing the Lean tuple projections.

## 4. The no-kernel theorem and the final conversion

The theorem linkG_without_kernels consumes:

- Adm1F through Adm9F;
- the unit-bound package AdmUF;
- TrivialStabiliserFiniteClasses;
- preservation of lattices by the relevant operators;
- OrbitLatticeBounds;
- LatticeSandwich;
- NPDescF.

It sets the auxiliary chain character to zero, so the kernel contribution vanishes. The admissibility package then gives the no-kernel hypothesis required by AuxBlock8.Pf.stmtF.

That theorem returns a constant C, a function delta(x) tending to zero, and the family conclusion Conclusion₂ with error scale b^delta(x).

Finally linkG_at_of turns Conclusion₂ and the local fill/support/tied-lattice data into the five clauses of LinkG_at. The family constructor enlarges the constant to 4|C|+1 and takes one threshold dominating all component thresholds.

In compressed form:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{FirstTheoremAtFamily}
+\text{SecondTheoremAtFamily}
+\text{KapIDefF}\\
&\quad+\text{StdSubAt}
+\text{hypIAt}+\text{hypIIAt}+\text{CbLarge}
+\text{Weyl/exponent bounds}\\
&\Longrightarrow
\text{Adm1--Adm9}+\text{OrbitLatticeBounds}
+\text{LatticeSandwich}\\
&\Longrightarrow
\text{no-kernel admissibility}\\
&\Longrightarrow
\text{Conclusion}_2\text{ with }b^{\delta(x)}\\
&\Longrightarrow \text{LinkG}.
\end{aligned}
\]

## 5. Where the first and second theorem estimates come from

The source also has a local-to-global closure theorem, link_of_local_closed. It uses a local condition Loc and closed family hypotheses to obtain the two theorem families:

- second_theorem_closed_form, built from the extremal-projector tools and local model results;
- first_theorem_closed_form, built from threshold functions and the local model/flatness results.

The other closed conditions supply signs, ones, Cb, trivial stabilizers, standard subspaces, Adm2F, NPDescF, and the family-window logarithmic bound. These are then passed to linkG_fam_noKer.

The local condition itself is proved by loc_proved, which applies the restricted criterion to TraceSpectra.stmtE.

This means the outer route is not a circular assumption of LinkG: the two theorem-family bounds are constructed from the local theorem package, and the no-kernel argument converts them into the local divisibility output.

## 6. The remaining independent premises

The current compressed proof still depends on:

- Loc, through TraceSpectra.stmtE and its restricted criterion;
- TrivialStabiliserFiniteClasses;
- StdSubAt;
- Adm2F;
- NPDescF;
- CornerGLContinuity.FamilyWindowLogBound;
- the first/second theorem local model and flatness inputs.

The family constructor is now explicit, but these premises still need to be traced to the underlying arithmetic and geometric construction. In particular, NPDescF is used to turn the lattice comparison data into local length bounds, while StdSubAt and the orbit hypotheses feed the linear-programming step.

## 7. Effect on the RH proof simplification

V3.30 identified LinkG as the remaining local-divisibility branch for H9. V3.31 shows that its family constructor has a compact architecture:

1. two quantitative theorem bounds;
2. one trivial kappa identity;
3. a Weyl/exponent estimate;
4. one linear-programming lemma;
5. a no-kernel argument;
6. conversion to LinkG_at.

The corrected-lattice half of H9 is therefore no longer a mysterious auxiliary assumption. Its proof can be presented as a sequence of named lemmas with a clear division between analytic estimates, finite-dimensional optimization, and lattice divisibility.

H5 (the first-lattice covolume) and H12 (uniform scale-window supply) remain separate obstacles to a full human proof of the arithmetic main inequality (M).
