# V3.49 — Coefficient descent and the uniform right inverse in a linked preframe

Pinned Lean source 7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4. Continues V3.47–V3.48. This replaces several elementwise computations by linear-map and quotient-matrix identities, and isolates the coefficient ideal theorem that makes the right-inverse denominator uniform. No new Lean verification or claim of novelty.

Notation: p=ℓ, S=bk.S, A=Ainf, W=WCb, e:S→A, i:A→W, eW=i∘e, OE=bk.OE. All maps on matrices are entrywise unless stated otherwise.

## 1. One S-linear functional descends an entire matrix congruence

The source AinfRetraction.main provides an additive map c:A→S with c(e(s)a)=s c(a) and c(1)=1. In particular it is S-linear and c∘e=id. The inclusion i is injective modulo p^n (both Witt base rings are perfect, so reduction modulo p^n is Witt truncation). Also eW(X) is a unit of W.

Suppose R and x have entries in OE and x≡R i(a) mod p^n W for an A-vector a. By the definition of OE, clear denominators in the finitely many entries of R and x simultaneously: choose h and a matrix B and vector b over S with
    eW(X)^h R≡eW(B),   eW(X)^h x≡eW(b) mod p^n W.
Multiplying the given congruence and using injectivity modulo p^n gives
    e(b)≡e(B)a mod p^n A.
Apply c coordinatewise:
    b≡B c(a) mod p^n S.
Map to W and undo the common denominator eW(X)^h, which is a unit. The result is
    x≡R eW(c(a)) mod p^n W.
This proves the exact descentCoefficients interface. The same c(a) works regardless of the chosen precision and denominator whenever the original congruence holds. The retraction need not be multiplicative; only S-linearity is used. The deep input is the existence of the retraction, supplied by the topological basis of A over S containing 1. It is not assumed that A is a finite free S-module.

## 2. Integral membership of the limit vectors

Suppose v∈W^d is approximable at every precision k by R_k eW(s_k), with s_k over S, and the étale basis relation
    T0 R_k≡Π_k T_k mod p^k W.
Since all entries of R_k lie in OE, closedness of OE gives v∈OE^d. Let Y_k be the integral bounded-pole matrix for T_k, i(Y_k)=i(τ)^N T_k. Then
    i(τ)^N T0 v ≡ Π_k i(Y_k)eW(s_k) mod p^k W.
The right side belongs to i(A)^d. The left side is fixed, so the Witt saturation/closedness result V3.47 §3 (α=1,r=0) gives a unique y∈A^d with i(y)=i(τ)^N T0 v. This is limvec_mem. No convergence of T_k themselves is needed.

## 3. Construct the links after saturation

The stabilized images of V3.48 give, for every n, a layer m≥n such that each column of R_m has a congruent limit vector v_j (mod p^n). Section 2 gives v_j∈OE^d and y_j∈A^d with i(y_j)=i(τ)^N T0 v_j.

The saturated basis Y from V3.48 generates all such y_j over S, so write y_j=Y e(c_j). Let T be the adjusted basis with i(Y)=i(τ)^N T; τ is a unit in W. Cancel i(τ)^N to obtain
    T0 v_j=T eW(c_j).
Assemble columns c_j into a rectangular matrix D_n over S. Using the étale basis relation and v_j≡(R_m)_j gives
    T eW(D_n)≡Π_m T_m mod p^n W.                       (1)
This is the first Link condition.

Choose the integral right inverse S_m of Π_m modulo p^m and the bounded-pole partner Y'_m satisfying T_m i(Y'_m)=i(τ)^N I. Set Q_n=Y'_m S_m over A. Multiply (1) and use i(Y)=i(τ)^N T:
    i(Y)eW(D_n)i(Q_n)
      ≡i(τ)^N Π_m T_m i(Y'_m) S_m
      =i(τ)^(2N) Π_m S_m
      ≡i(τ)^(2N) I mod p^n W.                         (2)
This gives both congruences of limit_basis_link_mod from one matrix identity.

## 4. Exact inverse with bounded poles and reversed product

Put Z=i(Y), which is invertible in W, and t=i(τ)^(2N), a unit. Equation (2) says that Z i(B_n)≡tI mod p^n where B_n=e(D_n)Q_n has entries in A. Therefore
    i(B_n)≡t Z^(-1) mod p^n.
Closedness of i(A) gives a matrix Y' over A with i(Y')=t Z^(-1). Injectivity gives
    YY'=Y'Y=τ^(2N)I.
This proves the exact inverse existence. Moreover (2) implies
    i(e(D_n)Q_n Y)≡tI mod p^n W.
Using the injectivity modulo p^n of A→W, conclude
    e(D_n)(Q_n Y)≡τ^(2N) I mod p^n A.                 (3)
The short argument simultaneously explains the source's AinfInvLim.exists_mul_eq_smul_one and AinfRinv.rinv_mod_pow. No claim that Y is invertible in A is required; only its image in W is invertible.

## 5. A single functional gives a uniform S-right inverse

The remaining issue in (3) is that τ^(2N) is not in e(S) in general. The source's topological basis TB=(b_j,c_j) provides S-linear coordinate maps c_j:A→S. For fixed M=2N it proves
    p^r ∈ (c_j(τ^M): all j) ⊂ S
for some r depending on the BK datum and M only. Thus choose finitely many λ_j∈S with Σ λ_j c_j(τ^M)=p^r and define the S-linear functional
    L_M(a)=Σ λ_j c_j(a).
Then L_M(τ^M)=p^r. Apply L_M entrywise to (3) and put G_n=L_M(Q_nY). By S-linearity,
    D_n G_n≡p^r I mod p^n S.                        (4)
The exponent r is independent of n, m, and all matrix dimensions. Equations (1) and (4) are exactly Link for the adjusted basis T. This is descend_rightInverse, written in a form that avoids any entrywise double sums. The matrix multiplication rule is simply L_M(e(D)H)=D L_M(H). Importantly c from Section 1 need not satisfy c(τ^M)=p^r; L_M is a different functional chosen using the coefficient ideal.

## 6. Why the coefficient ideal contains a power of p

The precise source pow_l_mem_span_coeff is not a formal consequence of τ≠0. It uses Frobenius stability, as follows.

Let I=(c_j(τ^M)) and P=I:p^∞={s∈S: p^a s∈I for some a≥0}. The ideal P is p-saturated. Since S/pS=F_q[[X]] is a PID, P mod p is principal; lift a generator g∈P. Then P=(g)+pP: reduce any element of P modulo p, subtract a multiple of g, and use saturation. Noetherianity and p in the Jacobson radical imply P=(g) by Nakayama (or iterative p-adic lifting). The generator is nonzero since τ^M≠0 and coordinates detect zero. Saturation implies p∤g: otherwise g/p∈P=(g), forcing p to be a unit.

The topological basis has finite expansions modulo increasing powers of its ideal J. If g divides every coordinate of τ^M, then φ_S(g) divides every coordinate of φ_A(τ^M). Indeed each finite Frobenius-transformed expansion has its coordinates in (φ_S(g)), and the quotient by that ideal is J-adically separated. This is the source c_frob_mem; it requires φ_S(J)⊂J and J⊂Jac(S). The period relation in IsPer is φ_A(τ)=e(E)τ. Thus
    φ_S(g) | E^M c_j(τ^M) for every j,
and therefore φ_S(g) | E^M I.

Since g∈P, p^a g∈I for some a. Frobenius satisfies φ_S(g)≡g^p mod p, so p∤φ_S(g) (because p is prime). Cancel p^a in the divisibility
    φ_S(g) | p^a E^M g
to obtain φ_S(g)|E^M g.

Now the deep source input is isUnit_of_frobenius_dvd_eisenstein_pow_mul: a g with p∤g and φ_S(g)|E^M g is a unit for the specified BK ring/Eisenstein datum. Consequently P=S and 1∈P, i.e. p^r∈I for some r. This supplies the functional L_M and completes the uniform-denominator descent. This source input cannot be dropped for a general ring or an arbitrary period.

## 7. Assembly and remaining tasks

At the level of the exact source interface, V3.48 and V3.49 now give stable image construction, limit vectors, integral saturated lattice, bounded inverse, and both Link congruences, once the initial étale basis and its Frobenius matrices are given and the arithmetic inputs listed above hold. To produce the Pre object in limit_pre_exists, one must also transfer the invariant étale basis by the OE unit Wm and descend the bounded Frobenius matrices to S; the source invokes invariantEtaleBasis_change_by_OE_unit and breuilKisin_basis_of_etale_invariant_bounded_poles.

Remaining high-value inputs: initial étale basis limit; topological basis existence; the Frobenius-divisibility unit lemma; bounded-pole characterization and descent of the Frobenius matrices. The preceding proof compresses the algebra after these inputs rather than pretending that they follow from arbitrary linear approximation.
