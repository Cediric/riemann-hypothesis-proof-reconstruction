## 1. Witt completeness directly in coordinates

Let O be a perfect characteristic-p domain, x nonzero, and O complete and separated for (x). Write W=W(O), T=[x]. The standard Witt multiplication identity ([x]a)_i=x^(p^i)a_i implies that T^nW consists exactly of vectors whose ith coordinate belongs to x^(np^i)O. A T-adic Cauchy sequence therefore converges coordinatewise. Witt addition and subtraction in coordinate i are polynomials in coordinates 0 through i, hence continuous for the x-adic topology. Each x^kO is closed (it is an open additive subgroup). For a Cauchy sequence a_m and its coordinatewise limit a, fix n and m sufficiently large that a_m-a_j belongs to T^nW for every sufficiently large j. Passing to the limit in each coordinate shows a_m-a belongs to T^nW. Separation follows from the same coordinate criterion. This proves the first component of WittTeichComplete.main without its tail-sum machinery.

For the second component put J=(p,T). The ideals (p^n,T^n) are cofinal with J^n, since J^(2n) is contained in (p^n,T^n), which is contained in J^n. Perfection identifies W/p^n with truncated Witt vectors of length n. In this quotient the image of T^n has the same coordinate divisibility criterion for i<n. Thus a J-Cauchy sequence is coordinatewise x-adic Cauchy: to control coordinate i use (p^n,T^n) with n>i and arbitrarily large. Its coordinatewise limit is a J-adic limit by the same polynomial-continuity argument, now applied to the finitely many coordinates i<n. Separation is immediate. No rank-one assumption is needed.

## 2. Distinguished quotient is a domain

Assume additionally O is a valuation domain and x is a nonunit. Let e>0, w a unit of W, and E=T^e+pw. Standard Witt facts: W is a domain, W/p=O, W is p-adically complete, and an element is a unit iff its residue modulo p is a unit.

The pair p,T is regular: p is regular and x is regular modulo p. Also T divides pa only if T divides a: if pa=Tb, reduction modulo p forces b=pc, then cancel p. Consequently T divides Ea only if T divides a, since E= pw modulo T and w is a unit. Induction and cancellation give (T^n):E=(T^n) for all n. If T^na=Eb, then b=T^nc, and cancellation gives a=Ec. In particular E cannot divide T^n, since that would make E a unit, whereas its residue x^e is a nonunit.

Set D=W/(E). It is T-adically separated: if a=Eb_n+T^nc_n for every n, the colon identity makes b_n a T-adic Cauchy sequence. Section 1 gives a limit b, and separation of W gives a=Eb. In fact the same colon identity allows compatible representatives of a Cauchy sequence in D to be adjusted by multiples of E to be Cauchy in W, so D is complete as well (not needed for primality).

In D, p=-T^e/w. Write any Witt vector a=[a_0]+pa_1. If x^e divides a_0, its class is divisible by T^e. Otherwise valuation comparability gives x^e=a_0 c with c a nonunit. Then in D,
    a=[a_0](1-[c]w^(-1)a_1).
The parenthesis is a unit already in W: its residue is 1 minus an element of the maximal ideal of O. Repeatedly divide by T^e in the first case. For a nonzero class this process must terminate, by separation. Hence every nonzero class is represented by [y] times a unit of W, where y divides some power of x. Such a class divides a power of T in D. No power of T vanishes, as shown above. The product of any two nonzero classes thus divides a nonzero power of T and cannot vanish. D is a domain; p is nonzero because it is associated to T^e.

This proves both conclusions of teichmuller_distinguished_span_isPrime and the regular-sequence input with one colon identity and a quotient normal form.

## 3. Untilting and Galois comparison

For the BK Eisenstein polynomial E_BK of degree e, write E_BK=X^e+p(w0+XG), with w0 a unit. For a map psi from the source tilt valuation ring to O satisfying the upstream hypotheses, x=psi(flat) is nonzero and nonunit. Its Witt image of E_BK has the form E above: the residue of w0+XG is a unit plus an element of the maximal ideal. Fontaine theta is surjective with kernel generated by ev(E_BK), so the Witt map followed by quotient to D factors uniquely through theta, giving theta_psi:OC→D. It is injective: each nonzero y in OC divides some p^n by the rank-one norm, and p remains nonzero in D. Moreover D/pD=O/(x^e), so if the class of a Witt vector is divisible by p, its zeroth coordinate is divisible by x^e. This is exactly the divisibility conclusion of exists_untilt_map.

For psi and psi' agreeing on the zeroth coefficients of ev(S), they agree on the finite residue field and flat (test constant Teichmuller lifts and X). The induced Witt maps on S agree on constants coordinatewise and on X. Truncating any power series at degree n gives equality modulo T^n; Section 1 gives equality. Hence both untilt maps have the same quotient D and agree on the integers of K, by the BK quotient identification S/(E_BK).

Extend the two injective maps OC→D to fraction fields, then restrict to the algebraic closure Om of Q_p inside C_p. They agree on K because K is the fraction field of its integers. Their images of Om in Frac(D) coincide: each is an algebraically closed algebraic extension of the common image of K, hence consists of exactly the elements of Frac(D) algebraic over that image. Composing one inverse with the other yields g in Gal(Om/K), with theta_2(y)=theta_1(g y) for integral algebraic y. The standard continuous Galois action on C_p extends that on Om. For any y in OC, density supplies an integral algebraic z with y-z in pOC (choose distance strictly less than |p|). Therefore theta_2(y)-theta_1(g y) belongs to pD. This uses no continuity of theta_i.

## 4. Recovering the tilt action

The untilts r_n of flat^(1/p^n) satisfy r_n^(p^n)=r_0, with r_0 the image of X in the integers of K under the BK quotient. Thus r_n belongs to Om, since Om is algebraically closed. The two tilt maps agree on flat and hence on its unique inverse Frobenius roots in O. Their untilt maps agree on r_n by the Teichmuller formula. Injectivity of theta_1 and algebraic Galois comparison imply g fixes every r_n. The tilt inverse-limit coordinates are reductions modulo p of these untilts, and Galois action commutes with untilting and inverse Frobenius. Hence g fixes flat.

For arbitrary tilt y, apply the modulo-p comparison to theta([y]). Equivariance of theta and Teichmuller lifts and D/pD=O/(x^e) give
    psi'(y)-psi(g y) in x^e O.
Apply this to the inverse Frobenius roots of y and raise to p^k. The difference lies in x^(e p^k)O for every k; separation implies equality. Thus the full conclusion of exists_galK_fixFlat_of_tilt_maps_agree follows.
