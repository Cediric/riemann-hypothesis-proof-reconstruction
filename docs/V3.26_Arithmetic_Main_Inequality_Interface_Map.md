## 1. A naming collision that must be removed

There are two different declarations named SizeData₂. They live in different namespaces and have different mathematical roles.

### A. Per-record construction data

Formalism.BasicNotions.SizeData₂ D is a structure in src/Formalism/LocalRingCardinality/DataParams₂.lean. It stores the actual data attached to one record D : D7, including:

- the scale and the integer parameters attached to the top set;
- the support and the local functions gain, F, E, tau, and io;
- local support, conjugation, inert-place, and prime-exclusion conditions;
- the depth and weight-length parameters;
- the thick/top/thin weight windows and cloud-gap bounds;
- the thick-set density and the normalizing constants.

This is the object that carries the concrete arithmetic and geometric construction.

### B. The one-scale analytic bundle

Formalism.LFunctionChainParameters.LChain.SizeData₂ Dd C Xpp is a proposition in src/Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/SChain.lean, defined as an eleven-part conjunction:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{HidaSetting}\land\text{DIV}\land\text{EComp5}\land\text{HComp}\\
&\land\text{Count5}\land\text{Slopes}\land\text{QuotientSlopeBoundsWith}\\
&\land\text{LossBounds}\land\text{SlopeIneq}\land\text{HHBound}\land\text{FParams}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is not the same structure. It packages the estimates that the growth argument needs after the concrete record data have been translated into the trace/height data Dd.

**Convention in this reconstruction:** whenever the distinction matters, call the first one *record SizeData* and the second one *LChain SizeData bundle*.

## 2. The exact one-scale package: ChainCore

In SChain.lean, ChainCore is the central one-scale existence statement. It supplies:

1. a finite group-like index set H, its arithmetic data D, and a trace-data object Dd;
2. the identifications tying Dd to the arithmetic character sums and weights;
3. the LChain SizeData bundle;
4. the error-window package ErrWindows;
5. the two budget bounds BNBounds;
6. GrowthInequalityRemainingHyps;
7. CostWindows;
8. the remaining scale, density, and rank bounds RestSize;
9. the host facts and the uniformity of the scale and constants.

The four interfaces that had been tracked separately are therefore not four unrelated theorems. They are four named pieces inside a larger package, alongside Ident, HostFacts, BNBounds, and RestSize.

## 3. What the four interfaces actually say

### 3.1 LChain SizeData bundle

This is the eleven-part analytic/geometric package above. It contains the height, divisibility, counting, slope, quotient-slope, loss, and parameter controls. It is a structured conjunction, not a single numerical estimate.

### 3.2 ErrWindows

Formalism.LFunctionChainParameters.LErr.ErrWindows is defined in ErrorFormsWeakWindowsFalse.lean as:

\[
\text{ErrWindows0}\ \land\
(\eta_{\rm bar}+4\sqrt{e_{\rm bar}})\,x^{c_{HH}}b
\leq Kx^\varepsilon(n_Ta).
\]

ErrWindows0 itself records the basic ranges and positivity assumptions for x, epsilon, a, b, b', k, n_T, the bounds on a_t, B_0, and N_c, the word-count bound, the defect bound, and the allowed ranges of the constants.

Thus ErrWindows is the quantitative error budget. It is not the main inequality; it makes the error term produced by the trace-formula side absorbable.

### 3.3 GrowthInequalityRemainingHyps

In SChain.lean this is exactly

\[
\text{GrowthInequalitySizes}
\ \land\ \text{ThinScales}
\ \land\ \text{ValuesAtX}.
\]

The size component includes the weight-window and node-size conditions, the approximation of a_t/b by x, and the cutoff a_t/w ≤ N. The other two components control the thin scales and the relevant values of the arithmetic function at the chosen scale.

### 3.4 CostWindows

This is only the pair of inequalities

\[
2f(x+1)\theta\leq\frac1x,
\qquad
6f(x+1)(\eta+1)\leq\frac bx.
\]

These bounds control the two small-cost contributions in the growth estimate. Their simplicity should not be confused with the difficulty of constructing parameters that satisfy them simultaneously with the other windows.

### 3.5 The additional package that must not be omitted

ChainCore also requires:

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_0&\leq 2|H|\,m\,b\,x^\varepsilon,\\
N_c&\leq
\frac{|T_2|\,k\,b\,x^\varepsilon}
{|H|^2x^{-\varepsilon}},
\end{aligned}
\]

and RestSize, which contains the scale, density, lift-count, and rank inequalities. Any compressed account that lists only the four interfaces is incomplete.

## 4. The central trace-to-main-term inequality

The most informative entry point is LinkDIV_at in src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalTheoremInputs.lean. It is a nine-part package. Its seventh clause is the central one-sided inequality:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{\rm dual}(u)+h(u)-F_o
\leq\;&\operatorname{MAIN}(u)
+C\,|TT|\,a\,L_a\\
&+C\,m(B_0+N_c)x^{C_{\rm abs}\varepsilon}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

Here MAIN(u) is the explicit main term

\[
\sum_{t\in TT}
\left[
m\left(\phi_F(a_t)-a_t\log\Omega\right)
-a_t\log T(u)
\right],
\]

where the source defines log Omega from the Euler constant, the archimedean parameter, and the logarithmic derivative of the relevant Dirichlet L-function.

The other eight clauses are not decorative. They provide the structural bridge needed to use (1):

1. the map from the relevant lattice into the complex comparison space;
2. a global frame/height bound;
3. the vanishing condition ZeroAbove;
4. the Bessel identity;
5. the full-rank condition;
6. a height-map bound;
7. the main inequality (1);
8. control of the local E-sum against the logarithm of the two level primes;
9. a uniform comparison between different words/translates.

So the useful compressed statement is not “the Lean proof assumes (M).” It is: **the trace-to-main-term inequality is obtained together with eight compatibility and uniformity controls, and the growth package turns those controls into the prime-power error bound.**

This clause is a central input to the paper's arithmetic inequality (M), not by itself the complete statement (M).

## 5. How the Lean source assembles the package

The route visible in the pinned source is:

\[
\text{family-level inputs}
\longrightarrow \text{ChainTied}
\longrightarrow \text{ChainCore}
\longrightarrow \text{ChainInputs}
\longrightarrow \text{growth bound}
\longrightarrow \mathrm{RH}.
\]

### A. linkCore: the one-scale constructor

LossesSmallShares.lean defines linkCore. Its premises are the following family-level inputs:

- CountF;
- SlopesMF and SlopesNF;
- LatF and NodesF;
- FrameF and LossFS;
- CharF and WinF;
- ChosenTT, ChosenQD, and ChosenThin;
- LinkIRR and LinkDIV.

From them, it explicitly constructs the tied data, the eleven fields of the LChain SizeData bundle, ErrWindows, BNBounds, GrowthInequalityRemainingHyps, CostWindows, and RestSize. The proof is a constructor, not a bare invocation of a single opaque “main inequality” axiom.

ChainTied.core_of in TieGlobal.lean then forgets the extra pointwise tying data and produces ChainCore.

### B. The global link layer

GlobalTheoremInputs.lean defines

\[
\text{GlobalTheoremInputs}
=\text{LinkG}\land\text{LinkS}\land\text{LinkIRR}
\land\text{LinkDIV}\land\text{LinkMu},
\]

and LinkGLOBAL turns ChosenE plus these global inputs into eventual ChainTied data on the family window.

MuTuOfTuSize.lean collects the wider list in AllLinks: LinkV, LinkShaFin, LinkN, LinkEta, LinkIRR, SmallLevelRankEq, LinkDIVt, the rank/tu-size/degree inputs, LinkG, LinkS, and LinkGLOBAL.

LinkMin.lean shows how several of the family-level inputs are derived from the more primitive lattice, rank, and global-link statements. In particular, linkGLOBAL7_items derives the count, slope, lattice, frame, node, loss, character, and window inputs before calling linkCore.

### C. From one-scale data to RH

In SChain.lean, ChainInputs is ChainCore plus the large-scale inequalities XLarge. ChainTop turns this into ReducedGrowthInequalityDataExists. The final theorem ChainGivesRiemannHypothesis says that if these inputs are supplied eventually for every sufficiently small positive epsilon, then RH follows.

ChainSlopeWindows/PChain.lean contains the proved bridge chain_top_bound and the final chain_weighted_mean_to_unit_weight theorem. This is the end of the arithmetic-growth route, not the construction of the data itself.

## 6. Relation to the thirteen hypotheses in the human exposition

The 22 November working exposition identifies five hypotheses as assumed from the Lean development: (H2), (H3), (H5), (H9), and (H12); (H4) is mixed. It describes them as follows:

- (H2): local-exponent level correction;
- (H3): comparison of the heights of two words;
- (H5): covolume of the first lattice of forms;
- (H9): range of slopes;
- (H12): supply of compatible data on every required scale window, with constants uniform in the scale.

The source map above narrows the search, but the field-by-field correspondence is not yet fully established. In particular, do not equate a paper hypothesis with an entire Lean structure merely because both mention a lattice, height, or slope.

The likely construction route for the scale-supply part passes through the family objects and their choice packages:

- FamP and its interval/window data;
- DesignChoices in DesignExistsOfChoices.lean;
- PageFix and PageWin, together with the global twisting and tame-distinct conditions in AuxiliaryPrimeMemAvoidedPrimes.lean;
- the chosen thick/top/prime-window data;
- LinkGLOBAL and linkCore, which turn the family data into ChainTied and ChainCore.

This is a **candidate Lean-side supply route**, not yet a proved equivalence with (H12). The exact coverage of every interval [Z,Z^(3/2)], and the uniformity of every constant required by (H12), still need a declaration-by-declaration audit.
