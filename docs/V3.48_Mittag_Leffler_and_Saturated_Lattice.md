# V3.48 — Mittag–Leffler stable images and saturated lattice freeness

Pinned upstream commit: 7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4. Continuation of V3.47. This is an alternative natural-language compression of the stable image and saturated module portions of limit_pre_exists, retaining the exact arithmetic inputs. No new Lean verification or novelty claim.

## 1. One denominator-clearing matrix identity

Write p=ℓ, S=bk.S, W=WCb, A=Ainf, i:A→W, e=bk.ev and eW=i∘e. Work throughout this section modulo p^n W. For m≥n the source has
    T0 R_j = Π_j T_j (j=n,m),    Π_m S_m=I,
where S_m is a right inverse modulo p^m of Π_m (not the coefficient ring S). T0 is invertible. The bounded-pole theorem supplies integral matrices Y_j,Y'_j with
    i(Y_j)=i(τ)^N T_j,    Y_j Y'_j=τ^(2N) I.
As i(τ) is a unit in W, T_j i(Y'_j)=i(τ)^N I. Uniform denominator clearing supplies c=c(n), y∈A such that e(X)^c≡τ^(2N)y mod p^n A.

For any s∈S^(d_n), form the integral vector
    a=y Y'_m S_m Π_n Y_n e(s)∈A^(d_m).
Here the integral maps S_m and Π_n are embedded in A. Then, in W/p^n,
    T0 R_m i(a)
      = i(y) Π_m T_m i(Y'_m) S_m Π_n i(Y_n)eW(s)
      = i(y)i(τ)^(2N) Π_n T_n eW(s)
      = T0 R_n eW(X^c s).
Cancel T0. The target vector R_n eW(X^c s) and all entries of R_m lie in OE. The coefficient descent theorem breuilKisin_descent_coefficients_ainf_mod_pow replaces a by e(s') for s'∈S^(d_m), preserving the congruence. Hence
    R_n eW(X^c s)≡R_m eW(s') mod p^n.
Crucially c is independent of m and of the vector s. This is the source's mittagLeffler_approx. The proof depends on bounded poles, uniform denominator clearing and coefficient descent; it does not derive those deep inputs here.

## 2. Stable images by a finite quotient

Here is a general formulation that avoids tracking representatives in the original ambient ring. Let S→W be any ring homomorphism, p∈Z, u∈S, W p-adically complete and separated. Let R_j:S^(d_j)→W^d denote the maps induced by the given coefficient homomorphism and matrices, with S-linear codomain action. For m≥n put
    I_(n,m)=image(R_m) in (W/p^n W)^d.
Suppose R_m Y_m≡R_(m+1) mod p^m for transition matrices Y_m over S. Then the I_(n,m) decrease as m increases.

Suppose for each n some c has u^c I_(n,n)⊂I_(n,m) for all m≥n, and S/(p^n,u^c) is finite. Since I_(n,n) is an image of S^(d_n) and is killed by p^n, the quotient
    I_(n,n)/u^c I_(n,n)
is a quotient of (S/(p^n,u^c))^(d_n), so is a finite set. Every I_(n,m) contains the common submodule u^c I_(n,n). Therefore the descending chain stabilizes, by passing to this single finite quotient. This is the same content as img_stable, expressed directly through quotient submodules rather than a chain of sets of coefficient representatives.

In the actual source S=W(F_q)[[X]], u=X and S/(p^n,X^c) is finite: it has c coefficients in the finite ring W(F_q)/p^n. For c,n≥1 its cardinality is q^(nc); for c=0 or n=0 the quotient is the zero ring. Thus no finiteness assertion for W/p^n or for I_(n,n) itself is needed.

Let J_n be the stabilized image, equal to I_(n,m) for all sufficiently large m. The reduction J_(n+1)→J_n is surjective: choose one m large enough for both stabilization indices, and lift a class in J_n using exactly the same layer vector at precision n+1. By recursively choosing lifts, any class in J_n extends to a compatible system in all J_k (lower k by reduction). Completeness gives v∈W^d representing the system. For each k, v mod p^k belongs to J_k⊂I_(k,k), and v mod p^n is the original class. This is the exact conclusion of mittagLeffler_image. No compactness of the full Witt ring W is used.

## 3. Extract a basis from stable images

Use n=1 and choose a stabilization layer m≥1. The étale-basis construction gives R_m with entries in OE and R'_m over OE satisfying R_m R'_m≡I mod p^m W.

Every entry of R'_m can, after multiplication by a suitable power of eW(X), be represented modulo p by eW(s), s∈S; this follows from the definition of OE. Take a common exponent h for the finitely many entries. This gives a matrix C over S with
    eW(C)≡eW(X)^h R'_m mod p.
For each column of R_m eW(C) use Section 2 to lift its residue to a vector approximable by all layers. Assemble those columns into V. Then
    V≡eW(X)^h I mod p.
Closedness of OE gives V∈M_d(OE); eW(X) is a unit in OE, so det V is not divisible by p in W. The OE factorization theorem writes every nonzero element as p^k times a unit in OE. Here k=0, so V is invertible over OE, with inverse given by adjugate/determinant. This is the entire argument of invertible_basis, retaining its exact dependence on the OE properties. It is not necessary to assume the layer maps themselves are surjective over S.

## 4. General saturated-module freeness lemma

Let S and A be commutative domains, e:S→A a ring homomorphism; A is p-adically separated and S is p-adically complete. Let red:S→D be surjective onto a PID D, b:D→F an embedding into a field and cc:A→F a homomorphism with cc∘e=b∘red and ker(cc)⊂pA. Let L⊂A^d be an S-submodule saturated for p (py∈L implies y∈L). Assume:
- L contains the columns of some d×d matrix Y1 over A with nonzero determinant;
- there is P∈GL_d(F) such that P cc(L)⊂b(D)^d, coordinatewise.

Then L is generated over S by d columns Y∈M_d(A) whose matrix cc(Y) is invertible over F.

Proof. Identify b(D) with D. The image Lbar=P cc(L) is a submodule of D^d because red is surjective; over a PID it has a basis b_1,...,b_t with t≤d. Lift the basis to y_j∈L and form the d×t matrix Y. For every y∈L choose c∈S^t lifting the D-coordinates of P cc(y) in that basis. Since P is invertible,
    y−Y e(c) ∈ pA^d.
By saturation y−Y e(c)=p y_1 with y_1∈L. Repeat, obtaining c_j∈S^t and
    y=Y e(Σ_(j<k) p^j c_j)+p^k y_k.
The coefficient series converges in S^t. Separatedness of A gives y=Y e(c_infty) exactly. This uses neither Noetherianity nor any compactness assumption on A; it does not require L itself to be closed.

Since all columns of Y1 lie in L, Y1=Y e(C1) for a t×d matrix C1 over S. Nonzero determinant of Y1 implies t≥d after passing to the fraction field of A. Therefore t=d. The columns P cc(Y) are the independent D-basis of Lbar; they are independent over the fraction field of D and hence over F (nonzero determinant stays nonzero under the embedding). Thus cc(Y) is invertible.

This is a concise proof of SatMod.abstract and approx. If e is injective, the columns are also independent over S by nonzero determinant of Y; in the concrete BK package eW is injective. It is essential to have a full-rank Y1, saturation, and the PID-bound on the residue image. An arbitrary submodule of Ainf^d need not be free over S.

## 5. Concrete saturation and the residue-field bound

Let M=i(τ)^N T0∈GL_d(W) and consider
    L={y∈Ainf^d : M^(-1)i(y)∈OE^d}.
This is an S-submodule because eW(S)⊂OE; it is p-saturated because OE is p-saturated inside W. The basis V from Section 3 and limitVector_mem_OE_and_ainf supply Y1 with i(Y1)=MV, so its determinant is nonzero and its columns lie in L.

Take D=F_q[[X]], F=C^flat, cc the Witt constant coefficient followed by inclusion O_C^flat→C^flat. The map b:D→F sends X to the nonzero topologically nilpotent flat element x0. Its injectivity and red:S→D surjectivity are supplied in AinfClosedMembershipLimit. The kernel of cc is pAinf. The residue of OE in C^flat is b(F_q((X))) by oe_mod_l.

Let Q be the reduction of M^(-1) modulo p. It is invertible over F. Multiply Q by a sufficiently large common power x0^h so that each entry of P=x0^h Q is in O_C^flat; possible by the rank-one valuation of the tilt and x0 topologically nilpotent. For y∈L we have Q cc(y) in b(F_q((X)))^d and cc(y) integral in the tilt. Thus
    P cc(y) ∈ O_C^flat^d ∩ b(F_q((X)))^d = b(F_q[[X]])^d.
The intersection equality follows directly: if a Laurent series has negative X-order, write it as X^(−j) times a power series with nonzero constant coefficient. That power series maps to a unit in O_C^flat because its residue modulo x0 is a nonzero element of the finite coefficient field (equivalently use its inverse in F_q[[X]]). Its quotient by x0^j cannot be integral. This is the source's mem_range_eo argument.

The general lemma applies and gives Y with invertible reduction. Its image i(Y) is invertible in W because W is a Witt DVR over the perfect field C^flat. Its columns generate L over S.

Put Wm=M^(-1)i(Y), which has entries in OE. Since Y1 is in L, write Y1=Y e(C) for C over S, so V=Wm eW(C). Hence
    Wm [eW(C) V^(-1)]=I.
The bracket has entries in OE. Thus Wm is invertible over OE, i(Y)=M Wm, and all vectors of L have coordinates over S in Y. This is SatLat.main, with the full saturated lattice identified explicitly. The columns of the original V need not generate L over S; this saturation step is necessary.

## 6. What remains

V3.48 compresses the central stable-image and freeness mechanisms in the linked-preframe construction, assuming the precise earlier inputs. It does not yet prove the étale basis construction, the bounded-pole coefficient descent lemma, or limitVector_mem_OE_and_ainf. Nor does it yet prove the twisted congruence lift, reconstruction of the Frobenius matrices over S, or the uniform approximate right inverse lifting that completes Link. Those are the remaining targets in this branch. The original source uses all of them.
