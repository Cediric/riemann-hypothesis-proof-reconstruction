## 2. Exact definition of the intermediate target

UpperTheory.EveryCharacter is:

\[
\forall N,\ \forall \chi \pmod N,\ \forall s\in\mathbb C,\quad
\Re(s)>\tfrac12,\quad(\chi\ne 1\ \lor\ s\ne1)
\Longrightarrow L(s,\chi)\ne0.
\]

The final proof constructs this proposition in UpperTheory.FinalTheorems.everyCharacter.

Its input is a global Galois-representation package:

\[
h:\texttt{UpperTheory.GalStar.Row0b recT},
\qquad
\texttt{recT}(h,rc):=\texttt{IsRecT}(h,rc).
\]

The main theorem is assembled as:

\[
\texttt{everyCharacter\_of\_row}(h)
=
\texttt{every\_character\_of\_literature\_of\_vWin}
\bigl(\langle h,\texttt{recTExists},h\rangle\bigr)
\bigl(\texttt{vwin\_of\_row}(h)\bigr).
\]

The OpenLiteratureStatements structure in the pinned source is a proposition with exactly three fields:

1. caraianiLocalGlobalDual : UpperTheory.One.M.Row;

The final constructor supplies h, UpperTheory.Zero.LLC.recTExists, and h. This is a compact input package, not a list of dozens of independent axioms. The numerous *_of_literature declarations construct theorem packages from these fields and other already-proved internal results. Their complete dependency closure still needs the planned #print axioms run.

### 3.2 The VWin arithmetic window bound

vwin_of_row h invokes automorphic_window_of_inputs with:

- the OpenImageCondition constructed from h;
- the definite base-change theorem baseChangeN;
- the same Galois row h.

The window theorem then builds a LiteratureRankNHypothesis from local inputs, automorphic inputs, Galois cohomology inputs, and CM abelian inputs, and applies the patching/descent route to obtain VWin.

The target VWin is a strong uniform arithmetic estimate. For every primitive character parameter ψ, it asserts VWinAt ψ 401. For each sufficiently small ε, each admissible scale function e(x), and each prime pair (q,d) satisfying the WinPair conditions, there is a window [Y,Y²] inside a prescribed range such that every number field E satisfying IsChiField obeys the VVle bound:

- the relevant Sha lengths are finite for all exponents below floor(x^401);
- the weighted sum of these lengths over primes is at most sqrt(exp(x^ε)).

This is the quantitative input used to construct good hosts for the zero-free argument. It is not merely a qualitative finiteness statement.

## 4. The zero-free bridge: Green + VWin + definite-side hypotheses

The final bridge is not a one-line appeal to FontaineBcris. It combines three substantial ingredients.

### 4.1 Green regular-character input

GreenRegularCharacters states that for every finite field F_g, every positive degree d, and every degree-d extension K/F_g, there is a parametrization of cuspidal characters of GL_d(F_g) by regular multiplicative characters of K×. The parametrization has:

- regular characters map to cuspidal characters;
- a trace formula on regular semisimple elements;
- Frobenius-orbit equivalence for the parametrization;
- every cuspidal character is obtained.

greenRegularCharacters_holds proves this using the existence of cuspidal characters attached to regular characters and a counting argument over monic irreducible polynomials. GreenBridge.green_of_regular then derives GlobalTheory.GLq.Green.

### 4.3 From the three inputs to every-character nonvanishing

The call chain is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\texttt{GreenRegularCharacters}
  \Longrightarrow \texttt{GlobalTheory.GLq.Green},\\
&\texttt{VWin}
  \Longrightarrow \texttt{HostExW}(3/2),\\
&\texttt{Green}+\texttt{VWin}+\texttt{Nonempty definite-side hypotheses}\\
&\hspace{2cm}\Longrightarrow \texttt{ZeroFreeAll}
  \Longrightarrow \texttt{EveryCharacter}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The exact mechanisms are:

1. shape_closed_link_n_of_green converts Green into the required topological/structural family-shape statement.
2. supplyChi converts VWinAt ψ 401 into HostExW (3/2) (GChi ψ).req.
3. zero_free_all_of_green_v_win combines the shape statement, the definite-side hypotheses, and the host bounds.
4. zeroFreeAll_of_topG uses the TopG family-connectivity property to construct a Chebyshev inequality on windows.
5. Comparison₂.level_gm_le_on_family_scales supplies the window Chebyshev comparison, and Comparison₂.formB supplies the Landau growth-to-zero-free implication.
6. zeroFreeOn_of_tiedWin_cyc converts these ingredients into zero-freeness for the cyclic level of each primitive character.
7. Top.imprimitive transfers the result to arbitrary Dirichlet characters. The proof uses the conductor-change identity and shows the extra finite Euler factors are nonzero when Re(s)>1/2.

The abstract analytic core is worth isolating. Level.FormBAt la says that if the set

\[
\{x:\Lambda.Gm(x)\le x^\theta\}
\]

contains multiplicative windows of scale la, then Λ has no zeros above θ. level_of_tiedWin obtains a Chebyshev growth exponent 1/2+δ, chooses δ below the distance from the candidate zero to the critical line, and applies FormBAt. This is the final analytic mechanism that turns the arithmetic window bounds into zero-freeness.

## 5. From EveryCharacter to RH and the strip statements

Once EveryCharacter is established:

- Formalism.VW.rh_of_every applies it to the principal character modulo 1, identifies that L-function with the Riemann zeta function, and invokes the right-half-plane RH criterion.
- Formalism.VW.critLine proves the strip statement for primitive Dirichlet characters. If a zero were left of 1/2, the functional equation reflects it to a zero right of 1/2; gamma-factor nonvanishing allows the completed L-function equation to be used without introducing a spurious zero.
- Claim.strip first transfers an imprimitive zero to the primitive character, then applies the functional-equation reflection argument.
- Claim.zeta handles the zeta function separately, excluding trivial zeros and the pole at 1.

This completes the source-level route from TLiftK to the headline RH statement.

## 6. What this audit does and does not establish
