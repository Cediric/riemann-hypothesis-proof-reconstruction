## 1. Rallis_k: analytic continuation of a parameterized integral

TPGRallis.lean proves Rallis_k from Tools_k. Its central idea is to compare two holomorphic functions of a complex parameter z.

Let

\[
s_0=\frac{d-n}{2}.
\]

The coefficient/matrix-coefficient integral is

\[
F(z)=
\int_{UA}
a(g)^{z-s_0}
\operatorname{coef}(\omega,\nu,\Phi)(g)
\overline{\operatorname{mc}(M,\phi)(g)}
\,d\mu(g).
\]

### Step A — Establish growth and integrability

The double identity, box-section estimate, and full Siegel–Weil identity give a polynomial height bound

\[
\|\operatorname{coef}(\omega,\nu,\Phi)(g)\|
\leq C\,a(g)^{d/2}.
\]

The automorphic matrix coefficient is bounded. The height integrability hypothesis then gives integrability of the parameterized integrand in a right half-plane.

The auxiliary lemma cpow_integral_pk proves that F(z) is differentiable on the required half-plane. Its proof is dominated differentiation, with the height powers at the two edges of a small complex disk providing an integrable majorant.

### Step B — Compare with the holomorphic k-pair

The theorem kpair_cont_k supplies a holomorphic continuation of the k-pair. On the initial right half-plane, the unfolding and height identities show

\[
kpair(z)=c\,c'\,F(z)
\]

for the appropriate nonzero constants c and c'.

The source proves this equality by unfolding the theta kernel and using the double identity.

### Step C — Continue to the special point

The identity theorem for holomorphic functions, packaged as zero_of_half_pk, extends the equality from the right half-plane to z=s0.

At z=s0, the special-value formula An.at_value expresses the k-pair as an integral of the theta kernel.

If the theta lift were zero, then the theta integral would vanish. Hence the special k-pair value is zero, and the continued identity forces

\[
F(s_0)=
\int_{UA}
\operatorname{coef}(\omega,\nu,\Phi)(g)
\overline{\operatorname{mc}(M,\phi)(g)}
\,d\mu(g)=0.
\]

This is exactly the implication required by Rallis_k.

So Rallis_k is an analytic-continuation argument:

\[
\text{height growth + integrability}
\Longrightarrow \text{holomorphic integral}
\Longrightarrow \text{unfolding identity}
\Longrightarrow \text{special-value theta formula}.
\]

## 2. SchurStep_k: compact-group orthogonality

TPGSchurStep.lean proves SchurStep_k from Tools_k.

The source extracts a finite-dimensional irreducible complex representation V with representation map rho and nonzero vector x0. It realizes V as a space of automorphic functions through an injective linear map

\[
f:V\longrightarrow (UA\to\mathbb C).
\]

The box operator defines an invariant sesquilinear form

\[
B_1(a,b)=
l_D\bigl(boxFun(\Psi(a),\overline{\Psi(b)})\bigr).
\]

The theta action and the box-unitarity identity show that B1 is invariant under the group action.

### The general Schur averaging lemma

The auxiliary theorem assemble_pk uses:

- a compact quotient/fundamental domain;
- a finite-dimensional irreducible representation;
- an invariant sesquilinear form;
- an equivariant realization of V by functions on the group.

Schur's lemma identifies the averaged rank-one operator with a scalar multiple of the identity. Taking traces determines the scalar:

\[
\kappa=
\frac{\mu_A(A)}{\dim_{\mathbb C}V}.
\]

Because A has positive finite Haar measure and V is nontrivial and finite-dimensional,

\[
\kappa\neq0.
\]

The resulting identity is

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\int_{gInf}
\operatorname{coef}(\omega,\nu,\Phi)(x)
\overline{\operatorname{mc}(M,\phi)(xc)}\,d\mu_A(x)\\
&\qquad =
\kappa\,
\operatorname{coef}(\omega,\nu,\Phi)(1)
\overline{\operatorname{mc}(M,\phi)(c)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is precisely SchurStep_k.

The proof is therefore a standard Schur-orthogonality computation, but the Lean development explicitly constructs the finite-dimensional representation, its invariant form, and the compact quotient.

## 3. Decomp_k: split the adelic group by orthogonal idempotents

TPGDecomp.lean proves Decomp_k by constructing three orthogonal idempotents in the adele algebra:

\[
e_\infty=realAd(F,1),\qquad
e_S=\sum_{v\in S}Euler.idem(v),\qquad
e_{off}=1-e_\infty-e_S.
\]

They satisfy

\[
e_i^2=e_i,\qquad e_i e_j=0\ (i\neq j),\qquad
e_\infty+e_S+e_{off}=1.
\]

Each idempotent defines a projection of the unitary group by applying it to the matrix \(mat(g)\). The corresponding subgroups are:

- gInf: the infinite-place component;
- gAt(S): the selected finite-place component;
- gOff(S): the remaining finite-place component.

The general lemma prod_equiv_pk proves that three pairwise orthogonal idempotents summing to one give a topological group isomorphism

\[
gInf\times gAt(S)\times gOff(S)
\simeq UA.
\]

The proof also establishes:

- closedness of each factor;
- compactness of gInf;
- compact openness of the integral subgroup;
- the relevant local-integrality properties;
- compatibility with the Haar product decomposition.

The key algebraic point is that the adelic matrix is reconstructed by adding its three idempotent projections. The topological and measure-theoretic properties then follow from the corresponding projection maps and compactness inputs.

## 4. How the three results close NonVan_k

The nonvanishing proof from V3.39 now has all of its major components explained:

| Component | Mathematical mechanism |
|---|---|
| Decomp_k | Orthogonal-idempotent decomposition of the adelic group |
| SchurStep_k | Finite-dimensional Schur orthogonality on the compact infinite-place factor |
| Rallis_k | Holomorphic continuation and special-value theta integral |
| BumpAny_k | Local Schrödinger/Weil congruence bump |
| Datum_k | Global finite test vector and coefficient factorization |
| Tail_k | Finite-place exhaustion and continuity from above |
| AbsNe | Strict main-term-versus-tail measure inequality |

The contradiction is now transparent:

1. Assume the theta lift vanishes.
2. Rallis_k forces the global coefficient integral to vanish.
3. Datum_k gives a nonzero coefficient at the identity.
4. SchurStep_k turns the infinite-place integral into a nonzero scalar multiple of that coefficient.
5. Tail_k makes the off-place error strictly smaller than the integral compact contribution.
6. AbsNe says the full integral cannot be zero.

Therefore the theta lift is nonzero.
