## 1. Where split rigidity enters

The VWin descent input is the theorem UpperTheory.V.Z.Tf.SR.descWeak in TraceFormula/SplitRigidity/HasOfSplit.lean. Its proof eliminates the finite obstruction branch of RunTh by applying has_of_split.

The chain is:

\[
\texttt{RunTh}
+\texttt{PartNorm}
+\texttt{UnitOf}
+\texttt{SplitRigid}
\Longrightarrow
\texttt{WeakAll}
\Longrightarrow
\texttt{DescWeak}.
\]

The actual DescWeak passed to published_hypotheses_from_patching_alt is this split-rigidity theorem, not the similarly named GaloisFromTraceFormula.descWeak that takes an additional GalWeak 4 input. See [HasOfSplit.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/TraceFormula/SplitRigidity/HasOfSplit.lean).

## 2. Exact SplitRigid statement

For a CM number field E with the required unramified base-change property, let:

- π be a unitary automorphic representation of rank n;
- P_j be a finite family of unitary representations, with positive ranks a_j;
- at almost every split finite place w, suppose there are Satake multisets b_j(w) such that each P_j has Satake multiset b_j(w), and π has Satake multiset equal to the multiset sum of the b_j(w).

Then SplitRigid concludes that one member of the family is exactly π, including its rank:

\[
\exists j,\quad \langle a_j,P_j\rangle=\langle n,\pi\rangle.
\]

This is a strong uniqueness statement: a finite isobaric sum cannot imitate a unitary cuspidal representation at almost all split places unless one constituent is already that representation.

Source: [SplitRigidAlt.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/TraceFormula/SplitRigidity/SplitRigidAlt.lean).

## 3. The analytic engine: ExtId, LogTwo, CrossUp, and PartCS

### 3.1 ExtId: analytic continuation of the Rankin–Selberg Euler product

For each pair x,y in the finite representation family, ExtId supplies an analytic function F on the half-plane Re(s)>0 satisfying, for real σ>1,

\[
F(\sigma)=
\begin{cases}
(\sigma-1)\exp\!\left(\sum_p \ell_{x,x}(\sigma,p)\right),&x=y,\\
\exp\!\left(\sum_p \ell_{x,y}(\sigma,p)\right),&x\ne y.
\end{cases}
\]

The theorem GOne.extId constructs F from the analytic Rankin–Selberg input hS.rs. It proves analyticity of the logarithmic Euler series in a right half-plane using convergence_of_landau_euler, identifies F with the Euler-product expression for sufficiently large σ using euler_product_log_bound, and then extends the identity to all σ>1 by the identity theorem on the connected interval (1,∞).

This is the key pole distinction: the diagonal pair has a simple factor σ−1; an off-diagonal pair does not.

Sources: [IntegralityGoodReduction.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/TraceFormula/GL1SpectralBounds/IntegralityGoodReduction.lean), [NormLterm.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/TraceFormula/GL1SpectralBounds/NormLterm.lean).

### 3.2 LogTwo and CrossUp

From ExtId:

- LogTwo proves a two-sided logarithmic estimate for the diagonal series:
  \[
  \left|\Re\sum_p\ell_{x,x}(\sigma,p)+\log(\sigma-1)\right|\le C_x
  \quad(1<\sigma\le2).
  \]
  The proof first shows F(1) is nonzero. If F(1)=0, the logarithmic derivative would remain bounded near 1, contradicting the Landau unboundedness theorem for the diagonal Dirichlet series. Compactness of [1,2] then bounds log‖F(σ)‖, and the explicit factor σ−1 gives the logarithmic estimate.

- CrossUp proves a uniform upper bound for an off-diagonal series:
  \[
  \Re\sum_p\ell_{x,y}(\sigma,p)\le C_{x,y}
  \quad(x\ne y,\ 1<\sigma\le2).
  \]
  Here F is analytic and bounded on [1,2], while F(σ)=exp of the off-diagonal series; the exponential norm identity yields the bound.

Sources: [LogTwo.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/TraceFormula/GL1SpectralBounds/LogTwo.lean), [CrossUp.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/TraceFormula/GL1SpectralBounds/CrossUp.lean).

### 3.3 ShareBd: average over many distinct twists

ShareBd takes M distinct objects z_i and a unit-modulus scalar c(w) satisfying c(w)=1 on a chosen set I. If their power sums obey

\[
\sum_a A(z_i,w,a)^k
=c(w)^{ik}\sum_a A(x,w,a)^k,
\]

then the Rankin–Selberg terms satisfy a corresponding phase relation. Summing over i,j produces a nonnegative square:

\[
\left|\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}c(w)^{i(k+1)}\right|^2.
\]

On I, c(w)=1, so this square is M². The diagonal terms contribute the logarithmic pole from LogTwo; the off-diagonal terms contribute only bounded constants from CrossUp. After dividing by M², the proof obtains

\[
\Re\sum_{p:\,p_1\in I}\ell_{x,x}(\sigma,p)
\le -\frac{\log(\sigma-1)}{M}+C.
\]

Source: [ShareBd.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/TraceFormula/SplitRigidity/ShareBd.lean).

### 3.4 PartCS and AbsShare: force one constituent to equal x

PartCS is a Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for the partial Rankin–Selberg sum over I:

\[
\left|\sum_{p:\,p_1\in I}\ell_{x,y}(\sigma,p)\right|^2
\le
\Re\sum_{p:\,p_1\in I}\ell_{x,x}(\sigma,p)
\cdot
\Re\sum_p\ell_{y,y}(\sigma,p).
\]

Its proof is termwise: the square of the mixed local term equals the product of the two diagonal norms, then a summable Cauchy–Schwarz inequality is applied.

AbsShare assumes that x differs from every y_j and that outside I the power sums of x equal the sum of the power sums of the y_j. It combines:

- the diagonal logarithmic estimate from LogTwo;
- the off-diagonal estimate from CrossUp;
- the partial Cauchy–Schwarz inequality PartCS;
- the large-cardinality condition \(M\ge16(|J|+1)^2\).

If x were distinct from every y_j, each mixed series would be too small to account for the full diagonal logarithmic divergence. The proof sums the mixed estimates, compares the complementary-place Dirichlet series using the power-sum identity, and obtains

\[
-\frac12\log(\sigma-1)\le |C_1|+|C|+\sum_j|D_j|.
\]

It then chooses \(\sigma=1+\exp(-K)\), with K strictly larger than twice the right-hand constant, contradicting the inequality. Hence some y_j=x.

Sources: [PartCS.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/TraceFormula/GL1SpectralBounds/PartCS.lean), [AbsShare.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/TraceFormula/SplitRigidity/AbsShare.lean).

## 4. AntiTw: produce arbitrarily many distinct unitary twists

AntiTw supplies a unitary Hecke character χ that:

- is unramified at almost every finite place;
- has norm one at those places;
- is trivial at nonsplit places;
- makes every sequence π⊗χ^i unitary and pairwise distinct for every unitary π.

The source takes χ=μ². The character μ comes from Mu3.muExists; muInf records how twisting by μ^k shifts the archimedean infinitesimal characters by k times an exponent e. The proof uses the nonzero exponent to show that equality of two twists forces i=j. The square is chosen so that the resulting character has the required triviality at nonsplit places.

Sources: [AntiTw.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/TraceFormula/SplitRigidity/AntiTw.lean), [MuInf.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/TraceFormula/GaloisFromTraceFormula/MuInf.lean).

## 5. Assemble SplitRigid

splitRigid_of takes ShareBd, AbsShare, and AntiTw. Given π and the finite family P_j:

1. Choose χ from AntiTw.
2. Set \(M=16(|J|+1)^2\) and form the M twists π⊗χ^i. AntiTw guarantees they are distinct and unitary.
3. Form X as the finite range of the twists together with the P_j.
4. Remove a finite exceptional set S so that all required Satake parameters exist, χ is unramified, and the Rankin–Selberg local identities hold.
5. Use the twist relation on power sums to apply ShareBd.
6. Use the original split-place Satake sum identity outside I to apply AbsShare.
7. Conclude that some P_j equals π.

The local Satake bounds and determinant-one identities needed to build the system are supplied by the Jacquet–Shalika bound and the determinant-one condition theorem. The finite exceptional set is assembled explicitly from the bad places for the original matching, the character χ, the existence of Satake parameters, and the Rankin–Selberg local comparison.

Source: [ListOfMultiset.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/TraceFormula/SplitRigidity/ListOfMultiset.lean).

## 6. Normalize the finite obstruction: PartNorm, UnitOf, Thin, and AbsInf

The other input to has_of_split is PartNorm. For every constituent D.P i of a finite obstruction D, it produces a cuspidal representative P with:

- CG.mk P = D.P i;
- P normalized;
- P unitary.

Its proof uses four ingredients:

- Thin: inert places have a summable reciprocal-norm series, while the reciprocal-norm series over all finite places diverges. Therefore the split places are infinite. This allows selection of a split place outside the finite exceptional set where D and π have matching Satake data.
- UnitOf: a conjugate-self-dual cuspidal representation is unitary. The proof derives conjugate self-duality from the paired finite-dimensional forms and Gelfand–Kazhdan duality using Jacquet–Shalika rigidity, then applies the normalized Speh-pair unitarity theorem.
- AbsInf: twisting by the absolute-value character Model.absC E z shifts each archimedean infinitesimal-character multiset by z. This is proved directly from the exponent formula.
- Clozel's normalization twist: each constituent is first twisted by a parameter s to become normalized. The source then proves s=0. Archimedean regularity and the conjugate infinitesimal-character relation force \(2s\in\mathbb Z\); the Jacquet–Shalika Satake bound and determinant-one condition force \(-1<2\Re(s)<1\). Thus the integer is zero and s=0.

Consequently each constituent is already normalized and unitary, which supplies the family hypotheses required by SplitRigid.

Sources: [PartNorm.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/TraceFormula/GaloisFromTraceFormula/PartNorm.lean), [AbsInf.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/TraceFormula/GaloisFromTraceFormula/AbsInf.lean), [UnitOf.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/TraceFormula/GL1SpectralBounds/UnitOf.lean), [Thin.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/TraceFormula/GL1SpectralBounds/Thin.lean).

## 7. Eliminate the RunTh obstruction branch

RunTh gives either the desired automorphic representation or a finite obstruction D that:

- does not contain π;
- is regular;
- has the prescribed Satake data outside split places;
- matches π at almost all split places.

In the second branch, has_of_split:

1. obtains a unitary π using UnitOf;
2. applies PartNorm to make every constituent D.P i normalized and unitary;
3. applies SplitRigid to the family of constituents using the split-place Satake matching;
4. obtains an index i with D.P i=π;
5. contradicts the obstruction's assertion that D does not contain π.

Thus only the automorphic-representation branch survives. This is the concrete obstruction-elimination step used to obtain the DescWeak theorem consumed by VWin.
