# V3.53 — Adic topological bases from exact graded strictness

Pinned Lean snapshot 7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4. This document supplies an alternative proof of AinfTopBasis.main via associated graded rings, improving the strictness exponent in StrictIndep.strict_indep_pow for the concrete BK regular pair. The original strictness theorem uses m=2n; the argument here yields m=n. It does not remove the upstream adic completeness inputs. No fresh Lean check or novelty claim.

## 1. A two-element regular sequence gives a polynomial graded ring

Let B be a commutative ring, u,v∈B with u a nonzerodivisor and v a nonzerodivisor on B/uB. Write J=(u,v). The natural graded map
    (B/J)[U,V] → gr_J B = ⊕_(n≥0) J^n/J^(n+1),
sending U,V to the initial classes of u,v, is an isomorphism.

Surjectivity follows from the generators of J^n. For injectivity we show: if
    Σ_(i=0)^n u^i v^(n-i) a_i ∈ J^(n+1),
then every a_i∈J. Express the right side as a sum of degree n+1 monomials. Regroup it as Σ u^i v^(n-i) b_i with b_i∈J (every degree n+1 monomial is a degree n monomial times u or v). Replacing a_i by a_i−b_i reduces to an exact zero relation.

Induct on n, with n=0 immediate. Reducing the exact relation modulo u gives v^n a_0=0 mod u. Since v is regular modulo u, a_0=u t. Cancel u from the whole relation, obtaining
    v^(n-1)(a_1+v t)+Σ_(i=2)^n u^(i-1) v^(n-i) a_i=0.
By the induction hypothesis, a_1+v t,a_2,...,a_n all lie in J. Thus a_1∈J and a_0∈J as well. This proves injectivity in every degree, including n=0. No Noetherianity of B is needed.

## 2. Strictness with no loss of precision

Let e:S→A be a ring map, I=(p,X) in S and J=I A. Assume e(p),e(X) form a regular pair on A as above. Choose lifts b_i∈A of a basis of A/J as an S/I-vector space (for this application S/I is a field and A/J≠0). In particular
    Σ e(c_i)b_i∈J for finite coefficients ⇒ c_i∈I for each i.
Assume the analogous regular pair p,X in S, as holds in the BK power series ring.

Claim: for every n≥0 and every finite coefficient family (c_i),
    Σ e(c_i)b_i ∈ J^n  ⇒  all c_i∈I^n.
For n=0 this is tautological. Induct from n to n+1. The induction hypothesis gives c_i∈I^n. Write
    c_i=Σ_(k=0)^n p^k X^(n-k) a_(i,k).
If the original sum belongs to J^(n+1), then
    Σ_(k=0)^n e(p)^k e(X)^(n-k) [Σ_i e(a_(i,k)) b_i] ∈ J^(n+1).
By Section 1 for A, each bracket belongs to J. Residue independence gives a_(i,k)∈I, so c_i∈I^(n+1). This proof in fact does not need the regular pair in S for this implication, only the finite monomial expression in I^n.

Thus the exact strictness estimate m=n holds, rather than the source's m=2n obtained by comparing J^(2n) with (p^n,X^n) and doing two divisibility inductions. The reverse implication is immediate because e(I^n)⊂J^n.

## 3. Finite quotient bases and inverse limits

For each n≥1 define
    F_n=⊕_(i∈index set) S/I^n,
    Ψ_n:F_n→A/J^n,    (c_i)↦Σ e(c_i)b_i mod J^n.
Section 2 proves injectivity. Surjectivity follows from residue spanning and induction. Given x∈A, represent it modulo J by a finite sum. If a finite sum already approximates x modulo J^k, express the residual y∈J^k as a finite sum of products e(t_j)z_j with t_j∈I^k. Approximate each z_j modulo J by a finite b_i-sum, giving a correction with coefficients in I^k and residual in J^(k+1). After n steps Ψ_n is surjective.

The Ψ_n commute with the reduction maps, so taking inverse limits gives an S-linear isomorphism
    lim_n ⊕_i S/I^n  ≅  lim_n A/J^n  ≅ A,
provided A is J-adically complete and separated. If S is I-adically complete and separated, the left side identifies with the restricted product
    C_I = { (c_i)∈∏_i S : for every n, only finitely many c_i∉I^n }.
Indeed compatibility at each coordinate gives one element of S, and finite support at each precision is exactly the displayed restriction. Conversely a restricted family produces a compatible system.

Consequently every x∈A admits a unique restricted expansion
    x=Σ_i e(c_i(x)) b_i
in the J-adic topology. The coefficients are additive and S-linear because the inverse limit map is S-linear. The coefficient of b_i in b_j is δ_ij. For every n and every finite F containing all indices with c_i(x)∉I^n, the corresponding partial sum is congruent to x modulo J^n. If some b_i0=1 then c_i0(1)=1 and gives the S-linear retraction used in V3.49.

This supplies all the TopBasis fields in the source construction: b,c, S-linearity, c_self/c_other, fin, approx, complete, and distinguished index i0. The argument replaces separate existence and uniqueness of approximate functions by the explicit compatible isomorphisms Ψ_n. The basis need not be finite, and the inverse limit of direct sums is NOT replaced by the unrestricted product.

## 4. Application to the concrete BK package

S=W(F_q)[[X]]; I=(p,X) is maximal, S/I=F_q. A=W(O_C^flat), with e(X) the Teichmüller lift of the nonzero nonunit flat parameter x0. A has no p-torsion. Since A/pA=O_C^flat is a domain and x0≠0, multiplication by e(X) modulo p is injective. Thus the regular pair hypothesis in Section 1 holds.

The ideal J_A=(p,e(X)) is proper: its image under constant coefficient lies in (x0), a proper ideal of O_C^flat. Hence A/J_A is a nonzero vector space over F_q, and the residue of 1 is nonzero. Choose an ordinary vector space basis including that residue and lift it to b_i∈A, with the lift of 1 chosen to be exactly 1. This yields precisely the residue spanning and independence conditions of IdealSpanPrimeVariableBasis.

The source provides completeness of S for (p,X) (power series over the complete Witt DVR), and A for (p,e(X)) (Per.AinfComplete.main, applied to the nonunit Teichmüller element). With these exact completeness inputs Section 3 gives the (p,X)-topological basis.

The ideal named bk.J in the source is (p,E), not (p,X). Since E is monic Eisenstein of degree h≥1, (p,E)=(p,X^h). If I=(p,X), I'=(p,E), then I'⊂I and I^h⊂I' (every degree-h monomial in p,X is divisible by p or X^h). In particular their powers are cofinal and induce the same topology, both on S and after extension to A. A coefficient family is restricted for I exactly when it is restricted for I', and its expansion has the same limit. The same b_i and c_i give a TopBasis for bk.J. For a partial sum taken over all coefficients nonzero modulo I'^n, use an I^(hn)-approximation and enlarge the finite set as needed; any extra terms have coefficients in I'^n. Completeness also transfers by cofinality.

Thus AinfTopBasis.main follows by the exact quotient basis construction. No assumption of finite generation of A over S, and no use of a multiplicative retraction, enters the argument.

## 5. Consequences for the reconstruction

This removes a remaining input of V3.49/V3.50: the existence of topological coefficient functionals (conditional only on the indicated adic completeness and elementary BK ring facts). It also gives strictness without precision loss at (p,X). The separate period coefficient ideal theorem still uses the Frobenius unit lemma V3.50 and the topology of bk.J; no change to its hypotheses is needed.

Remaining: the source's Ainf completeness proof and the tilt two-copy descent/geometric comparison, plus the period seed and period comparison theorems. The sharper strictness is a mathematical alternative under the same regular-pair conditions, not a measured improvement of Lean runtime.
