## 1. The parameterized integral

Let

\[
s_0=\frac{d-n}{2},
\qquad
F(z)=
\int_{UA}
a(g)^{z-s_0}
\operatorname{coef}(\omega,\nu,\Phi)(g)
\overline{\operatorname{mc}(M,\phi)(g)}
\,d\mu(g).
\]

The Rallis_k proof establishes that F is holomorphic on a right half-plane and relates it to the k-pair.

The coefficient bound is

\[
\|\operatorname{coef}(\omega,\nu,\Phi)(g)\|
\leq C\,a(g)^{d/2},
\]

while the matrix coefficient is bounded. The height-integrability hypothesis therefore dominates the integrand by an integrable height power.

The lemma cpow_integral_pk is the analytic engine: it proves integrability and differentiability of parameterized integrals of the form

\[
z\longmapsto \int A(g)^{z-s_0}b(g)\,d\mu(g)
\]

whenever A is positive, b has polynomial growth in A, and sufficiently large real powers of A are integrable.

## 2. The k-pair continuation

ThkBaseKPair.lean proves kpair_cont_k. Its inputs are:

- a good base and Hermitian/skew space;
- compactness of the stabilizer subgroup;
- an admissible height a, positive and continuous;
- an Hecke character;
- an Eisenstein continuation package IsEisCont;
- integrability of sufficiently large powers of a.

It returns:

1. holomorphy of the k-pair on the Eisenstein continuation domain;
2. a right half-plane on which the k-pair unfolds as an integral against the height weight, the character determinant factor, and the fundamental-domain pet coefficient.

The proof uses the identity theorem for the Eisenstein continuation to establish right-invariance, then dominated differentiation on a compact fundamental-domain quotient. Finally it applies unfold0_k to obtain the integral formula.

## 3. Identify the two holomorphic functions

In TPGRallis.lean, the double identity, box-section estimate, full Siegel–Weil formula, and polynomial-Schur relation define a function f on the unitary group.

The source proves the exact coefficient identity

\[
f(e(1,g))\,\detInv(\chi_W)(g)
=
c'\,\operatorname{coef}(\omega,\nu,\Phi)(g^{-1}),
\qquad c'\neq0.
\]

Using this identity, the k-pair unfolding formula becomes

\[
kpair(z)=c\,c'\,F(z)
\]

on a sufficiently far right half-plane, where c is the nonzero Haar inversion-scaling constant.

The source also proves that F is differentiable on the corresponding half-plane using cpow_integral_pk.

## 4. Continue the equality to the special point

The auxiliary lemma zero_of_half_pk is an identity-theorem argument on a half-plane. It says that if two holomorphic functions agree on a right half-plane, then they agree at every point in the connected continuation domain.

Apply it to

\[
z\longmapsto kpair(z)-c\,c'F(z).
\]

This extends the equality to z=s0.

## 5. Evaluate the k-pair at s0

The special-value theorem An.at_value gives a theta-kernel formula for kpair(s0). In the current setting it expresses the value as a nonzero scalar multiple of the integral of the theta kernel against the automorphic vector.

If the theta lift is identically zero, then the inner integral vanishes for every target variable. The special-value formula therefore gives

\[
kpair(s_0)=0.
\]

Since c and c' are nonzero, the continued identity yields

\[
F(s_0)=
\int_{UA}
\operatorname{coef}(\omega,\nu,\Phi)(g)
\overline{\operatorname{mc}(M,\phi)(g)}
\,d\mu(g)=0.
\]

This is the zero-lift implication required by Rallis_k.

## 6. Where the Eisenstein continuation comes from

The Tools_k field swFull is constructed by swFullX_of from:

- SWAnisoX, the anisotropic Siegel–Weil formula;
- EisContinues, the Eisenstein continuation statement;
- BoxSecX, the box-section estimate.

The theorem normZeta_of then assembles the full package from swFull, BoxSecX, DblX, HgtEx, HgtInt, Stab2Cpt, CptAll, ThetaBasic, and PolSch.

This shows that the Rallis_k input is not an isolated axiom. Its analytic continuation and special-value formula are assembled from the same Siegel–Weil, Eisenstein, height, compactness, and theta identities already present in Tools_k.

## 7. Human-proof summary

The Rallis step can be written as:

1. Prove polynomial height growth of the coefficient.
2. Use height integrability to make the parameterized integral holomorphic.
3. Unfold the k-pair in a right half-plane.
4. Identify the unfolded integral with the coefficient/matrix-coefficient integral.
5. Continue the equality to the special point.
6. Evaluate the special point by the theta-kernel formula.
7. If the lift were zero, the special value and hence the coefficient integral would vanish.

This is the precise analytic-continuation bridge used in NonVan_k.
