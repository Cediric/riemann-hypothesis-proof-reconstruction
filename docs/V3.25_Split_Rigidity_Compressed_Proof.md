# V3.25 — Split rigidity: a compressed proof and the next bottleneck

> Upstream source snapshot: `CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean` at commit `7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4`.
>
> Scope: compress the source-level reconstruction in V3.24 into a short mathematical argument, clarify exactly where it enters the RH proof, and update the external verification boundary. This is an exposition of the pinned proof term, not a new proof or an independent re-proof of its deep inputs.

## 1. The proposition being proved

Let E be a CM number field. Let π be a unitary automorphic representation of rank n ≥ 1, and let P_j (j ∈ J) be a finite family of unitary automorphic representations of positive ranks. Suppose that at almost every split finite place w, the Satake multiset of π is the multiset sum of the Satake multisets of the P_j.

The split-rigidity conclusion is

\[
\exists j\in J,\qquad P_j\simeq\pi
\]

with equality understood in the disjoint union of representation ranks: the rank must agree as well as the representation.

This is the exact input used to eliminate the finite-obstruction branch of `RunTh` and obtain the `DescWeak` theorem consumed by the `VWin` descent.

## 2. The analytic quantities

Write `ell_{ρ,ρ′}(σ;w,r)` for the local logarithmic Rankin–Selberg term at a finite place w and prime-power index r ≥ 0. For a set of places S, define the complex mixed series and its real part by

\[
\mathcal C_{\rho,\rho'}(S,\sigma)
=\sum_{w\in S}\sum_{r\geq0}\ell_{\rho,\rho'}(\sigma;w,r),
\qquad
L_{\rho,\rho'}(S,\sigma)=\operatorname{Re}\mathcal C_{\rho,\rho'}(S,\sigma),
\qquad
T=-\log(\sigma-1),
\]

where \(1<\sigma\leq2\), so \(T\geq0\).

The two analytic estimates used by the proof are:

- **Diagonal pole:** for each fixed unitary ρ,
  \[
  L_{\rho,\rho}(\text{all},\sigma)=T+O_\rho(1).
  \]
  This is the content of `LogTwo`: the diagonal Rankin–Selberg series has the logarithmic divergence forced by its simple pole at s = 1.

- **Off-diagonal upper bound:** if ρ ≄ ρ′, then
  \[
  L_{\rho,\rho'}(\text{all},\sigma)\leq O_{\rho,\rho'}(1).
  \]
  This is `CrossUp`. It is a one-sided upper bound; it must not be silently strengthened to an absolute-value estimate.

All constants here are uniform as σ ↓ 1, with the representations and the field fixed.

## 3. The proof in five moves

Let m = |J|, choose

\[
M=16(m+1)^2,
\]

and let I be the set of nonsplit finite places.

### Move 1 — Make many distinct twists that are invisible on I

The `AntiTw` lemma constructs a unitary Hecke character χ such that:

1. χ is unramified at almost every finite place;
2. |χ(w)| = 1 at those places;
3. χ(w) = 1 at nonsplit places;
4. the representations
   \[
   \pi_i=\pi\otimes\chi^i,\qquad 0\leq i<M,
   \]
   are pairwise distinct and unitary.

Consequently, the twists have the same local Satake data as (\pi) on I, while their global Rankin–Selberg series are off-diagonal when i ≠ j.

### Move 2 — Average all pairs of twists

At a good place, twisting multiplies the Satake parameters by a unit complex phase c(w)^i. In the sum over all ordered pairs ((i,j)), the local contribution contains

\[
\left|\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}c(w)^{i(r+1)}\right|^2\geq0.
\]

On I, (c(w)=1), so this factor is exactly M². Globally, the (M) diagonal pairs contribute (M) logarithmic poles, while the (M(M-1)) off-diagonal pairs contribute only bounded terms. Positivity lets us discard the contribution outside I. Dividing by M² gives

\[
L_{\pi,\pi}(I,\sigma)
\leq \frac{T}{M}+O(1).
\tag{1}
\]

This is `ShareBd`. The crucial point is the gain 1/M: many globally distinct twists become locally indistinguishable on the nonsplit places.

### Move 3 — Cauchy–Schwarz makes every mixed term too small

Assume, for contradiction, that P_j ≄ π for every j. The termwise identity behind \`PartCS\` gives

\[
\left|\mathcal C_{\pi,P_j}(I,\sigma)\right|^2
\leq
L_{\pi,\pi}(I,\sigma)\,
L_{P_j,P_j}(\text{all},\sigma),
\]

with the diagonal terms nonnegative. Combining this with (1), the diagonal pole estimate, and the off-diagonal estimate yields the bound used in `AbsShare`:

\[
L_{\pi,P_j}(I^c,\sigma)
\leq \frac{T}{3(m+1)}+O_j(1).
\tag{2}
\]

Here I^c is the set of split places. The numerical choice M ≥ 16(m+1)² is what makes the Cauchy–Schwarz loss small enough. The displayed bound is a bound for the real part of the complementary mixed series, not a claim that every individual local term is nonnegative.

### Move 4 — The Satake identity forces an exact sum identity

At almost every split place, the assumed multiset identity

\[
\operatorname{Sat}_w(\pi)
=\sum_{j\in J}\operatorname{Sat}_wP_j
\]

implies equality of every power sum. Since the logarithmic Rankin–Selberg term is built from these power sums, summing over split places gives

\[
L_{\pi,\pi}(I^c,\sigma)
=\sum_{j\in J}L_{\pi,P_j}(I^c,\sigma).
\tag{3}
\]

The finite exceptional set is removed before this identity is used, so all Satake parameters and local twist identities are defined.

### Move 5 — The two estimates contradict each other

By the diagonal pole estimate and (1),

\[
L_{\pi,\pi}(I^c,\sigma)
=L_{\pi,\pi}(\text{all},\sigma)
 -L_{\pi,\pi}(I,\sigma)
\geq
\left(1-\frac1M\right)T-O(1).
\]

But (2) and (3) give

\[
L_{\pi,\pi}(I^c,\sigma)
\leq
\frac{m}{3(m+1)}T+O(1)
\leq\frac13T+O(1).
\]

Therefore

\[
\left(1-\frac1M-\frac{m}{3(m+1)}\right)T\leq O(1).
\]

Since M ≥ 16, the coefficient is at least

\[
\frac{15}{16}-\frac13=\frac{29}{48}>\frac12.
\]

Thus (T\) would have to remain bounded. But T = −log(σ−1) → ∞ as σ ↓ 1. Contradiction. Hence at least one `P_j ≃ π`, proving split rigidity.

## 4. Why the finite obstruction can be eliminated

`RunTh` produces either the desired automorphic representation or a finite obstruction D. In the obstruction branch, D is regular, matches the target Satake data at almost all split places, and by definition does not contain the target representation.

The constituents of D are not initially in the exact normalized-unitary form required by split rigidity. `PartNorm` supplies that form:

- `Thin` ensures there are infinitely many split places, so a suitable split place can be chosen outside any finite exceptional set;
- `UnitOf` obtains unitarity of the target from the conjugate-self-dual input;
- the normalization twist and `AbsInf` show that the normalizing exponent is zero.

Now apply split rigidity to the normalized unitary constituents of D. It forces one constituent to equal the target representation, contradicting the defining exclusion in the obstruction branch. Therefore only the automorphic-representation branch of `RunTh` survives.

This is the role of V3.24 in one sentence: **it closes the finite-obstruction branch needed for the descent theorem; it is not the whole proof of RH.**

## 5. What this compression does—and does not—remove

The detailed source-module analysis in V3.24 reduce to three mathematical engines:

1. a diagonal/off-diagonal Rankin–Selberg pole dichotomy;
2. averaging over many twists that agree on nonsplit places;
3. a Cauchy–Schwarz contradiction against the split-place Satake identity.

The proof still relies on substantial inputs: the Rankin–Selberg pole theorem and analytic continuation, Jacquet–Shalika bounds and determinant-one Satake identities, the existence and archimedean behavior of the Hecke character used in `AntiTw`, and the normalization/unitarity results used by `PartNorm`. The compression exposes where these inputs enter; it does not re-prove them.
