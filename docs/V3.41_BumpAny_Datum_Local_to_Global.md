## 1. BumpAny_k: a local bump from a congruence subgroup

BumpAny_k says that for every finite place v and every neighborhood N of the identity in the local unitary group, there are:

- an open subgroup H contained in N;
- a local Schwartz/vector function phi;
- the required IsBump properties: phi is finite, nonzero, H-fixed, and separates elements of the local integral group that do not lie in H.

The theorem bumpAny_k constructs this from Tools_k.

### Step A — Choose a local Schrödinger model and a lattice

- locRepEx_k gives the local representation model rho.
- exists_weilLatticeDatum gives a lattice Lambda, a vector x1, a uniformizer varpi, and a package of lattice/Gram properties.
- The vacuum vector has a nonzero local component and is fixed by a sufficiently small open subgroup.

### Step B — Choose a small congruence subgroup

Choose k large enough that the congruence conditions lie inside N and inside the subgroup fixing the vacuum. Put

\[
s=\operatorname{sg}(v,\varpi^k)
\]

and define H by requiring:

- local integrality at v;
- \(mat(g)-1\in s\Lambda\);
- \(mat(g^{-1})-1\in s\Lambda\).

The lattice multiplication and scalar-closure properties prove that H is a subgroup. The neighborhood property of the lattice datum gives H ⊆ N.

### Step C — Construct the nonzero H-fixed vector

Set \(x_0=s^{-1}x_1\) and define

\[
\phi_v=\operatorname{Schr}(\psi_v,x_0)(vac_v).
\]

The local representation identity shows that phi_v is finite and nonzero. The Gram relation forces the relevant symplectic pairing to vanish on H, so H fixes phi_v.

### Step D — Separate every element outside H

If g is locally integral but g∉H, the lattice criterion gives a vector u separating the cosets. The theorem exists_separating_of_not_mem_lattice_coset produces two different eigenvalues for the local action on phi_v. Therefore g cannot satisfy the bump stabilizer condition.

This proves IsBump and hence BumpAny_k.

The local argument is a standard congruence-subgroup construction in the Schrödinger/Weil model, with the lattice datum controlling both invariance and separation.

## 2. Datum_k: assemble local bumps into one finite test vector

Datum_k takes:

- a finite set S of places outside which the target character is unramified;
- local bump subgroups H(v) and functions phi(v) for v∈S;
- the vacuum function outside S;
- a nonzero polynomial vector f0.

It constructs the global finite function

\[
\phi_f(v)=
\begin{cases}
\phi(v),&v\in S,\\
vac(v),&v\notin S.
\end{cases}
\]

The result has three properties.

### (D1) Nonzero coefficient at the identity

\[
coef(\omega,\nu,pure(f_0,\phi_f))(1)\neq0.
\]

The proof uses the double identity T.dbl to express the coefficient as a nonzero scalar times the square norm of the pure vector. The pure vector is nonzero because f0 is nonzero and every local component of phi_f is nonzero.

### (D2) Support is localized in the selected bump subgroups

For x in the infinite-place group, b in the selected finite-place group, and c in the off-place group,

\[
b\notin\bigvee_{v\in S}H(v)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
coef(\omega,\nu,pure(f_0,\phi_f))(x b c)=0.
\]

If b lies outside the product of the local bump subgroups, choose a place v where it fails. The local separating vector from BumpAny_k and the orthogonality lemma force the coefficient to vanish.

### (D3) Off-place factorization

There is a scalar s(c) such that

\[
\|s(c)\|=a(e(c,1))^{d/2},
\]

\[
IntAll(c)\Longrightarrow s(c)=1,
\]

and for x in the infinite-place group and b in the product of the local bump subgroups,

\[
coef(\omega,\nu,pure(f_0,\phi_f))(x b c)
=s(c)\,coef(\omega,\nu,pure(f_0,\phi_f))(x).
\]

The proof uses:

- T.dbl to relate the box operator to the coefficient;
- T.vacFix to control places outside S;
- T.polSch and the polynomial eigenvector relation;
- the local Iwasawa decomposition cut_iwa_pk;
- the unramified character condition outside S.

For a nonintegral off-place element c, the Iwasawa factorization gives a parabolic factor with determinant t. The scalar is

\[
s(c)=\chi_W(\det t)\,|\det t|^{d/2}.
\]

Its norm is exactly the height weight required by NonVan_k. If c is integral everywhere, the parabolic factor is trivial and s(c)=1.

## 3. How BumpAny and Datum fit into NonVan_k

The construction is deliberately split:

1. BumpAny_k is local: for each selected place and each prescribed neighborhood, produce one nonzero bump vector with a controlled stabilizer.
2. Datum_k is global: place those bumps at finitely many places, use the vacuum elsewhere, and prove the global coefficient has the exact support and factorization properties.
3. NonVan_k combines Datum_k with Tail_k, Decomp_k, SchurStep_k, and Rallis_k.

The resulting proof has a clean architecture:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{local Weil lattice datum}
\Longrightarrow \text{BumpAny}_k,\\
&\text{BumpAny}_k+\text{double/vacuum/Schur identities}
\Longrightarrow \text{Datum}_k,\\
&\text{Datum}_k+\text{Tail}_k+\text{Decomp}_k+\text{SchurStep}_k
+\text{Rallis}_k\\
&\qquad\Longrightarrow \text{NonVan}_k.
\end{aligned}
\]

## 4. What is genuinely difficult

The local bump construction is a lattice/Schrödinger calculation. The global datum construction is a finite-support assembly plus the double identity, polynomial eigenrelation, and Iwasawa factorization.

The genuinely deep inputs are therefore concentrated in:

- existence of the local Weil lattice datum;
- the double identity;
- vacuum fixing and polynomial-Schur eigenrelations;
- the local Iwasawa/cut decomposition;
- the global Rallis integral and Schur identity.

The long Lean proofs are largely bookkeeping for these explicit constructions, rather than one opaque nonvanishing assumption.
