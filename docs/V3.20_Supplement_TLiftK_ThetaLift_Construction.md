## 1. What TLiftK actually asserts

The definition is in ThetaParseval/HalfIndexLattices/TLiftK.lean.

For every CM number field E and a compatible base B over its maximal real subfield, assume the base is good. For every positive rank n and a hermitian datum X of rank n, there is a threshold d₀ such that for every d ≥ d₀ and every compatible skew-hermitian datum W of rank d, with the stated determinant condition, the following data:

- Hecke characters χX and χW and their exponent/weight conditions;
- a unitary automorphic representation U of the unitary group attached to X;
- a dominant-weight bound relating d, mW and the weights of U;

produce a unitary automorphic representation U' of the unitary group attached to W, together with the required theta-lift relation and local compatibility.

In short, TLiftK is a uniform high-rank theta-lift existence theorem: sufficiently large target rank d admits a nonzero automorphic lift from the given source representation, with prescribed algebraic and local constraints.

This is the exact theorem consumed by the Galois-representation construction; it is not itself the final Galois row.

Source: [TLiftK.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/HalfIndexLattices/TLiftK.lean).

## 2. The intermediate Tools_k package

GalTPDefs.lean defines Tools_k as a proposition with fourteen fields:

| Field | Mathematical role |
|---|---|
| swFull | full Siegel–Weil/doubling input |
| boxSec | box-section control |
| dbl | doubling identity |
| stab2 | stabilizer and compactness input |
| cpt | compactness/finite-volume package |
| basic | basic theta-kernel identities |
| polSch | polynomial/Schur input |
| unfold0 | unfolding identity |
| pairEx | existence of the relevant pair/model |
| split | split-model construction |
| vacFix | fixed-vector/vacuum statement |
| acts | existence and compatibility of actions |
| lie1 | first Lie-algebra statement |
| lie2 | second Lie-algebra statement |

The key point is that Tools_k is not introduced as an extra hypothesis at the final theorem. TPGHolds.lean constructs a term tools_holds_k : Tools_k by filling all fourteen fields with named theorem results.

## 3. How tools_holds_k is assembled

The constructor in TPGHolds.lean makes the following dependencies explicit:

- swFull is built from anisotropic Siegel–Weil, Eisenstein continuation, frame conjugation, and the finite-level/box-section results.
- boxSec, dbl, and stab2 are supplied by the finite-level, doubling, and stabilizer theorems.
- cpt is assembled from group-topology bounds, epsilon estimates, Siegel-set input, finite volume, and unimodularity.
- basic, polSch, and unfold0 come from the theta-basic, polynomial-Schur, and unfolding theorems.
- pairEx is obtained from the pair-existence/model-existence results and the Weil-representation existence theorem.
- split, vacFix, and acts come from the split-free, vacuum-fixed, and action-existence constructions.
- lie1 and lie2 are assembled from the local-lift theorem and the real Lie-algebra derivative-existence theorem.

The height-existence input hgtEx is then obtained from stab2Cpt via hgtEx_of_k.

This matters because the proof of TLiftK is a composition of substantial subtheorems, not a single opaque axiom. The remaining task is to audit the full transitive dependencies of these named inputs.

## 4. The proof skeleton of tLift_of_tools_k

The main construction is in GalTPDefs.lean. The wrapper in TPGTop.lean supplies the derived lemmas:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\texttt{tLift\_of\_tools\_k}(T,h_H)
={}&\texttt{tLift\_of\_k}\ T\ h_H\ \texttt{brEx}\\
&(\texttt{hgtFst\_k}\ T)\ (\texttt{realPair\_k}\ T)\\
&(\texttt{nonVan\_k}\ T\ \texttt{rallis\_k}\ T\ \texttt{decomp\_k}\ T\\
&\qquad\texttt{bumpAny\_k}\ T\ \texttt{datum\_k}\ T\ \texttt{tail\_k}\ T\
\texttt{absNe}\ \texttt{schurStep\_k}\ T)\\
&(\texttt{liftAut\_k}\ T)\ \texttt{projEx}\\
&(\texttt{readLift\_k}\ T\ (\texttt{liftAut\_k}\ T)\ \texttt{absIso}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Inside tLift_of_k, the proof follows a concrete sequence:

1. Obtain a stabilizer K, height function a, and exponent ε from the height-existence theorem.
2. Obtain a threshold t₀ from the first height-integrability theorem.
3. Choose a natural threshold N greater than 2t₀, then set the target threshold to max(N, 2n).
4. Use pairEx to construct the model M and its representation datum.
5. Use brEx to obtain the comparison/isometry maps for X and W.
6. Use realPair_k to produce a nonzero polynomial datum f₀, a nonzero vector φ in U, and the required weight/type data.
7. Use nonVan_k to produce a finite vector φf and prove the relevant lifted coefficient is nonzero. This sub-result is itself assembled from Rallis, decomposition, bump, datum, tail, absolute nonvanishing, and Schur-step inputs.
8. Use liftAut_k to construct the theta-lift operator Θ.
9. Use the nonvanishing statement to show Θ is nonzero on the selected datum.
10. Use projEx to construct the target automorphic representation U' and an equivariant projection.
11. Use readLift_k to read off the target weights and local-pair compatibility.

The output is exactly the U' and local compatibility required by TLiftK.

\[

\Longrightarrow
\texttt{galoisRepresentations\_of\_thetaLift}
\Longrightarrow
\texttt{galoisLocalGlobalStrong\_recParameter}

GaloisRepresentationsOfThetaLift.lean invokes galoisLocalGlobalStrong_of_geometry_lift with Geo.geoOne_holds. Development.lean converts the theta-lift and geometric inputs into CoreAll, then obtains the local-global compatibility row and upgrades it to Row0b.

## 6. Audit boundary

This source-level decomposition establishes that TLiftK is constructed from a fourteen-field Tools_k package and a separate height-existence theorem, and that its proof has a concrete nonvanishing/theta-lift/projection sequence.

It does not establish that every imported theorem is free of unexpected axioms, nor that every analytic or representation-theoretic input is mathematically correct. The pending remote run must first complete the pinned Lean build and the #print axioms scripts. After that, the high-priority follow-up is to audit the transitive dependencies of:

- the Siegel–Weil and doubling inputs;
- the Rallis/nonvanishing chain;
- the real-pair and automorphic-lift constructions;
- the geometric Galois-row upgrade.
