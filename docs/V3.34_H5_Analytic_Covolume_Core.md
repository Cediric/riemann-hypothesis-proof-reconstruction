# V3.34 — H5 analytic core: the lcv_le inequality and exponential slack

> Scope: compress the generic covolume assembly theorem. This isolates the analytic inequality that turns eight geometric/lift hypotheses into the family covolume bound.

## 1. The eight hypotheses are pointwise estimates

For a fixed family point p and index i, the eight inputs become:

- GramAt: a Gram-matrix/inner-product control;
- RecAt: the recursive lift relation;
- CutAt: the truncation/cut estimate;
- BadAt: control of the bad locus;
- LamAt: the lambda/height bound with exponent A;
- SizeAt: the size bound with exponent B and parameter PP;
- CoreAt: a core bound with constant C1;
- LowAt: a low-bound estimate with constant C2.

These are the pointwise forms of GramCondition, LiftRecursive, LiftTruncation, LiftBadSet, LiftLambdaBound, SizeCondition, CoreBound', and LiftLowBound.

## 2. The analytic core: lcv_le

CovolumeFromGramAndBounds.lean proves the central estimate:

\[
\frac{\operatorname{lcv}(XW_i,M_i)}{D}
\leq
\operatorname{mainT}(p,i)
+(C_1+C_2+1)\operatorname{unitE}(p).
\tag{H5-core}
\]

The proof uses all eight pointwise hypotheses. Its key analytic input is CutNum.slope_good_ge, applied with the exponentially small parameter

\[
\delta(x)=\exp\!\left(-x^{e_z/7}\right).
\]

The comparison is between the squared slope on the truncated lattice type and the squared slope on the good lattice. The Gram, cut, and size hypotheses provide the geometric side conditions; the bad-locus and low/core estimates control the residual terms.

The resulting loss is bounded by

\[
12\exp\!\left(-x^{e_z/7}\right)
\left(x^A+x^B+C_1+C_2+1\right)\operatorname{unitE}(p).
\]

The size hypothesis supplies nonnegativity of unitE, so the scalar smallness estimate can be multiplied through without reversing inequalities.

## 3. Why the exponential error can be absorbed

The auxiliary lemma tendsto_exp_mul_rpow proves that for every c > 0 and real A,

\[
\exp(-x^c)x^A\longrightarrow0
\qquad (x\to\infty).
\]

Applying this to A, B, and the constant term gives exists_loss_le: there is x0 such that

\[
12\exp\!\left(-x^{e_z/7}\right)
\left(x^A+x^B+C_1+C_2+1\right)\leq1
\]

for every x ≥ x0.

This is the only asymptotic absorption step in the generic covolume assembly. The rest is pointwise inequality manipulation.

## 4. Globalize the pointwise estimate

The theorem covolX_of_steps takes the eight family-level hypotheses and:

1. extracts their individual thresholds;
2. chooses x0 as the maximum of those thresholds and the exponential-error threshold;
3. chooses the common covolume constant
   \[
   C_0=C_1+C_2+1;
   \]
4. applies lcv_le at every family point beyond x0;
5. enlarges the right-hand constant from C1+C2+1 to any C ≥ C0, using unitE ≥ 0.

The result is CovolX. The wrapper covolX_of_truncation_badSet_lambda_lowBound then packages the same conclusion as CovolumeBoundOfX.

Thus the generic argument is:

\[
\text{eight pointwise lift/Gram estimates}
\Longrightarrow \text{lcv\_le}
\Longrightarrow \text{exponential absorption}
\Longrightarrow \text{uniform CovolumeBoundOfX}.
\]

## 5. What this removes from the human proof

The final covolume assembly is not a mysterious global compactness argument. Once the eight estimates are established, the proof is:

- one slope comparison;
- one exponential-versus-polynomial limit;
- one common-threshold choice;
- one monotonicity step in the constant.

The real geometric work is upstream: proving GramAt, RecAt, CutAt, BadAt, LamAt, SizeAt, CoreAt, and LowAt from the toroidal/Hodge/Arakelov data.
