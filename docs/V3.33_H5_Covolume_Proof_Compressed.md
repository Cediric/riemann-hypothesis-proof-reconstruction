## 1. The last step is a clean assembly theorem

CovolXOfStepsStatement.lean proves

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{GramCondition}\land\text{LiftRecursive}\land
\text{LiftTruncation}\land\text{LiftBadSet}\\
&\land\text{LiftLambdaBound}\land\text{SizeCondition}
\land\text{CoreBound}'\land\text{LiftLowBound}\\
&\qquad\Longrightarrow \text{CovolumeBoundOfX}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The proof is a direct application of covolX_of_lift_bounds_and_coreLe. That theorem chooses one common constant C0 from the core and low-bound constants, takes a threshold large enough for the eight family estimates, and applies the pointwise covolume estimate lcv_le.

The difficult work is therefore to construct these eight inputs.

## 2. The proof of topCovolG_alt_alt in four blocks

The source theorem topCovolG_alt_alt takes a volume-estimate package, HypothesesX, PublishedHodgeBundleTheorems, HypothesesH, GrothendieckDeformation, and two quoted/published analytic packages. Its proof can be compressed into four blocks.

### Block A — Geometric setup and the Gram/size/measure bounds

From the Hodge-bundle and toroidal inputs it obtains:

- a covering Gram matrix from the published Hodge-bundle data;
- the basic size bound;
- a covering-measure bound;
- an anchor bound;
- a deep-extension bound.

These are the source lemmas covering_gram_of_prints, cov_size_bound, covering_meas_of_prints, anchor_bound, and covering_deepEx_of_prints.

### Block B — Good reduction, flat degree, cover degree, and sharing

The toroidal, semi-abelian, Grothendieck-deformation, and quoted analytic inputs give:

- deep good reduction;
- flat degree;
- multiplicative cover degree;
- the share estimate.

The key dependency is that the cover-degree and share estimates are not independent: coverDegree_of_grothendieck_deformation uses deep good reduction, size, and flat degree; share_of_deep_good_size_flat_deg_cover_degree then combines those with the same geometric data.

### Block C — Heights and a uniform lower bound

The volume-estimate package and Hodge/toroidal data give:

- a general lower bound for heights;
- a cover-height-sum estimate;
- a uniform lower bound for the relevant sums.

The source calls these height_lower_bound_general_alt, coverHeightSum_of_flatDeg_coverDegree_share, and uniform_lower_bound_of_sums'.

This block is where the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel, Moriwaki, Fargues/Zarhin, and nef inputs feed into a usable uniform height estimate.

### Block D — Build the lift hypotheses and apply the eight-input assembly

The proof constructs:

- LiftRecursive from the Gram bound;
- LiftTruncation from the published bundle data;
- LiftBadSet from deep reduction, size, flat degree, cover degree, share, and the Green/Gram estimates;
- LiftLambdaBound from the Gram and measure bounds;
- CoreBound' from Gram, measure, and rational-lift data;
- LiftLowBound from the uniform lower bound, low-defect estimate, covering ring/maps/rank/genus, and flatness.

It then calls covolX_of_truncation_badSet_lambda_lowBound, which is exactly the eight-input assembly from Section 1.

In short:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Hodge/toroidal + volume inputs}\\
&\Longrightarrow
\text{Gram, size, measure, anchor, deep extension}\\
&\Longrightarrow
\text{deep good reduction, flat degree, cover degree, share}\\
&\Longrightarrow
\text{height lower bound and uniform lower bound}\\
&\Longrightarrow
\text{eight Lift/Gram/Core/Low conditions}\\
&\Longrightarrow \text{CovolumeBoundOfX}.
\end{aligned}
\]

## 3. What the fifteen high-level inputs are doing

The fifteen inputs to topCovolSlim2_use_alt_alt divide into two groups.

### Deep slim-volume inputs

ResolutionCharZero, PrincipalisationCharZero, PoincareLelongInv, HSUse, MoriwakiBigStatement, FarguesExact, ZarhinStatement, and NefRR are passed to deep_slim_use. They produce the volume estimate consumed by the global covolume theorem.

### Toroidal and Hodge-bundle inputs

SiegelToroidalIntegral, ToroidalForFanKS, SemiAbelianLieSeminegative, PointSeparationPair, ArDegree, PublishedHodgeBundleTheorems, and GrothendieckDeformation build HypothesesX, toroidal charts, HypothesesH, Chow-form/height comparisons, and the extension/covering geometry.

This separation is important: the first group controls the deep volume/height side; the second group constructs the geometric objects and the Hodge/toroidal framework on which those estimates are evaluated.

## 4. The top-level wrapper is short; the mathematical inputs are not

topCovolSlim2_use_alt_alt is essentially a composition:

1. construct HypothesesX from the toroidal fan, semi-abelian, Green, point-separation, and Fubini–Study/Chow-height inputs;
2. construct toroidal charts and HypothesesH;
3. build the Chow-form and small-section injectivity package;
4. apply topCovolG_alt_alt with the deep slim-volume estimate.

The proof term is compact because the intermediate theorems are already available. A human-readable proof must still explain what those intermediate estimates say and where the published hypotheses enter.

## 5. H5 remains conditional on published geometry

The formal theorem is kernel-checked relative to its explicit inputs. The current reconstruction has not independently proved resolution, principalisation, the Poincare–Lelong inverse statement, arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel, Moriwaki bigness, or the full Hodge/toroidal package.

The next step is to trace the eight Lift/Gram/Core/Low conditions back through their individual constructors and then identify the exact published theorem used for each. This is a more productive route than trying to translate the top-level theorem's one-line composition directly.

## 6. Connection back to the main inequality

Once CovolumeBoundOfX is available, linked_bundles_l3_of_open_alt converts it into CovolumeBoundThirdPlaceSeries, provided the discrete-series, Harish–Chandra, cusp-decay, Casselman–Wallach, RayField, and open-extension inputs hold.

That row-level H5 input then enters the arithmetic main inequality (M). H5 is therefore a geometric covolume supply theorem, not a local Rankin–Selberg or slope estimate.
