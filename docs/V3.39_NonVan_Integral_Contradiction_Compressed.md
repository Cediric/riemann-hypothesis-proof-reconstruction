# V3.39 — NonVan_k compressed: a Rallis integral contradiction

> Upstream snapshot: CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean at commit 7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4.
>
> Scope: compress the proof of NonVan_k into its actual analytic argument. The theorem proves that a suitable finite test vector gives a nonzero theta lift. The key mechanism is a nonzero main integral plus a strictly smaller tail.

## 1. The target

NonVan_k states that for every admissible source/target pair, automorphic representation U, nonzero polynomial vector f0, and nonzero automorphic vector phi of the prescribed type, there is a finite test function phi_f such that

\[
\operatorname{lift}\bigl(M,\omega,\operatorname{pure}(f_0,\phi_f),
\phi\circ e^{-1}\bigr)\neq0.
\]

This is the nonvanishing input used by tLift_of_tools_k.

## 2. The eight inputs and their jobs

The theorem nonVan_k takes:

| Input | Role in the proof |
|---|---|
| Tools_k | Supplies compactness, stabilizer, theta, and model data |
| Rallis_k | Gives an integrable global theta integral and says a zero lift forces that integral to vanish |
| Decomp_k | Decomposes the adelic unitary group into infinite, selected finite, and off-place factors; supplies Haar-measure factorization |
| BumpAny_k | Produces local compactly supported bump functions near the identity |
| Datum_k | Produces a finite test vector and a nonzero coefficient scalar with off-place factorization |
| Tail_k | Makes the nonintegral off-place tail smaller than the integral compact part |
| AbsNe | Abstract measure lemma turning the main-term identity and strict tail bound into nonvanishing |
| SchurStep_k | Supplies a nonzero scalar Schur identity for the infinite-place integral |

Tools_k is passed separately because the proof uses its compactness field cpt and the stabilizer data.

## 3. The proof in six steps

### Step 1 — Control the matrix coefficient near the identity

The proof converts phi to the function

\[
\phi'(g)=\phi(e^{-1}g)
\]

on the unitary group. Since phi is a nonzero automorphic form, its matrix coefficient is bounded by its value at the identity. This gives constants r, r0, r1, r2.

The level subgroup fixing phi is open. Its inverse image gives an open neighborhood N of the identity on which right translation leaves phi' unchanged.

### Step 2 — Choose a finite set of places

Decomp_k supplies a compact open off-place subgroup. The finite-place-neighborhood lemma gives a finite set S0 such that the off-place subgroup outside S0 lies in N.

A separate unramified-outside-finite-set lemma gives R. Tail_k enlarges S0 ∪ R to a finite set S for which

\[
\int_{\{c\in gOff(S):\neg IntAll(c)\}}
a(\epsilon(c,1))^{d/2}\,d\mu_C
<
\mu_C\{c:IntAll(c)\}.
\tag{tail}
\]

This is the strict tail estimate.

### Step 3 — Build the finite test function

For each v in S, BumpAny_k supplies a compactly supported local bump function b_v in a neighborhood H_v of the identity. Define

\[
\phi_f(v)=
\begin{cases}
b_v,&v\in S,\\
M.vac(v),&v\notin S.
\end{cases}
\]

The finite-support condition IsFin follows because S is finite and the vacuum is the default outside S.

Datum_k then supplies:

- a nonzero scalar hc1;
- a coefficient factorization hco;
- a scalar function s(c) describing the off-place dependence.

In particular, the coefficient at the identity is nonzero.

### Step 4 — Use the adelic group decomposition

Decomp_k provides a topological group embedding/decomposition

\[
j:\ gInf\times gAt(S)\times gOff(S)\longrightarrow UA
\]

with the required closedness, compactness, commutation, and Haar-measure triple-product decomposition.

The local bump subgroups are chosen inside the stabilizer of phi'. Hence the matrix coefficient is invariant under the selected finite-place subgroup. The integral over the adelic group can therefore be split into:

- the infinite-place integral;
- the selected finite-place compact integral;
- the off-place integral.

The compact integral has finite measure and nonzero measure.

### Step 5 — Compute the main term by Schur

SchurStep_k supplies \(\kappa\neq0\) and the identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\int_{gInf}
\operatorname{coef}(\omega,\nu,\operatorname{pure}(f_0,\phi_f))(x)\\
&\qquad\cdot
\overline{\operatorname{mc}(M,\phi')(xc)}\,d\mu_A(x)\\
&=
\kappa\,
\operatorname{coef}(\omega,\nu,\operatorname{pure}(f_0,\phi_f))(1)
\overline{\operatorname{mc}(M,\phi')(c)}
\end{aligned}
\]

for the relevant off-place elements c.

Because both \(\kappa\) and the coefficient at the identity are nonzero, the main term is nonzero.

### Step 6 — Contradict a zero lift

Rallis_k gives an integrable global integral and the implication

\[
\operatorname{lift}=0
\Longrightarrow
\text{global theta integral}=0.
\]

Assume the lift is zero. HaarMeasureTripleProductDecomposition and the Datum_k factorization rewrite the global integral as the main term minus the off-place contribution.

The tail estimate (tail) makes the nonintegral off-place contribution strictly smaller than the integral compact contribution. AbsNe then says the resulting global integral cannot vanish, contradicting the Rallis implication.

Therefore the constructed finite test function has nonzero theta lift.

## 4. The proof's real structure

The Lean proof is long because it explicitly constructs all topological and measure-theoretic objects. The mathematical argument is shorter:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{nonzero automorphic vector}\\
&\Longrightarrow \text{open stabilizer near identity}\\
&\Longrightarrow \text{finite set of bad places and local bumps}\\
&\Longrightarrow \text{finite test vector with nonzero identity coefficient}\\
&\Longrightarrow \text{adelic product decomposition}\\
&\Longrightarrow \text{nonzero Schur main term}\\
&\quad+\text{strictly smaller tail}\\
&\Longrightarrow \text{global Rallis integral is nonzero}\\
&\Longrightarrow \text{theta lift is nonzero}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is the correct human-readable skeleton of NonVan_k.

## 5. What still needs a separate proof

The compression does not eliminate the analytic content of its inputs:

- Rallis_k: the global integral identity and zero-lift implication;
- Decomp_k: the adelic decomposition and Haar measure formula;
- BumpAny_k: local bump construction;
- Datum_k: construction and factorization of the test datum;
- Tail_k: integrable tail control;
- AbsNe: the final measure-theoretic strict-inequality lemma;
- SchurStep_k: the infinite-place Schur identity.

The next reduction should focus on Tail_k and Datum_k, because they control the construction of the test vector and the strict nonzero main-term/tail comparison.
