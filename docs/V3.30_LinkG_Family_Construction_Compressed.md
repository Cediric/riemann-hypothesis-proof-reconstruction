## 1. What LinkG_at actually contains

For a fixed record, LinkG_at is a five-part package. It should not be summarized as merely “the local lattice map is integral.”

### (G1) Local divisibility and length

Away from the level prime T(u), the rounded local exponent

\[
q_w=\left\lfloor \operatorname{ur}(w,\tau(w))\right\rfloor_+
\]

gives a lattice inclusion

\[
\operatorname{LocLe}(w,q_w,NuLat(u),MramL(u)),
\]

and the local length satisfies

\[
\operatorname{lenMN}(u,w)
\leq duS(u)\bigl(\tau(w)+io(w)\bigr).
\]

If the corresponding orbit set is empty, then tau(w), io(w), and lenMN(u,w) all vanish.

### (G2) Prime-orbit count

For every relevant prime p and orbit parameter t0,

\[
Sv(p,t_0)\leq
\frac{2m\,Nv(p,t_0)\,a}{p}.
\]

### (G3) Gain/loss estimate at inert primes

For inert p up to the cutoff X'':

\[
0\leq gain_j(w)\leq \frac{4|TT|a}{p}
\]

for every lift j, and the total von-Mangoldt-class contribution, after subtracting the local lower term and the flG correction, is bounded above by the total gain minus tau(w).

This is the prime-sum inequality that supplies the gain term used in the slope comparison.

### (G4) Served-orbit containment

The served orbits are contained in the prescribed orbit set.

### (G5) Mean-kappa bound

The average kappa contribution is bounded by the family constant K.

These five clauses are precisely the target of the family-level LinkG constructor.

## 2. The family-level statement

LinkG(Fm) supplies constants C and x0 and a function delta(x) such that

\[
\delta(x)\longrightarrow0
\qquad (x\to\infty),
\]

and for every x ≥ x0 and every family index i,

\[
\operatorname{LinkG\_at}
\bigl(G_x(i),G_K,C,b_x(i)^{\delta(x)}\bigr)
\]

holds.

The slowly varying factor b^delta is important: it is not replaced by a fixed constant. The proof preserves its dependence on x while ensuring delta tends to zero.

## 3. The no-kernel construction

The main constructor is linkG_without_kernels in AdmissibleFamilyNoKernel.lean. It takes:

- the nine admissibility conditions Adm1F through Adm9F;
- eventual trivial-stabilizer and finite-class conditions;
- preservation of lattices by the relevant operators;
- orbit-lattice bounds;
- a lattice-sandwich estimate;
- the NPDescF comparison condition.

Its proof has three stages.

### Stage A — Remove the kernel

The auxiliary lattice is replaced by zeroCh(Gy), which keeps the underlying lattice data but sets the chain character to zero. The chain-kernel calculation then gives zero valuation contribution. Together with trivial stabilizers, operator preservation, orbit-lattice bounds, and the lattice sandwich, this proves the no-kernel admissibility package.

### Stage B — Apply the auxiliary prime-orbit theorem

The admissibility package is passed to AuxBlock8.Pf.stmtF. This produces:

- a constant C;
- a function delta with delta(x) tending to zero;
- the family conclusion Conclusion₂ with error scale b^delta(x).

This is where the slowly varying error factor in LinkG comes from.

### Stage C — Convert the auxiliary conclusion to LinkG_at

The theorem linkG_at_of combines Conclusion₂ with:

- the family fill identities;
- support saturation;
- the tied-lattice and NuDef data;
- the local divisibility/length bounds;
- the served-orbit, prime-orbit, and kappa estimates;
- the local size-data bounds.

It returns the five clauses (G1)–(G5). The final constant can be enlarged to 4|C|+1 without changing the asymptotic delta(x).

## 4. How linkG_fam_noKer builds the admissibility package

The higher constructor linkG_fam_noKer derives the inputs to linkG_without_kernels from a smaller list.

Its explicit premises are:

1. SecondTheoremAtFamily;
2. FirstTheoremAtFamily;
3. positivity of the global La constant and the lower bound Ce ≥ 1;
4. an eventual CbLarge estimate;
5. eventual trivial stabilizers and finite classes;
6. the standard-subspace condition StdSubAt;
7. Adm2F and NPDescF;
8. the family-window logarithmic bound.

The proof assembles the missing pieces as follows.

- The second theorem gives the second family bound h6.
- The kappa identity gives h7.
- The first theorem gives the first family bound h8.
- The global-order estimate, Weyl sandwich, and exponent bound give Adm9F.
- The linear-programming input is obtained from h6, h7, StdSubAt, the two orbit hypotheses, and CbLarge.
- The lattice-sandwich estimate follows from Adm9F.
- The first theorem's elementary family lemma supplies Adm1F; the remaining admissibility conditions are passed through.

It then invokes linkG_without_kernels.

This is a real proof assembly: LinkG is not introduced as a custom axiom or simply assumed at the final H9 step.

## 5. The local-to-global closure

The theorem link_of_local_closed shows how the family theorem is obtained from a closed family class.

Given:

- the local condition Loc;
- eventual trivial-stabilizer/finite-class conditions;
- StdSubAt;
- Adm2F;
- NPDescF;

the theorem combines:

- the second-theorem closed form;
- the first-theorem threshold functions;
- sign and positivity conditions;
- the Cb bound;
- the family-window logarithmic bound;

and applies linkG_fam_noKer.

The local condition itself is discharged by loc_proved, which uses the restricted criterion together with TraceSpectra.stmtE.

Thus the outer logical route is:

\[
\text{local trace/model criterion}
\Longrightarrow \text{closed family inputs}
\Longrightarrow \text{First/SecondTheoremAtFamily}
\Longrightarrow \text{LinkG}.
\]

The remaining family hypotheses are still genuine dependencies; the closure theorem does not erase them.

## 6. What this contributes to the H9 proof

Combining V3.28–V3.30, the corrected-lattice half of H9 now has a transparent structure:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{LvlF, TuSize, DGamSize, ChosenProviso}
\Longrightarrow \text{NuFullF, MuRank, LinkMu},\\
&\text{local trace/model inputs}
\Longrightarrow \text{LinkG},\\
&\text{LevelTFamily, NuCommensF, TuSize, MuRank, LinkG, LinkMu}
\Longrightarrow \text{PlacesF}\\
&\qquad\Longrightarrow \text{SlopesNF}.

The conversion from PlacesF to SlopesNF is short. The substantial work is now localized in two branches:

1. the level/rank branch (LvlF, NuFullF, MuRank, LinkMu);
2. the local divisibility branch (LinkG).

This is a much more useful human proof outline than a single assumed “range of slopes” statement.
